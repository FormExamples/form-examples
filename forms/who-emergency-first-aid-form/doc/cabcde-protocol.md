# CABCDE Protocol — WHO Community First Aid Responder

This form implements the WHO Community First Aid Responder (CFAR)
documentation aligned with the WHO Community First Aid (CFA) curriculum
and the WHO *Basic Emergency Care* (BEC) approach. CFAR is the WHO
designation for a community-trained lay rescuer who provides early
first aid before the prehospital emergency medical services arrive.

## CABCDE rationale

WHO BEC uses a catastrophic-haemorrhage-first variant of the classical
ABCDE primary survey. The first "C" denotes catastrophic external
bleeding, which is the leading preventable cause of early trauma death
and must be addressed before securing the airway.

| Letter | Meaning | Why before / after the others |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C | Catastrophic haemorrhage | Exsanguinating bleeding kills in minutes; controlled first |
| A | Airway | Open airway must be secured before ventilation can be assessed |
| B | Breathing | Adequate breathing required before circulation interventions |
| C | Circulation | Shock, fluid loss, pulse, capillary refill |
| D | Disability | Neurological status, glucose, seizure, hypothermia |
| E | Exposure / Other | Expose to identify all injuries; protect from environment |

## Step → CABCDE alignment

| Form step | CABCDE letter | Key interventions captured |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5. Major Bleeding | C (catastrophic) | Direct pressure, deep wound packing, tourniquet (with time of application), uterine massage |
| 6. Airway | A | Neck immobilization, head-tilt chin-lift, jaw thrust, choking care |
| 7. Breathing | B | Position of comfort |
| 8. Circulation | C | Pelvic binder, minor bleeding control, fracture care, oral hydration, left-lateral position |
| 9. Disability | D | Spinal immobilization, glucose, seizure care, hyperthermia/hypothermia care |
| 10. Exposure / Other | E | Recovery position, burn care, wound care, drowning care, snakebite care |

## Tourniquet protocol

WHO BEC (consistent with US Stop the Bleed and TCCC) recommends a
commercial windlass tourniquet (CAT, SOFT-T) for life-threatening limb
haemorrhage that fails to respond to direct pressure. CFARs are
specifically taught:

1. Apply 2-3 inches above the wound (never on a joint).
2. Tighten until bleeding stops.
3. Write the **time of application** on the tourniquet or skin — the
   form captures this explicitly.
4. Do not loosen or remove until handed over to medical care.
5. Reassess if applied for prolonged transport (>2 hours).

Reference: Bulger EM, Snyder D, Schoelles K, et al. *An evidence-based
prehospital guideline for external hemorrhage control.* Prehosp Emerg
Care. 2014;18(2):163-73. doi:10.3109/10903127.2014.896962
Stop the Bleed (American College of Surgeons):
<https://www.stopthebleed.org/>

## Choking care

WHO BEC sequence:

- **Conscious adult or child >1 y**: encourage coughing → 5 back blows
  → 5 abdominal thrusts (chest thrusts in pregnancy or infant).
- **Infant <1 y**: 5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts in alternating cycles;
  never abdominal thrusts.
- **Unconscious**: lay on firm surface, look in mouth, finger sweep only
  if visible solid object, start CPR.

## Recovery position

WHO BEC recovery position (left-lateral) is indicated for:

- Unresponsive but breathing patients
- Risk of vomiting
- Suspected poisoning
- Post-ictal patient

The form captures recovery position under step 10 (Exposure / Other).

## Spinal immobilization triggers

CFARs apply spinal precautions when any of the following are present:

- Significant fall (height > rescuer's own height)
- Road-traffic crash
- Diving incident in shallow water
- Penetrating injury above the clavicles
- Patient complaining of neck or back pain after trauma
- Unconscious trauma patient

Manual in-line stabilization is taught for CFARs; rigid cervical collar
application is delegated to prehospital EMS in the WHO BEC scope.

## Hand-over

The completed form is given to the receiving prehospital provider or
referral facility. CFAR signs at the bottom; receiving provider may
optionally sign a counter-acknowledgement.

The form is designed to fit on a single A4/Letter page so it can be
photographed and transmitted by mobile phone if the original cannot be
delivered.
