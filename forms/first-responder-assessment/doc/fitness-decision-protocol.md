# Fitness Decision Protocol

This document describes how the assessor reaches the overall fitness
decision in step 10 from the competency scores and occupational-health
findings.

## Decision categories

The four-category outcome mirrors the Faculty of Occupational Medicine
fitness-for-task vocabulary used by NHS occupational-health services:

| Decision | Definition | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Fit for duty** | All required competencies at Competent (3) or Expert (4); no occupational-health restrictions | Sign off for full operational duty |
| **Fit with restrictions** | One or more competencies at Developing (2) **or** a controlled occupational-health condition (e.g. needle phobia, controlled back injury, recovering musculoskeletal injury) | Define restrictions and remediation plan; reassess at a defined interval |
| **Temporarily unfit** | Critical clinical or driving competency at Not Competent (1), or an acute occupational-health condition that prevents safe operational duty | Remove from operational duty; defined retraining and / or treatment period; reassess at a defined interval |
| **Permanently unfit** | Persistent inability to reach minimum competency after retraining, or a permanent medical condition that prevents safe operational duty | Redeploy or terminate registration; HCPC notified if relevant |

## Decision tree

```
Step 4 (clinical) — any "Not Competent" in airway, CPR, AED,
                    haemorrhage control, anaphylaxis?
    │
    ├── Yes → Temporarily unfit (clinical remediation required)
    │
    └── No → continue

Step 5 (vehicle) — any "Not Competent" in C1 driving competency or
                   emergency response driving?
    │
    ├── Yes → Temporarily unfit (driving remediation required)
    │
    └── No → continue

Step 7 (psychological readiness) — current acute PTSD, severe burnout,
                                   or active suicidality?
    │
    ├── Yes → Temporarily unfit (occupational health referral)
    │
    └── No → continue

Step 8 (occupational health) — any uncontrolled DVLA-notifiable
                               condition (epilepsy, severe sleep apnoea,
                               uncontrolled diabetes, cardiac arrhythmia)?
    │
    ├── Yes → Temporarily unfit (DVLA notification; medical clearance)
    │
    └── No → continue

Any competency at Developing (2)?
    │
    ├── Yes → Fit with restrictions; define remediation plan
    │
    └── No → Fit for duty
```

## DVLA notification thresholds (UK)

The DVLA *Assessing Fitness to Drive* guidance is the statutory authority
for medical fitness to drive a Group 2 (HGV / ambulance C1) vehicle. The
most-cited categories for ambulance personnel:

- **Epilepsy**: any unprovoked seizure → 10-year seizure-free period
  before Group 2 licence reinstated.
- **Type 1 diabetes**: must be aware of hypoglycaemia and report blood
  glucose monitoring; otherwise notify DVLA.
- **Severe sleep apnoea**: must be treated and DVLA notified.
- **Cardiac arrhythmia / pacemaker / ICD**: see chapter 2 of *Assessing
  Fitness to Drive*.

Reference: DVLA. *Assessing fitness to drive: a guide for medical
professionals*. Updated periodically.
<https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/assessing-fitness-to-drive-a-guide-for-medical-professionals>

## Physical fitness benchmarks

There is no nationally mandated UK ambulance fitness battery. Common
references used by NHS trusts and adopted in this form:

- **NHS Pre-Employment Health Assessment** — chest x-ray, audiometry,
  vision (Snellen 6/9 each eye uncorrected; colour vision Ishihara
  plates), spirometry, BMI.
- **Chester Step Test** — VO2-max proxy used by some trusts for ambulance
  fitness gating.
  Sykes K, Roberts A. *The Chester step test — a simple yet effective
  tool for the prediction of aerobic capacity.* Physiotherapy.
  2004;90(4):183–8. doi:10.1016/j.physio.2004.03.008
- **NFPA 1582 (US)** — comprehensive medical evaluation including
  treadmill stress test; cited by US EMS agencies.
  <https://www.nfpa.org/codes-and-standards/1582>

## Psychological readiness benchmarks

- **PHQ-9** (Patient Health Questionnaire) — depression screening; score
  ≥10 prompts clinical review. Kroenke K, Spitzer RL, Williams JBW.
  *The PHQ-9: validity of a brief depression severity measure.* J Gen
  Intern Med. 2001;16(9):606-13. PMID: 11556941.
- **GAD-7** (Generalised Anxiety Disorder 7-item) — anxiety screening;
  score ≥10 prompts clinical review. Spitzer RL, Kroenke K, Williams JBW,
  Löwe B. Arch Intern Med. 2006;166(10):1092-7. PMID: 16717171.
- **PCL-5** (PTSD Checklist for DSM-5) — PTSD screening; score ≥33
  prompts clinical review.
  <https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/assessment/adult-sr/ptsd-checklist.asp>

## CPD requirements

- HCPC paramedics must complete CPD over each 2-year registration
  period; HCPC audits a random sample.
  <https://www.hcpc-uk.org/cpd/>
- College of Paramedics recommends a minimum of 30 CPD hours per year.
  <https://collegeofparamedics.co.uk/COP/Professional-development/CPD.aspx>

## Documentation

- Decision and rationale recorded in the assessment record.
- Copy to the responder.
- Copy to the line manager.
- Copy to occupational health if restrictions or unfit decisions are made.
- HCPC notified if a registrant is found permanently unfit.
