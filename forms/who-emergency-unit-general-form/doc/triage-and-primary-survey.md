# Triage and Primary Survey

This document describes how the triage category, high-risk signs panel,
and ABCD primary survey are intended to be used at the bedside.

## WHO triage approach

The WHO general EU form does not bind a specific triage algorithm but is
designed to be compatible with the four-tier WHO triage tools published
alongside the SCF series:

- **Interagency Integrated Triage Tool (IITT)** — WHO / Médecins Sans
  Frontières / ICRC consensus tool for adults and children in resource-
  limited settings; three-tier (RED / YELLOW / GREEN).
  <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240017344>
- **South African Triage Scale (SATS)** — five-tier (RED / ORANGE /
  YELLOW / GREEN / BLUE). Adopted across sub-Saharan Africa.
  Reference: Twomey M, Wallis LA, Thompson ML, Myers JE. *The South
  African Triage Scale (adult version) provides reliable acuity ratings.*
  Int Emerg Nurs. 2012;20(3):142-50. doi:10.1016/j.ienj.2011.08.002
- **Emergency Severity Index (ESI), v4** — US Agency for Healthcare
  Research and Quality five-tier scale.
  <https://www.ahrq.gov/patient-safety/settings/emergency-dept/esi.html>
- **Manchester Triage System (MTS)** — UK-origin five-tier scale.
  Mackway-Jones K, Marsden J, Windle J (eds). *Emergency Triage: Manchester
  Triage Group*, 3rd edition. BMJ Books; 2014. ISBN 978-1118299067.

## High-risk-signs panel

Step 3 of the form captures eight high-risk indicators that — in WHO BEC
— immediately upgrade triage to RED regardless of the chief complaint.

| Sign | Threshold | Underlying physiology |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Abnormal AVPU | V, P, or U | Reduced perfusion or intracranial process |
| Adult HR | <55 or >130 bpm | Significant haemodynamic compromise |
| Stridor or voice change | Present | Upper airway obstruction risk |
| Poor perfusion | Capillary refill >3 s, weak pulse | Hypovolaemic / distributive shock |
| Temperature | >39 °C or <36 °C | Sepsis (NICE NG51 criterion) |
| SpO2 | <90% on room air | Hypoxaemic respiratory failure |
| Respiratory distress | Grunting in child; retractions; cyanosis | Imminent respiratory failure |
| Cannot drink / vomits everything | Present | IMCI paediatric red flag |

References:
- NICE NG51. *Sepsis: recognition, diagnosis and early management*.
  2016 (updated 2024). <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng51>
- WHO. *Integrated Management of Childhood Illness chart booklet*. 2014.
  <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241506823>

## ABCD primary survey

The form's ABCD survey is the *standard sequential primary survey*
described in WHO BEC.

### Airway (A)

- **Patent / partially obstructed / obstructed**.
- **Cause** — tongue, blood, secretions, vomit, foreign body.
- **Intervention** — repositioning, suction, OPA, NPA, LMA, BVM, ETT.
- Critical decision: if airway cannot be maintained by basic adjuncts,
  escalate to definitive airway (ETT) immediately.

### Breathing (B)

- **Spontaneous RR**, chest rise, tracheal position, breath sounds.
- **Oxygen** — flow rate and delivery device.
- **Chest needle or tube** — laterality, size, depth.
- Critical decision: tension pneumothorax → needle decompression
  (2nd ICS MCL or 4th-5th ICS AAL per ATLS 10th edition).

### Circulation (C)

- **Skin**, capillary refill, pulses, JVD.
- **Access** — IV, CVL, IO with location and gauge.
- **IV fluids** — type and volume.
- **Blood ordered**.
- **Epinephrine** (anaphylaxis or cardiac arrest).

Crystalloid resuscitation in undifferentiated shock follows the *Surviving
Sepsis Campaign* recommendation of 30 mL/kg balanced crystalloid (LR or
Hartmann's preferred over normal saline).

Reference: Evans L, Rhodes A, Alhazzani W, et al. *Surviving Sepsis
Campaign: international guidelines for management of sepsis and septic
shock 2021*. Intensive Care Med. 2021;47:1181-1247. PMID: 34599691.

### Disability (D)

- **AVPU** or **Glasgow Coma Scale**.
- **Moves all extremities** or specific deficit.
- **Pupils** — size and reactivity both sides.
- **Blood glucose** — abnormal if <3.5 mmol/L (the form notes the
  WHO threshold).
- **Interventions** — glucose, antiepileptic, naloxone, others.

Reference: Teasdale G, Jennett B. *Assessment of coma and impaired
consciousness. A practical scale*. Lancet. 1974;2(7872):81-4.
PMID: 4136544.

## High-risk trauma adjuncts

The general form does not include the F (FAST) row. If a non-trauma
patient is later reclassified as trauma (e.g. occult haemorrhage from a
missed mechanism), the clinician transitions to the WHO Emergency Unit
Form: Trauma.

## Treating-provider workflow

1. Bedside nurse or triage clinician completes steps 1-3 within 5
   minutes of arrival.
2. Treating provider takes over from step 4 onwards.
3. Reassessment (step 15) is repeated at intervals dictated by acuity:
   - RED — every 15 minutes
   - YELLOW — every 30 minutes
   - GREEN — every 60 minutes
4. Disposition (step 16) is documented at the end of the encounter.
