# Clinical guideline alignment — autism

## NICE CG142 — Autism spectrum disorder in adults: diagnosis and management

Source: National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE).
Guideline ID: **CG142** (published June 2012; last updated June 2021).
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg142

CG142 §1.2 sets out the recommended assessment process. The form aligns
with the following recommendations:

- **§1.2.4**: A comprehensive assessment of core autism signs, including
  difficulties in reciprocal social interaction and communication and the
  presence of stereotypic behaviour, resistance to change, or restricted
  interests. The Social Communication and Repetitive Behaviors steps cover
  this domain.
- **§1.2.5**: Detailed developmental history. Captured in the Developmental
  History step.
- **§1.2.6**: Assessment of physical health, mental health, and behaviour
  that may indicate a coexisting condition. The Family History and Current
  Support steps support this.
- **§1.2.7**: Use of formal assessment tools (e.g. ADOS-2, ADI-R) where
  there is diagnostic uncertainty. This form captures screening; a positive
  AQ-10 referral triggers a full multi-disciplinary diagnostic assessment.

## NICE NG142 — Autism in under-19s

Source: NICE. Guideline ID: **NG142** (replaces CG128 in some scope).
For children and young people the relevant historical guideline is CG128
*Autism spectrum disorder in under 19s: recognition, referral and
diagnosis* (https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg128) and CG170 for management.
This form is primarily adult-focused; clinicians assessing under-19s should
follow CG128 / CG170. See https://www.nice.org.uk for the index.

## NICE QS51 — Autism

Source: NICE Quality Standard **QS51** (published 2014).
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/qs51

QS51 statement 1 recommends that adults with possible autism are referred
for a comprehensive autism assessment if they have one or more of the core
signs and an AQ-10 score of 6 or more. The form's referral logic encodes
this exact rule.

## DSM-5-TR

American Psychiatric Association (2022) *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual
of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR)*.
Code **299.00 / F84.0** — Autism Spectrum Disorder.

DSM-5-TR criteria require:

- Persistent deficits in social communication and social interaction across
  multiple contexts (3 of 3 sub-criteria).
- Restricted, repetitive patterns of behaviour, interests, or activities
  (2 of 4 sub-criteria).
- Symptoms present in the early developmental period.
- Clinically significant impairment.
- Disturbances not better explained by intellectual developmental disorder
  or global developmental delay.

DSM-5-TR specifies severity levels 1, 2, 3 ("requiring support",
"requiring substantial support", "requiring very substantial support") for
each of the social communication and restricted-repetitive behaviour
domains.

## ICD-11

World Health Organization. ICD-11 MMS. Code **6A02** — Autism spectrum
disorder. https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en

ICD-11 6A02 sub-codes:

- 6A02.0 — without disorder of intellectual development and with mild or no
  impairment of functional language
- 6A02.1 — with disorder of intellectual development and with mild or no
  impairment of functional language
- 6A02.2 — without disorder of intellectual development and with impaired
  functional language
- 6A02.3 — with disorder of intellectual development and with impaired
  functional language
- 6A02.4 / 6A02.5 / 6A02.Y / 6A02.Z — additional specifiers.
