# Autism assessment protocol

This document describes the interview structure, behavioural observations,
and corroborating-history sources used alongside the AQ-10 screener.

## Interview structure

NICE CG142 §1.2.4–§1.2.6 specifies the domains a comprehensive adult autism
assessment must cover. The mapping to this form's steps:

| Domain                              | Captured in step(s)            |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Self-reported screening items       | AQ-10 Questionnaire            |
| Reciprocal social interaction       | Social Communication           |
| Social communication                | Social Communication           |
| Restricted, repetitive behaviours   | Repetitive Behaviors           |
| Sensory differences                 | Sensory Profile                |
| Early developmental history         | Developmental History          |
| Current support needs               | Current Support                |
| Family history of autism            | Family History                 |

## Behavioural observations during interview

The clinician records observations in the free-text fields:

- **Eye contact** — quality, frequency, and reciprocity.
- **Facial expression** — range, fit to context.
- **Prosody and pragmatics** — monotone speech, literal interpretation,
  difficulty with conversational turn-taking.
- **Stimming / repetitive motor behaviours** — flapping, rocking, finger
  movements, vocal stims.
- **Special interests** — depth, breadth, dominance of conversation.
- **Sensory reactions** — to clinic lighting, noise, smells, touch.

## Sensory profile prompts

NICE CG142 §1.2.4 includes sensory differences. The Sensory Profile step
prompts the clinician to record reactions in each modality:

- Auditory hyper- or hyposensitivity (e.g. distress from background noise,
  not responding to name).
- Visual (e.g. distress from bright lights, fascination with patterns or
  movement).
- Tactile (e.g. clothing-tag avoidance, distress on touch, preference for
  deep pressure).
- Gustatory / olfactory (food selectivity, smell avoidance).
- Proprioceptive / vestibular (seeking spinning, jumping, balance issues).
- Interoceptive (difficulty recognising hunger, thirst, pain, toilet needs).

## Corroborating history sources

Diagnosis must not rest on AQ-10 alone. The clinician obtains:

- **Developmental history** — parental account, baby books, paediatric
  records, school reports. Captured in Developmental History.
- **Adult collateral** — partner, close family, employer account.
  Captured in Current Support and Social Communication.

## Differential diagnosis and comorbidity prompts

NICE CG142 §1.2.6 requires recording of coexisting conditions. The
clinician reviews:

- **Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder** (ICD-11 6A05) — common
  coexisting condition.
- **Intellectual developmental disorders** (ICD-11 6A00) — particularly
  for the ICD-11 6A02.1 / 6A02.3 subtypes.
- **Anxiety disorders** (ICD-11 6B00–6B0Z) — generalised anxiety and
  social anxiety are highly prevalent in autistic adults.
- **Depressive disorders** (ICD-11 6A70–6A7Z).
- **Obsessive-compulsive disorder** (ICD-11 6B20) — must be distinguished
  from restricted/repetitive autism behaviours.
- **Eating disorders** — particularly ARFID.
- **Sleep-wake disorders** (ICD-11 7A00–7B2Z).
- **Personality disorders** — must be considered cautiously; many
  presentations attributed to personality disorder are unrecognised
  autism, particularly in women.

## Outcome recording

At the end of the assessment, the clinician records:

- AQ-10 total (0–10) and whether the referral threshold (≥6) is met.
- Provisional ICD-11 / DSM-5-TR diagnostic impression.
- Sensory profile summary.
- Support needs and reasonable adjustments required for further
  appointments (e.g. quiet waiting area, written instructions, named
  contact).
- Onward referral to a specialist autism assessment service if the AQ-10
  threshold is met or if clinical judgement so indicates.
