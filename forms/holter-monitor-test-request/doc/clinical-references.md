# Clinical references

Authoritative guidance underpinning the four-axis grading engine.

## Monitor-type / symptom-frequency matching (ACC/AHA, ISHNE-HRS, NICE NG196)

The diagnostic yield of ambulatory ECG depends on capturing the rhythm during
symptoms. A 24-hour Holter is most appropriate for daily or near-daily symptoms;
infrequent symptoms need longer or patient-activated monitoring.

| Symptom frequency | Recommended monitor |
| --- | --- |
| Daily / near-daily | 24-hour or 48-hour Holter |
| Weekly | 7-day monitor |
| Monthly | 14-day monitor or event recorder |
| Rare (< monthly) | event recorder or implantable loop recorder |

## Appropriateness (ACC/AHA ambulatory ECG)

Ordinal 1–9 scale in three bands: usually-appropriate (7–9),
may-be-appropriate (4–6), usually-not-appropriate (1–3). Unexplained syncope,
near-syncope, episodic dizziness, and palpitations are Class I indications;
brief palpitations with no heart disease are Class III. Mapped per
(indication × monitor type × symptom frequency).

## Triage tiers

| Tier | Target | Triggers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency | same day | syncope with suspected high-grade block or VT, haemodynamic concern |
| Urgent | 24–72 h | syncope, suspected VT, post-stroke AF detection, urgent referrer flag |
| Routine | standard booking | palpitations, AF detection, rate-control / QT / pacemaker surveillance |
