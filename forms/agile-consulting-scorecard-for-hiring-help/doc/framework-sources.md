# Framework Sources

The Agile Consulting Scorecard is a non-clinical self-assessment of
organisational readiness to engage external agile coaching help. Unlike
clinical forms in this monorepo, the canonical sources are the Agile
Manifesto, the Principles Behind the Agile Manifesto, the Scrum Guide,
and adjacent Lean / Toyota Production System literature. This document
captures the citations and the rationale for each.

## Agile Manifesto (2001)

The four-statement Manifesto for Agile Software Development is the
foundational reference for items 1–4 of the scorecard.

- Beck, K. *et al.* *Manifesto for Agile Software Development* (2001) —
  <https://agilemanifesto.org/>

The four values are:

1. Individuals and interactions over processes and tools.
2. Working software over comprehensive documentation.
3. Customer collaboration over contract negotiation.
4. Responding to change over following a plan.

Each of these maps directly to one of items 1–4 in the scorecard, with
the checklist item phrased as a binary operational test the organisation
can audit against today.

## Principles Behind the Agile Manifesto (2001)

The twelve principles supplied by the same authors form the basis of
items 5–16 of the scorecard.

- *Principles Behind the Agile Manifesto* —
  <https://agilemanifesto.org/principles.html>

Each principle maps to one scorecard item; the item rephrases the
principle as a measurable yes/no test (e.g. "Every product lead measures
customer NPS" for the customer-satisfaction principle).

## Scrum Guide

The Scrum Guide (Schwaber and Sutherland) supplies the underlying
process discipline against which several scorecard items are calibrated.

- Schwaber, K. & Sutherland, J. *The Scrum Guide* (current edition) —
  <https://scrumguides.org/>

Scrum Guide concepts used:

- Sprint cadence — informs "deliver working software frequently" (item 7).
- Sprint Review — informs "every leader has shared their previous 2
  retrospectives" (item 16).
- Product Owner / Scrum Master / Development Team roles — informs
  "commitment from every product / project / programme / practice lead"
  (item 8).

## Lean and Toyota Production System

The "process improvement skills" item (item 14) is calibrated against the
Lean / TPS canon.

- Womack, J.P. & Jones, D.T. *Lean Thinking* (1996).
- Ohno, T. *Toyota Production System* (1988).
- Seddon, J. *Freedom from Command and Control* — The Vanguard Method.

## Net Promoter Score

The customer satisfaction item (item 5) references NPS as the measurement
mechanism.

- Reichheld, F. *The One Number You Need to Grow.* Harvard Business
  Review, December 2003 —
  <https://hbr.org/2003/12/the-one-number-you-need-to-grow>

## Change management

The change-management readiness item (item 4) is calibrated against the
representative agile change-management literature.

- Kotter, J.P. *Leading Change* (1996).
- Kotter, J.P. *Our Iceberg Is Melting* (2006).
- Patterson, K. et al. *Influencer: The New Science of Leading Change*
  (2007).
- Sutherland, J. & Schwaber, K. *Software in 30 Days* (Wiley, 2012).
- Reinertsen, D. *The Principles of Product Development Flow* (Celeritas,
  2009).

## Why not SAFe / LeSS / Disciplined Agile?

The scorecard is intentionally framework-agnostic. SAFe (Scaled Agile
Framework), LeSS (Large-Scale Scrum), and Disciplined Agile / Disciplined
Agile Delivery each have their own readiness self-assessments; the
scorecard is not a substitute. Organisations evaluating those specific
frameworks should consult:

- SAFe — <https://scaledagileframework.com/>
- LeSS — <https://less.works/>
- PMI Disciplined Agile — <https://www.pmi.org/disciplined-agile>

The scorecard's value is that it precedes framework selection: an
organisation that scores Low on the manifesto basics is unlikely to
benefit from any framework-specific transformation, regardless of which
one is on offer.
