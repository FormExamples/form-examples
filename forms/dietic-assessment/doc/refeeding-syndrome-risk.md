# Refeeding-syndrome risk

Refeeding syndrome is the potentially fatal shift in fluids and electrolytes
that can follow the reintroduction of nutrition in a malnourished patient. The
criteria below are those in NICE *Nutrition support for adults* (CG32).

This is the only part of the form that can raise a composite risk of
`critical` on a single finding, because the clinical consequence — cardiac
arrhythmia, respiratory failure, death — is immediate and the mitigation is
cheap: measure electrolytes and start feeding slowly.

## High risk — one or more of

| Rule ID | Criterion |
| --- | --- |
| `R-REFEED-H-BMI` | BMI < 16 kg/m² |
| `R-REFEED-H-WL` | Unintentional weight loss > 15 % in the last 3–6 months |
| `R-REFEED-H-INTAKE` | Little or no nutritional intake for > 10 days |
| `R-REFEED-H-LYTES` | Low potassium, phosphate, or magnesium **before** feeding |

## High risk — two or more of

| Rule ID | Criterion |
| --- | --- |
| `R-REFEED-M-BMI` | BMI < 18.5 kg/m² |
| `R-REFEED-M-WL` | Unintentional weight loss > 10 % in the last 3–6 months |
| `R-REFEED-M-INTAKE` | Little or no nutritional intake for > 5 days |
| `R-REFEED-M-HISTORY` | History of alcohol misuse, or of insulin, chemotherapy, antacid, or diuretic use |

## Highest risk

The engine records `highest` where BMI < 14 kg/m², or there has been negligible
intake for more than 15 days. These patients need cardiac monitoring and
specialist nutrition-support-team input before feeding begins.

## Engine output

```
refeedingRisk: 'none' | 'high' | 'highest'
```

`high` or `highest` fires the `refeeding-syndrome-risk` safety flag at high
priority, contributes `critical` to the composite risk, and sets the step-16
recommendation default to `urgent-referral`.

## Monitoring protocol (recorded on the care plan)

For patients at high or highest risk, NICE CG32 recommends:

1. Check potassium, phosphate, magnesium, and calcium **before** feeding.
2. Start nutrition support at a maximum of 10 kcal/kg/day, increasing slowly to
   meet full needs by 4–7 days. For the highest-risk group, start at
   5 kcal/kg/day with cardiac monitoring.
3. Restore circulatory volume and monitor fluid balance and overall clinical
   status closely.
4. Provide immediately before and during the first 10 days of feeding: oral
   thiamine 200–300 mg daily, vitamin B co-strong 1–2 tablets three times daily
   (or full-dose intravenous vitamin B preparation), and a balanced multivitamin
   and trace-element supplement once daily.
5. Provide potassium, phosphate, magnesium, and calcium supplementation unless
   pre-feeding levels are high, and monitor daily for the first week.

The form records the monitoring plan and the review interval on step 16; it
does not prescribe. Doses above are reproduced from the guideline for reference
only and are not generated by the engine.
