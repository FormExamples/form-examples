# Assessment Protocol — Patient Assessment Station

This document describes the assessment flow the examiner observes and
scores, and how it maps to the form's 6 steps. The protocol draws on the
NREMT National EMS Education Standards and the WHO Basic Emergency Care
(BEC) systematic approach.

## Scene size-up

Before approaching the patient, the EMT must:

1. **Take Body Substance Isolation (BSI) precautions** — gloves at
   minimum, plus eye protection, gown, or N95 mask as risk dictates.
   Failure to take PPE precautions is a critical criterion.
2. **Determine scene safety** — for both the rescuer and the patient.
   Failure to assess scene safety is a critical criterion.
3. **Determine mechanism of injury (MOI) for trauma** or **nature of
   illness (NOI) for medical** — from bystanders, dispatch, and visual
   inspection.
4. **Determine the number of patients** — and request additional
   resources (additional ambulances, fire suppression, law enforcement) if
   the scene exceeds the responding crew's capacity.
5. **Consider spinal stabilization** if the mechanism suggests possible
   spinal injury (motor-vehicle crash, fall from height, penetrating
   trauma above the clavicles).

## Primary survey (ABCDE-adjusted)

NREMT uses the historic A-B-C-D-E primary survey, although the catastrophic
haemorrhage "C-ABCDE" variant adopted by US military and many civilian
systems is acceptable.

| Letter | Assessment | Intervention threshold |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (C) | Catastrophic external haemorrhage — life-threatening compressible bleeding | Direct pressure, wound packing, tourniquet |
| A | Airway patency — open, threatened, or obstructed | Head-tilt chin-lift, jaw thrust (trauma), OPA / NPA, suction |
| B | Breathing — rate, depth, effort, sounds, oxygenation | High-flow oxygen via non-rebreather, BVM ventilation, occlusive dressing for open chest wound |
| C | Circulation — pulse, skin colour/temp/moisture, capillary refill, external bleeding | Bleeding control, position of comfort, fluid (ALS adjunct) |
| D | Disability — AVPU, gross motor exam, pupils | Manual spinal stabilization, glucose for hypoglycaemia |
| E | Exposure — undress and inspect, then prevent hypothermia | Remove clothing, log roll for back inspection, blanket |

Failure to manage A, B, or C, failure to voice and provide oxygen when
indicated, or failure to control major bleeding in a timely manner are
all critical-criteria failures.

## History taking

Two complementary mnemonics are tested:

- **OPQRST** for the chief complaint:
  - Onset
  - Provocation / palliation
  - Quality
  - Region / radiation
  - Severity (0-10 pain scale)
  - Time of onset and duration
- **SAMPLE** for past history:
  - Signs and symptoms
  - Allergies
  - Medications
  - Past pertinent medical history
  - Last oral intake
  - Events leading up to the illness or injury

The form captures both mnemonics in step 4.

## Secondary assessment

Vital signs and a focused or detailed physical examination directed by the
chief complaint. NREMT expects:

- Baseline vitals: BP, pulse, respirations, skin signs, pupils, and SpO2.
- Focused exam of the affected body region.
- Detailed head-to-toe exam for unresponsive patients or significant
  trauma.

## Transport decision and reassessment

The candidate must verbalize a transport decision (urgent transport vs
stay-and-treat) **within 15 minutes** of patient contact. Failure to
make the transport decision in that window is a critical criterion.

After interventions, the candidate must perform a reassessment that
repeats:

- Primary survey
- Vital signs
- Effectiveness of each intervention
- Any change in the patient's mental status or condition

Reassessment frequency:

- **Unstable patient**: every 5 minutes
- **Stable patient**: every 15 minutes

## Critical-criteria checkpoints

| Critical criterion | Form step |
| --- | --- |
| Failed to take or verbalize PPE precautions | 2 |
| Failed to determine scene safety before approaching patient | 2 |
| Failed to voice and provide high-concentration oxygen when indicated | 3 |
| Failed to assess and manage airway, breathing, or circulation in correct order | 3 |
| Failed to differentiate urgency of patient transport | 3 / 4 |
| Did or ordered a dangerous or potentially harmful intervention | 3 / 4 / 5 |
| Failed to provide manual spinal stabilization when indicated | 2 / 3 |
| Did not call for or initiate transport of patient within 15-minute window | 4 / 5 |

Any one of these triggers an automatic Fail on step 6 regardless of point
total.
