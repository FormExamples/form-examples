# NREMT Psychomotor Examination — Source and Structure

This form implements the patient assessment station of the National Registry
of Emergency Medical Technicians (NREMT) psychomotor competency portfolio
for the EMT level of certification.

## Authoritative source

- **National Registry of Emergency Medical Technicians (NREMT)** — the United
  States national certification agency for emergency medical services
  personnel. NREMT is a not-for-profit registry founded in 1970.
- **NREMT website**: <https://www.nremt.org/>
- **Candidate handbook (EMT)**: <https://www.nremt.org/CMSPages/GetFile.aspx?guid=42fbc4a8-21d2-4f43-bea4-aa8edd1faea3>
  (downloadable from <https://www.nremt.org/Document/handbook>)
- **EMT psychomotor portfolio guidance**: <https://www.nremt.org/Resources/EMT-Recertification>

NREMT no longer administers a single nationally-standardized psychomotor
examination day for the EMT level; since the 2023 transition the
psychomotor verification is delegated to the candidate's state EMS office
or training programme. The candidate handbook and the EMT Education
Standards (US DOT National EMS Education Standards, 2009; refreshed 2021)
remain the primary reference for the skills covered.

## National EMS Education Standards

The competencies tested in the patient assessment station are drawn from:

- **National EMS Education Standards** — US Department of Transportation,
  National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), DOT HS 811 077A,
  January 2009.
- **URL**: <https://www.ems.gov/projects/education-standards.html>

The standards define the EMT scope of practice and the cognitive,
affective, and psychomotor objectives the candidate must demonstrate.

## Station components mirrored by this form

The NREMT EMT psychomotor portfolio historically included the following
stations; the patient assessment station combines several of them into a
single integrated scenario:

1. **Patient Assessment / Management — Medical** (this form, medical track)
2. **Patient Assessment / Management — Trauma** (this form, trauma track)
3. Cardiac arrest management / AED (separate skill)
4. Bag-valve-mask ventilation of an apneic adult patient (separate skill)
5. Oxygen administration by non-rebreather mask (separate skill)
6. Spinal immobilization — seated and supine (separate skill)
7. Long bone immobilization (separate skill)
8. Joint immobilization (separate skill)
9. Bleeding control / shock management (separate skill)

The patient assessment scenarios test the candidate's ability to integrate
those discrete skills into a flowing assessment-treatment-transport
decision.

## Step-by-step alignment

| Form step | NREMT skill sheet element |
| --- | --- |
| 2. Scene Size-Up | Takes BSI / PPE; determines scene safety; determines mechanism of injury / nature of illness; determines number of patients; requests additional EMS assistance; considers stabilization of spine |
| 3. Primary Survey | Verbalizes general impression; determines responsiveness (AVPU); determines chief complaint / apparent life threats; assesses airway and breathing; assesses circulation; identifies priority patient / transport decision |
| 4. History Taking & Secondary Assessment | OPQRST and SAMPLE history; vital signs (BP, HR, RR, SpO2, pupils, skin); detailed exam directed by complaint or mechanism |
| 5. Reassessment | Repeat primary survey; repeat vitals; check interventions; document and report changes |
| 6. Critical Criteria Review | Any failure → Fail outcome |

## Critical-criteria authority

The eight critical-criteria failure conditions encoded in step 6 come
directly from the NREMT EMT Patient Assessment / Management skill sheets
historically published by NREMT. The most-cited public reference for the
exact wording is the NREMT EMT skill sheet PDFs available through state
EMS training offices; see for example:

- New York State Department of Health — EMT psychomotor skill sheets:
  <https://www.health.ny.gov/professionals/ems/>
- Texas Department of State Health Services — EMT psychomotor skill sheets:
  <https://www.dshs.texas.gov/emergency-medical-services-ems>

These state-published copies of the federal NREMT sheets are the canonical
public reference for the critical-criteria list.

## Related WHO / international references

For international users adapting this form outside the United States:

- **WHO Basic Emergency Care** (BEC) course manual — provides a
  WHO-equivalent assessment framework (ABCDE) that maps to the NREMT
  primary survey. See <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/basic-emergency-care-approach-to-the-acutely-ill-and-injured>.
- **International Trauma Life Support (ITLS)** and **Prehospital Trauma
  Life Support (PHTLS)** courses use very similar primary-survey
  structures.

## Versioning note

The NREMT skill sheets and candidate handbook are revised periodically.
Implementations should record the handbook revision date at the time of
each examination and re-validate the form against the current published
sheet before any change of NREMT policy is published.
