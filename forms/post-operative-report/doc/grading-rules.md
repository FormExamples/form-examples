# Grading Rules — Post-Operative Report

## Clavien–Dindo Classification of Surgical Complications

The primary instrument is the Clavien–Dindo classification, the most widely
used systematic framework for grading postoperative complications.

- Original publication: Dindo D, Demartines N, Clavien P-A. *Classification
  of surgical complications: a new proposal with evaluation in a cohort
  of 6336 patients and results of a survey.* Ann Surg. 2004;240(2):205-213.
  DOI: 10.1097/01.sla.0000133083.54934.ae. PMID: 15273542.
- Five-year experience update: Clavien PA, Barkun J, de Oliveira ML, et al.
  *The Clavien-Dindo classification of surgical complications: five-year
  experience.* Ann Surg. 2009;250(2):187-196.
  DOI: 10.1097/SLA.0b013e3181b13ca2. PMID: 19638912.
- Reference site (Univ. Hospital Zurich): https://www.assessurgery.com/clavien-dindo-classification/

### Grade definitions

| Grade | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | No complication |
| I | Any deviation from the normal postoperative course not requiring pharmacological treatment or surgical, endoscopic or radiological intervention. Allowed therapeutic regimens: antiemetics, antipyretics, analgesics, diuretics, electrolytes, and physiotherapy; wound infections opened at the bedside |
| II | Requiring pharmacological treatment with drugs other than those allowed for grade I, including blood transfusions and total parenteral nutrition |
| IIIa | Requiring surgical, endoscopic or radiological intervention not under general anaesthesia |
| IIIb | Requiring surgical, endoscopic or radiological intervention under general anaesthesia |
| IVa | Life-threatening complication (single-organ dysfunction, including dialysis) requiring intermediate care / intensive care unit management |
| IVb | Life-threatening complication with multi-organ dysfunction |
| V | Death of a patient |

### Suffix "d"

A suffix "d" (for "disability") can be added if the patient suffers from a
complication at the time of discharge. The form records this on step 9.

## Comprehensive Complication Index (optional secondary)

For multiple complications, the Comprehensive Complication Index (CCI) can
be used as a continuous measure.

- Slankamenac K, Graf R, Barkun J, et al. *The Comprehensive Complication
  Index: a novel continuous scale to measure surgical morbidity.*
  Ann Surg. 2013;258(1):1-7. DOI: 10.1097/SLA.0b013e318296c732. PMID: 23728278.
- CCI calculator: https://www.assessurgery.com/cci-calculator/

This form records the per-event Clavien–Dindo grade and can be paired with
an external CCI calculator for aggregate scoring.

## WHO Surgical Safety Checklist (sign-out)

The sign-out portion is recorded on step 8 (immediate post-op status).

- WHO Surgical Safety Checklist:

## Estimated blood loss thresholds

Banding follows the *Good Surgical Practice* op-note dataset (RCS England)
and the ATLS / NICE major-haemorrhage thresholds.

- Royal College of Surgeons of England, *Good Surgical Practice*.
  https://www.rcseng.ac.uk/standards-and-research/standards-and-guidance/good-practice-guides/good-surgical-practice/
- NICE NG24 *Blood transfusion*. https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng24
