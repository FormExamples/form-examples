# Safety case notes — learning disability assessment

People with a learning disability experience persistently worse health
outcomes than the general population, including a life expectancy gap
that LeDeR reviews have identified as preventable in many cases. This
form is designed to surface safety-critical issues and reasonable
adjustments at the point of care.

## Safety-critical flags surfaced by this form

| Flag                                            | Trigger                                              | Action prompted |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| STOMP concern                                   | Antipsychotic prescribed without clear mental illness diagnosis | Pharmacist / GP review |
| Epilepsy review overdue                         | Known epilepsy + no specialist review in 12 months   | Refer to epilepsy service |
| Constipation / bowel concern                    | Reported constipation OR no bowel review documented  | Investigate and treat |
| Dysphagia / aspiration risk                     | Coughing on food/fluids OR weight loss               | SLT referral |
| Behaviour that challenges, unexplained          | New behaviour change without functional analysis     | Investigate physical / mental / environmental cause |
| Communication needs unmet                       | Patient cannot use the format in which information is presented | Provide easy-read / Makaton / AAC |
| Mental capacity assessment missing              | Decision-specific capacity not recorded              | Conduct and record assessment |
| Best-interests decision required                | Lacks capacity for relevant decision                  | Convene best-interests meeting |
| Cancer screening overdue                        | Mammography / cervical / bowel screening missed      | Reasonable-adjustment booking |
| Reasonable adjustments not recorded             | AIS communication needs not flagged in the record    | Add to Summary Care Record / EPR |

## Diagnostic overshadowing

Diagnostic overshadowing — attributing physical symptoms to the person's
learning disability rather than investigating them — is a recurring
finding in LeDeR. The form prompts the clinician to record symptom
investigation explicitly in the Medical Review step rather than
attributing distress, withdrawal, or behaviour change to the underlying
condition.

## Behaviour as communication

Captured in the Behavioural Concerns & Triggers step. NG93 emphasises
that behaviour that challenges is communication, often of an unmet need.
The clinician should investigate, in order:

1. Acute physical illness (UTI, dental pain, constipation, fracture,
   reflux, ear pain).
2. Chronic untreated pain (osteoarthritis, dysmenorrhoea).
3. Mental health condition (depression, anxiety, psychosis, trauma).
4. Sensory or environmental triggers (noise, lighting, crowding, change
   in routine).
5. Communication difficulties or unmet support need.
6. Adverse medication effect, including akathisia.

## Crisis and admission-avoidance pathway

When acute distress or risk-of-harm emerges during the assessment, the
clinician should:

- Where present risk to self or others is immediate — escalate to the
  Community Learning Disability Team and consider Mental Health Act
  assessment, recording reasonable adjustments throughout.
- Avoid unplanned admission to general hospital wherever possible;
  preferring admission-avoidance via the LD Intensive Support Team where
  available.
- Document reasonable adjustments required for any inpatient setting
  (named staff, sensory environment, communication needs, food
  preferences, behaviour support plan).

## Mental Capacity Act safeguards

The Mental Capacity & Consent step records:

- Whether the person has capacity for the decision in question.
- The practicable steps taken to support capacity (easy-read, Makaton,
  pictures, visit at a familiar time).
- If lacking capacity, who is the consultee or proxy, and the
  best-interests decision-making process.
- Whether a Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) or Court-appointed Deputy
  is in place.
- Whether a Deprivation of Liberty Safeguard (DoLS) authorisation
  applies or is needed.

## Safeguarding

The form prompts the clinician to consider safeguarding under the
Care Act 2014 (England), Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act
2014, or equivalent legislation. Particular vulnerabilities include
hate crime, financial abuse, sexual abuse, and neglect by carers.

## Equality Act 2010 reasonable adjustments

Captured in the Reasonable Adjustments Required step. The form records:

- Longer appointment time.
- Quiet waiting area.
- Familiar carer present.
- Easy-read appointment letter and information.
- Specific communication aids.
- Named contact in the service.
- Recorded on the patient's NHS Summary Care Record or local EPR
  with a learning disability flag.
