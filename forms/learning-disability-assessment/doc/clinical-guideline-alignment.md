# Clinical guideline alignment — learning disability

## NHS England Annual Health Check

Source: NHS England. *Annual health checks for people with a learning
disability*.
URL: https://www.england.nhs.uk/learning-disabilities/improving-health/annual-health-checks/

The NHS Long Term Plan committed to ensuring at least 75% of people aged
14 and over on the GP Learning Disability Register receive an annual
health check. The check covers:

- General health review and physical examination.
- Review of long-term conditions and chronic disease management.
- Epilepsy and medication review.
- Behaviour that may indicate distress or unmet need.
- Communication support needs.
- Mental health screen.
- Production of a Health Action Plan with reasonable adjustments.

This form's 10 steps map directly to the NHS England AHC template.

## NICE NG54 — Mental health problems in people with learning disabilities

Source: NICE. Guideline ID: **NG54** (published September 2016).
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng54

NG54 covers prevention, assessment and management of mental health
problems in people with learning disabilities. Relevant alignments:

- **§1.3 Assessment**: structured, multi-disciplinary, in-depth assessment
  using observation, information from family members and carers, and
  adapted instruments.
- **§1.5 Reasonable adjustments**: communicate in a way that suits the
  person, offer easy-read information, allow longer appointments.
  Captured in Communication Needs and Reasonable Adjustments Required.
- Recommends consideration of the **Disability Distress Assessment Tool
  (DisDAT)** for people who cannot self-report distress.

## NICE NG93 — Learning disabilities and behaviour that challenges

Source: NICE. Guideline ID: **NG93** (published March 2018).
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng93

NG93 covers care and support for adults with learning disabilities and
behaviour that challenges. The Behavioural Concerns & Triggers step
aligns with NG93's emphasis on functional analysis of behaviour and
identification of unmet needs.

## NICE NG11 — Challenging behaviour and learning disabilities

Source: NICE. Guideline ID: **NG11** (published May 2015).
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng11

NG11 covers prevention and interventions for people whose behaviour
challenges. Used in conjunction with NG93.

## STOMP / STAMP — Reducing inappropriate psychotropic medication

Source: NHS England STOMP (Stopping Over-Medication of People with a
learning disability, autism or both) and STAMP (Supporting Treatment and
Appropriate Medication in Paediatrics).
URL: https://www.england.nhs.uk/learning-disabilities/improving-health/stomp/

The Medical Review step prompts the clinician to review psychotropic
medications, with particular attention to antipsychotics prescribed
without a clear mental illness indication.

## LeDeR — Learning from Lives and Deaths

Source: NHS England Learning from Lives and Deaths review of people with
a learning disability and autistic people (LeDeR).
URL: https://www.england.nhs.uk/learning-disabilities/improving-health/mortality-review/

LeDeR identifies persistent gaps in care including delayed cancer
screening, under-treatment of epilepsy, and inadequate management of
constipation, dysphagia, and respiratory disease. The Physical
Examination & Observations and Medical Review steps prompt review of
these high-risk areas.

## UK Mental Capacity Act 2005

URL: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/contents

The Mental Capacity Act sets out the five principles of capacity and the
procedures for best-interests decisions. Captured in the Mental Capacity
& Consent step. Decisions must be:

- Decision-specific.
- Time-specific.
- Made with all practicable steps to support the person.

## UK Accessible Information Standard

Source: NHS England Accessible Information Standard (DCB1605).
URL: https://www.england.nhs.uk/about/equality/equality-hub/patient-equalities-programme/accessible-information-standard/

The Communication Needs step captures the AIS requirements: identify,
record, flag, share, and meet the communication needs of people with a
disability, impairment or sensory loss.

## DSM-5-TR

American Psychiatric Association (2022). DSM-5-TR.
Code **319 / F70–F79** — Intellectual Developmental Disorder
(Intellectual Disability).

DSM-5-TR criteria require:

- Deficits in intellectual functions confirmed by both clinical
  assessment and individualised, standardised intelligence testing.
- Deficits in adaptive functioning resulting in failure to meet
  developmental and sociocultural standards for personal independence and
  social responsibility.
- Onset during the developmental period.
