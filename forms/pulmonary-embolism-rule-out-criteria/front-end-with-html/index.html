Complete the eight PERC criteria below for an adult in whom you have already judged the pre-test probability of pulmonary embolism to be low. PERC is not a numeric score: the result is PERC-negative when the pre-test probability is low AND all eight criteria are satisfied — PE is excluded without a D-dimer or imaging — and PERC-positive otherwise, meaning further workup is warranted. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

The rule is deliberately conservative: a single failed criterion, or a pre-test probability that is not low, makes the patient PERC-positive. PERC does not apply to patients with moderate or high pre-test probability, pregnant patients, or patients on anticoagulation. A PERC-positive result is not a diagnosis of PE; it only means PE cannot be ruled out by PERC alone.

The Pulmonary Embolism Rule-out Criteria (Kline et al., J Thromb Haemost 2004; validated 2008; PROPER trial, JAMA 2018) is a validated rule-out instrument for low-risk emergency-department patients. This tool is for information only and is not a diagnosis; assess, escalate, and investigate in line with NICE NG158 and local venous-thromboembolism policy alongside clinical judgement.
