# Clinical guideline alignment — functional movement screening

Functional Movement Screening sits at the boundary of sports medicine,
physical therapy, athletic training, and occupational health. There is
no single canonical clinical guideline equivalent to NICE in this space;
the relevant authoritative resources are the original FMS publications,
the FMS proprietary certification programme, and the broader sports
medicine / musculoskeletal literature.

## Functional Movement Systems

The FMS instrument is published and curated by Functional Movement
Systems (Cook, Burton et al.).
URL: https://www.functionalmovement.com

The proprietary FMS Level 1 / Level 2 certification programme defines
the standardised administration procedure, scoring rules, and corrective
exercise progressions. Practitioners administering the FMS for clinical
or diagnostic purposes should be appropriately certified.

## American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM)

URL: https://www.acsm.org

ACSM publishes *Guidelines for Exercise Testing and Prescription* (12th
edition, 2025), which is the principal English-language reference for
exercise prescription and pre-participation evaluation.

## NICE clinical guidelines (related but not FMS-specific)

- **NICE NG183** — Spondyloarthritis in over 16s (2017).
- **NICE NG59** — Low back pain and sciatica in over 16s (2016, updated
  2020). https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng59
- **NICE CG177** — Osteoarthritis: care and management (2014, updated
  2022). https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg177 (superseded by NG226)
- **NICE NG226** — Osteoarthritis in over 16s: diagnosis and management
  (2022). https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng226

## Pre-participation evaluation (sport)

- AHA / ACC consensus statements on pre-participation screening for
  competitive athletes — the cardiovascular screening literature is the
  primary clinical reference; FMS is a complementary musculoskeletal
  screen and does not replace cardiovascular pre-participation
  evaluation.

- Maron, B. J., et al. (2014). Assessment of the 12-lead ECG as a
  screening test for detection of cardiovascular disease in healthy
  general populations of young people (12–25 years of age): a scientific
  statement from the American Heart Association and the American
  College of Cardiology. *Circulation*, 130(15), 1303–1334.
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000025
  PubMed ID: 25223981

## Industry / occupational use

The FMS is used in occupational fitness contexts including:

- Military — eligibility and rehabilitation screening.
- Firefighters and emergency responders — task-specific fitness
  screening.
- Industrial / workplace ergonomics — pre-employment musculoskeletal
  screening.

In employment contexts, FMS results must be interpreted in line with
disability discrimination legislation (UK Equality Act 2010; US
Americans with Disabilities Act).

## ICD-11 (relevant only for documenting pain or injury found)

World Health Organization. ICD-11 MMS.
URL: https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en

Chapter 15 — Diseases of the musculoskeletal system or connective tissue;
Chapter 22 — Injury, poisoning or certain other consequences of external
causes. ICD-11 codes are applied where the FMS identifies an underlying
medical condition warranting documentation.

## Differential consideration

Pain or markedly limited movement on FMS testing prompts referral to:

- Sports medicine physician.
- Chartered physiotherapist (UK CSP) or licensed physical therapist.
- Sports / orthopaedic consultant if structural pathology is suspected.

## Note on the term "kinesiology"

In the UK and many countries "kinesiology" refers to the academic study
of human movement and is used by qualified academics, sports scientists,
and licensed health professionals. "Applied kinesiology" (Goodheart et
al.) is a separate complementary-medicine practice that has not been
supported by mainstream evidence reviews. This form addresses the
former: standardised, evidence-graded functional movement assessment.
