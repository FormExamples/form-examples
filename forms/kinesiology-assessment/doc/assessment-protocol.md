# Functional movement assessment protocol

This document describes the FMS administration procedure, the recommended
environmental setup, and follow-up referral pathways.

## Assessor qualifications

The FMS proprietary materials recommend that the screen be administered
by an individual holding FMS Level 1 certification (or equivalent
training). For clinical / diagnostic use, the assessor should hold an
appropriate professional registration (Chartered Physiotherapist,
Physical Therapist, Athletic Trainer, Sports Medicine Physician, or
Strength & Conditioning Coach with FMS certification).

## Equipment

- FMS test kit (dowel, hurdle, board, measuring stick) — or equivalent
  standardized equipment.
- Flat, non-slip surface, at least 3 m × 3 m clear area.
- Standardized footwear (athletic shoes or barefoot, consistent across
  occasions for the same individual).

## Pre-screen preparation

- The participant should be free of acute injury and pain.
- Light warm-up (5–10 minutes general mobility) is permitted but should
  be standardized.
- Explain that the screen measures movement quality, not strength or
  endurance.
- Reassure that scoring is not pass/fail in a competitive sense.
- Capture demographics and the Referral Information step.

## Movement Test order and procedure

Standardized order (as published):

1. **Deep squat** — feet shoulder-width apart, dowel overhead; full squat
   with heels remaining on the ground.
2. **Hurdle step** — narrow base; step over hurdle at tibial tuberosity
   height; touch heel down without losing balance.
3. **In-line lunge** — tandem stance on a board; dowel held vertically
   behind back contacting head, thoracic spine, sacrum.
4. **Shoulder mobility** — closed fists behind back; measure the distance
   between fists.
5. **Active straight leg raise** — supine; raise one leg with knee
   extended; record the position of the malleolus relative to the
   contralateral knee/thigh.
6. **Trunk stability push-up** — prone; thumbs at forehead (men) or
   chin (women); push up as a single rigid plank.
7. **Rotary stability** — quadruped; same-side arm and leg raised, then
   touch elbow to knee under torso.

Each test allows up to 3 attempts; the highest score of the 3 is
recorded.

Clearing tests are administered after their related parent test:

- After test 4: shoulder clearing (hand-to-opposite-shoulder reach).
- After test 6: spinal extension clearing (press-up).
- After test 7: spinal flexion clearing (quadruped rock-back).

Pain on a clearing test forces the related test score to 0.

## Recording and feedback

For each test the assessor records:

- Score (0–3).
- Left and right side scores where bilateral.
- Pain present (yes/no), location, character.
- Compensations observed (e.g. heels rising, knees valgus, hip hike,
  shoulder elevation, low-back arching, breath holding).

After scoring:

- Provide qualitative feedback to the participant.
- Avoid offering specific corrective exercises until a separate session
  unless trained to do so.
- Schedule any required referrals.

## Referral pathways

| Finding                                  | Action                                        |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Any score of 0 (pain present)            | Refer to sports medicine / physiotherapy      |
| Score of 1 on multiple tests             | Recommend corrective-exercise programme       |
| Marked asymmetry (≥ 2-point difference) | Targeted corrective exercise and re-screen    |
| Composite ≤ 14                           | Higher injury risk; corrective programme      |
| Composite ≥ 15 with no pain or asymmetry | Continue training; re-screen periodically     |

## Re-screening cadence

- Active athletes: every 8–12 weeks during training, or after a layoff.
- Rehabilitation: re-screen to monitor progression and inform
  return-to-play decisions.
- Occupational pre-employment: at the start of employment and on
  return-to-work after significant absence.

## Limitations to communicate

- The FMS does not measure strength, speed, power, or endurance.
- A passing FMS does not protect against injury.
- A failing FMS does not predict injury in all individuals.
- FMS is one element of a broader sports medicine evaluation.
