# Clinical guideline alignment — ADHD

This assessment is aligned to the UK NICE guideline and to the diagnostic
classifications used internationally.

## NICE NG87 — Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder: diagnosis and management

Source: National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE).
Guideline ID: **NG87** (published March 2018; updated September 2019).
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87

Key alignments implemented in this form:

- **§1.3.1**: Diagnosis of ADHD in adults requires a **full clinical and
  psychosocial assessment**, including discussion of symptoms across multiple
  settings, full developmental and psychiatric history, and observer reports.
  The form's steps for Childhood History, Functional Impact, and Social &
  Support directly capture this evidence.
- **§1.3.2**: Diagnosis must only be made by a specialist psychiatrist,
  paediatrician, or other appropriately qualified healthcare professional
  with training and expertise in the diagnosis of ADHD.
- **§1.5**: Coexisting conditions (anxiety disorders, depression, autism,
  learning disabilities, substance misuse) should be screened for. The form's
  Comorbid Conditions step prompts the clinician to record these.
- **§1.7**: Pharmacological treatment decisions require recording of baseline
  cardiovascular and growth parameters. Captured in the Medical History step.

## DSM-5-TR

American Psychiatric Association (2022) *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual
of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR)*.
Code **314.0x / F90.x** — Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder.

The DSM-5-TR criteria require:

- ≥5 symptoms of inattention OR ≥5 symptoms of hyperactivity-impulsivity in
  adults (≥6 in children under 17), present for ≥6 months.
- Several symptoms present **before age 12**.
- Impairment in two or more settings.
- Clear evidence of clinically significant impairment.
- Symptoms not better explained by another mental disorder.

The ASRS items map to the DSM symptom list and the form's Functional Impact
step captures cross-setting impairment evidence.

## ICD-11

World Health Organization. ICD-11 for Mortality and Morbidity Statistics
(ICD-11 MMS). Block: Neurodevelopmental disorders. Code **6A05** —
Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder.
URL: https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en

ICD-11 6A05 subtypes captured by this form:

- 6A05.0 — predominantly inattentive presentation
- 6A05.1 — predominantly hyperactive-impulsive presentation
- 6A05.2 — combined presentation
- 6A05.Y / 6A05.Z — other specified / unspecified

ICD-11 differs from DSM-5-TR in not specifying a precise number of symptoms
and in not requiring onset before age 12 (it requires onset during the
developmental period). The clinician should record which classification system
their diagnosis follows.

## Royal College of Psychiatrists

Royal College of Psychiatrists CR235 — *ADHD in adults: good practice
guidance* (2023). The form's Comorbid Conditions and Social & Support steps
align with the RCPsych emphasis on assessing coexisting conditions and the
psychosocial context. See https://www.rcpsych.ac.uk for the index of College
Reports.
