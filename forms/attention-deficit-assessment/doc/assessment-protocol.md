# ADHD assessment protocol

This document describes the interview structure, behavioural observations,
and corroborating-history sources that the clinician should capture
alongside the ASRS-v1.1 screener.

## Interview structure

NICE NG87 §1.3.1 specifies that the diagnostic assessment must cover the
following domains. Each maps to one or more steps in this form.

| Domain                          | Captured in step(s)                           |
| ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Symptom presence and frequency  | ASRS Part A, ASRS Part B                      |
| Onset before age 12 (DSM-5-TR)  | Childhood History                             |
| Cross-setting impairment        | Functional Impact, Social & Support           |
| Coexisting mental health        | Comorbid Conditions                           |
| Substance misuse                | Comorbid Conditions, Social & Support         |
| Physical health and medications | Medical History, Current Medications, Allergies |
| Family history                  | Medical History                               |

## Recommended behavioural observations during interview

The clinician should record the following observable behaviours in the
free-text fields of the relevant steps:

- **Attention drift** — does the patient lose track of the question, ask for
  repetition, or change subject mid-answer?
- **Motor restlessness** — pacing, leg-bouncing, fidgeting, getting up.
- **Impulsivity** — interrupting the clinician, blurting answers before
  the question is complete.
- **Time management** — was the patient late, did they forget appointment
  details, did they bring requested documents?
- **Speech pattern** — rapid, tangential, or hyperverbal speech.

## Corroborating history sources

A diagnosis must not rest on the ASRS alone. The clinician should obtain
corroborative evidence from:

- **Childhood evidence**: school reports, parental account, paediatric
  records. Captured in the Childhood History step.
- **Adult cross-setting evidence**: partner, employer, or close family
  member account. Captured in the Social & Support step.
- **Objective records**: occupational appraisals, driving record (where
  inattention is implicated), academic records.

## Differential diagnosis prompts

NICE NG87 §1.3.4 requires explicit consideration of differential
diagnoses. The clinician should review and record their reasoning for
excluding (or co-diagnosing):

- **Generalized anxiety disorder** (ICD-11 6B00) — anxiety can mimic
  inattention.
- **Major depressive disorder** (ICD-11 6A70) — anhedonia and cognitive
  slowing can mimic inattention.
- **Bipolar affective disorder** (ICD-11 6A60–6A61) — hypomania can mimic
  hyperactivity.
- **Substance misuse** (ICD-11 6C40–6C4Z) — stimulant, alcohol, and
  cannabis use can mimic or mask ADHD.
- **Autism spectrum disorder** (ICD-11 6A02) — frequently co-occurs.
- **Sleep disorders** (ICD-11 7A00–7B2Z) — sleep deprivation produces
  inattention.
- **Thyroid disease** — physical exam and TFTs may be indicated.

## Outcome recording

At the end of the assessment, the clinician records:

- ASRS Part A positive item count (0–6) and whether the threshold (≥4) is met.
- ASRS Part B item codes (for monitoring, not diagnosis).
- Provisional ICD-11 / DSM-5-TR diagnostic code (or "diagnosis deferred").
- Functional impairment category (none / mild / moderate / severe).
- Onward referral or treatment plan, with reasoning.
