# Regulatory Framework

An Advance Decision to Refuse Treatment ("ADRT", sometimes called a "Living
Will") is a legally binding statement that an adult with capacity can make to
refuse specified medical treatment in anticipated future circumstances in
which they have lost capacity. The form is governed primarily by the **Mental
Capacity Act 2005** in England and Wales, supplemented by the Mental Capacity
Act 2005 Code of Practice and case law on consent and capacity.

## Primary statute — Mental Capacity Act 2005

The Mental Capacity Act 2005 ("MCA 2005") creates the statutory framework
for ADRTs in sections 24 to 26.

- Mental Capacity Act 2005 (UK Public General Acts, 2005 c. 9) —
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/contents>
- Sections 24–26 cover advance decisions to refuse treatment —
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/part/1/crossheading/advance-decisions-to-refuse-treatment>

Key statutory requirements distilled from sections 24–26:

| Section | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| s.24(1) | The maker must be aged 18 or over and have capacity at the time of making the decision. |
| s.24(1)(a)–(b) | The decision must identify the treatment to be refused and the circumstances in which the refusal applies. |
| s.25(2) | A decision is **not applicable** if the maker has capacity at the relevant time, the circumstances differ from those specified, or the maker has done anything clearly inconsistent with the decision. |
| s.25(5) | A decision to refuse **life-sustaining treatment** is only valid if it is in writing, signed by the maker (or by another in their presence and at their direction), the signature is witnessed, and it contains an express statement that the decision is to apply "even if life is at risk". |
| s.26 | A valid and applicable advance decision has the same effect as a contemporaneous refusal of treatment by a person with capacity. |

## Code of Practice

The Mental Capacity Act 2005 Code of Practice (Department for Constitutional
Affairs, 2007 — issued under s.42 of the Act) gives statutory guidance that
healthcare professionals must "have regard to" when assessing and applying an
ADRT. Chapter 9 of the Code deals specifically with advance decisions.

- Mental Capacity Act 2005 Code of Practice — index page on GOV.UK:
  <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mental-capacity-act-code-of-practice>

## Interaction with Lasting Power of Attorney

Where the maker has also granted a Lasting Power of Attorney for Health and
Welfare (LPA HW) under s.9 MCA 2005, an ADRT made **before** the LPA is
overridden by the attorney's authority to consent to or refuse the relevant
treatment, unless the LPA reserves the ADRT (MCA 2005 s.25(7)). The
information governance flow for LPAs is published by the UK Office of the
Public Guardian:

- Office of the Public Guardian — Lasting Power of Attorney service:
  <https://www.gov.uk/power-of-attorney>
- LP1H form (Health and Welfare):
  <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/make-a-lasting-power-of-attorney>

## Common-law consent precedent

The validity test for an ADRT mirrors the contemporaneous consent test under
the common law of negligence. The leading modern authority on the duty of
disclosure and the materiality of risks is:

- *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11 —
  <https://www.supremecourt.uk/cases/uksc-2013-0136.html>

Although *Montgomery* concerns consent to (rather than refusal of) treatment,
the principle that the patient's autonomous choice — provided it is informed
and made with capacity — must be respected applies equally to a refusal in an
ADRT.

## Professional standards

The General Medical Council publishes binding standards for doctors that
incorporate the MCA 2005 framework:

- GMC *Decision Making and Consent* (2020) —
  <https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/professional-standards-for-doctors/decision-making-and-consent>
- GMC *Treatment and Care Towards the End of Life: Good Practice in
  Decision-Making* (2010, with subsequent updates) —
  <https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/professional-standards-for-doctors/end-of-life-care>
