# Clinical references

Authoritative guidance underpinning the four-axis grading engine.

## Sleepiness and OSA-risk instruments

| Instrument | Range | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Epworth Sleepiness Scale | 0–24 | >10 indicates abnormal daytime sleepiness; ≥16 severe |
| STOP-BANG | 0–8 | 0–2 low, 3–4 intermediate, 5–8 high OSA risk |
| Neck circumference | cm | ≥43 cm (male) / ≥41 cm (female) raises OSA risk |
| BMI | kg/m² | ≥35 is a STOP-BANG point and OSA-risk factor |

## Study-type selection

| Study type | Typical use |
| --- | --- |
| Home sleep apnoea test (HSAT) | First-line for uncomplicated suspected OSA |
| Polysomnography (PSG) | Complex / discordant cases, comorbidity, non-respiratory sleep disorders |
| Overnight oximetry | Screening where HSAT is unavailable |
| Multiple sleep latency test (MSLT) | Suspected narcolepsy / hypersomnolence |
| Actigraphy | Circadian-rhythm and insomnia assessment |

## Appropriateness (1–9 ordinal)

Three bands: usually-appropriate (7–9), may-be-appropriate (4–6),
usually-not-appropriate (1–3). Mapped from Epworth + STOP-BANG against the
stated indication (NICE NG202 / SIGN). NICE NG202 cautions that the Epworth
score should **not** be used alone to determine whether a referral is needed,
because not everyone with OSAHS or COPD–OSAHS overlap has excessive sleepiness.

## Triage tiers

| Tier | Target | Triggers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Urgent | fast-track / same week | occupational (vocational) driver with excessive sleepiness; severe daytime sleepiness (high Epworth); suspected narcolepsy with sleepiness |
| Routine | standard booking | uncomplicated suspected OSA, snoring, insomnia, restless legs |

DVLA: anyone with excessive sleepiness having or likely to have an adverse
effect on driving should not drive; moderate/severe OSA syndrome must be
notified to the DVLA. Many sleep centres fast-track vocational drivers.
