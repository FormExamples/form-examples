# SBAR — Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation

The form's central architecture is the SBAR communication framework,
originally developed by the US Navy for nuclear submarine
communication and adapted for healthcare by Kaiser Permanente and the
US Institute for Healthcare Improvement (IHI).

## The four SBAR elements

| Element | Maps to step | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S** Situation | Step 4 | What is happening *now*; immediate reason for transfer |
| **B** Background | Step 5 | Relevant history, comorbidities, recent investigations |
| **A** Assessment | Step 6 | Current clinical status, observations, working diagnosis |
| **R** Recommendation | Step 7 | What the requesting clinician asks the receiving team to do |

## Related handover tools

- **I-PASS** — *Illness severity, Patient summary, Action list,
  Situation awareness, Synthesis by receiver.* Used widely in US
  paediatric inpatient handover.
  - Starmer AJ et al. *Changes in medical errors after implementation
    of a handoff program.* N Engl J Med 2014; 371: 1803–1812.
    PMID: 25372088. <https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMsa1405556>
- **ATMIST** — *Age, Time of incident, Mechanism, Injuries, Signs,
  Treatment given.* Used in prehospital trauma handover (UK NHS
  ambulance services).

The form uses SBAR as the dominant pattern because SBAR is the most
widely adopted UK NHS handover convention and is the basis of the
NHS England *QSIR* (Quality, Service Improvement and Redesign)
training.

## Read-back / closed-loop confirmation

The *Sign-off & Acknowledgement* step implements the **read-back**
expectation: the receiving clinician is required to acknowledge
receipt, with optional confirmation of key clinical facts (allergy
status, current medications, working diagnosis).
