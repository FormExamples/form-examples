# Pre-Operative Assessment (POA)

Comprehensive research summary and step-by-step workflow checklist.
Compiled 2026-08-05. UK NHS emphasis (CPOC / NICE / RCoA / AAGBI), with US
(ASA / CMS) comparisons. Sources listed at the end.

---

## Part 1 — Comprehensive summary

### 1. What POA is and what it is for

Pre-operative assessment (POA; US: pre-anesthesia evaluation) is the
structured clinical process, before the day of surgery, that:

1. Identifies medical comorbidities affecting anaesthetic and surgical risk.
2. Optimises the patient's physiological state before surgery (anaemia,
   diabetes, blood pressure, fitness, smoking, alcohol, nutrition).
3. Identifies patients needing enhanced post-operative care (HDU/ICU).
4. Supports shared decision making and informed consent (UK: the
   *Montgomery* standard — disclose all material risks, alternatives, and
   the option of doing nothing).
5. Produces an anaesthesia plan and feeds the WHO Surgical Safety
   Checklist and the intra-operative anaesthetic record.

CPOC (Centre for Perioperative Care, June 2021 guideline) frames the
aspiration for every patient by the day of surgery: shared in the decision;
fully informed of benefits, risks, and alternatives including doing nothing;
significant comorbidities identified and optimised; modifiable risk factors
(smoking, inactivity, alcohol, nutrition, psychological preparedness) being
actively improved; and under active clinical surveillance while waiting —
the waiting list treated as a **preparation list** ("waiting well").

### 2. The end-to-end elective pathway

| Stage | What happens | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 0. GP referral | "Fitness for surgery" referral: key diagnoses, medications, recent results, COVID status, optimisation opportunities. Shared decision making starts at the point surgery is *contemplated*. | GP |
| 1. Surgical clinic / Decision To Treat (DTT) | Indication confirmed; risks discussed (BRAN: Benefits, Risks, Alternatives, do Nothing); patient listed. Screening at DTT: comorbidity, frailty, BMI, functional capacity, procedure severity, risk scoring, polypharmacy. | Surgeon |
| 2. Screening questionnaire | Patient self-assessment health questionnaire at the earliest possible point, ideally digital (ePOA); basis of triage. NHS England mandates digital pre-assessment offers (from Sept 2024); ~25% of patients need completion help. | Patient + care coordinator |
| 3. Triage | Desk triage (risk tools summarised: DASI, SORT, CFS) then stream into remote / nurse-led face-to-face / anaesthetist-led high-risk clinic. | POA nurse ± coordinator |
| 4. POA clinic visit | Structured history, examination (airway focus), observations, risk scores, investigations per NICE NG45. Every surgical patient gets formal POA **before** the day of admission. | POA nurse ± anaesthetist |
| 5. Optimisation / prehabilitation | Anaemia correction, diabetes optimisation, BP management, smoking/alcohol cessation, exercise, nutrition, psychology, surgery school; specialist referrals (cardiology, sleep/CPAP, POPS geriatrics, pain team). | MDT |
| 6. Anaesthetic review & sign-off | High-risk patients reviewed by anaesthetist/perioperative physician; anaesthetic plan, post-op destination, treatment escalation plan documented. | Anaesthetist |
| 7. Scheduling (TCI date) | Date offered only once POA confirms "fit or ready for surgery". NHS Scotland standard: POA ≥12 weeks before the To-Come-In date; POA validity extended to 6 months where safe. | Scheduler |
| 8. Pre-admission checks | Confirmation call; fasting and medication instructions (anticoagulant holds, SGLT2i, day-of ACEi/ARB omission); MRSA decolonisation if positive; escort arranged for day cases. | POA team |
| 9. Day-of-surgery admission | Ward/day-unit pre-op checklist: identity, consent confirmation, site marking, fasting, VTE assessment, glycaemic check, pregnancy test, jewellery/prostheses, results check. | Ward nurse |
| 10. Theatre | WHO Surgical Safety Checklist: team brief → **Sign In** (before induction) → **Time Out** (before incision) → **Sign Out** (before leaving theatre) → debrief. In England embedded in NatSSIPs 2 (2023). | Whole theatre team |

**US comparison (ASA / CMS):** history & physical (H&P) within 30 days
before admission, updated within 24 h of admission; a separate
pre-anesthesia evaluation by a qualified anaesthesia provider within 48 h
of surgery; post-anesthesia evaluation within 48 h after. ASA practice
advisory: high-severity patients assessed by anaesthesia staff *before*
the day of surgery; low-severity/low-invasiveness may be assessed on the day.

### 3. Triage: who sees whom

- **No comorbidities, digitally comfortable** → online/virtual consultation
  + protocolised tests only.
- **Minimal, stable comorbidity** → telephone/video or nurse-led POA.
- **Moderate–significant comorbidity** → nurse-led face-to-face POA with
  anaesthetic notes review and anaesthetist review as needed.
- **High-risk** → anaesthetist-led clinic. Typical escalation criteria:
  - ASA ≥ 3, especially with major/complex surgery
  - Functional capacity < 4 METs (can't climb two flights of stairs)
  - Predicted 30-day mortality > 1% (SORT) → consider enhanced care;
    > 5% → critical care; > 10% → intensivist discussion
  - EF < 40%, symptomatic murmur/valve disease, severe respiratory disease,
    CKD, poorly controlled diabetes
  - Frailty (CFS screen in ≥65s), suspected OSA (STOP-BANG), BMI extremes,
    difficult-airway history, chronic pain on high-dose opioids
- Very-high-risk patients may go to the high-risk clinic **before** the
  surgical approach is even decided, to inform shared decision making.

### 4. Content of the POA clinic visit

**History**
- Presenting condition, planned procedure, side/site, urgency.
- Systems review: cardiac (IHD, MI, heart failure + NYHA class, arrhythmia,
  pacemaker/ICD, valve disease, hypertension), respiratory (asthma, COPD,
  OSA, ability to lie flat), renal, hepatic, endocrine (diabetes type and
  control, thyroid, adrenal/steroids), neuro (stroke/TIA, epilepsy),
  GI (reflux/hiatus hernia → aspiration risk), musculoskeletal (rheumatoid
  arthritis → atlanto-axial instability), sickle cell risk, pregnancy
  possibility.
- Surgical history; **anaesthetic history**: previous GA/regional,
  difficult intubation, awareness, PONV, dental damage.
- **Family anaesthetic history**: malignant hyperthermia, suxamethonium
  (pseudocholinesterase) apnoea, unexplained perioperative death.
- Medicines reconciliation (see §7) and allergies (drug, latex,
  chlorhexidine, plaster — with the nature of each reaction).
- Social: smoking (pack-years), alcohol (units, AUDIT-C), recreational
  drugs, occupation, home support and escort availability (day-case
  suitability), interpreter/communication needs, safeguarding.
- Functional capacity: DASI questionnaire, METs estimate.
- Embedded screens: STOP-BANG, Clinical Frailty Scale (≥65s), cognition
  (AMT-4/MoCA), malnutrition (MUST), psychological distress.

**Examination**
- **Airway** (the anaesthetist's core exam): Mallampati class I–IV,
  thyromental distance (< 6–6.5 cm concerning), mouth opening /
  inter-incisor gap (< 3 cm concerning), neck flexion/extension,
  jaw protrusion / upper-lip bite test, dentition (loose teeth, crowns,
  dentures), beard, cervical-spine stability, prior documented difficult
  intubation.
- Cardiovascular: pulse, BP, JVP, heart sounds/murmurs, oedema.
- Respiratory: SpO₂ on air, auscultation, chest-wall deformity.
- General: BMI (measured), veins, spine (for neuraxial), operative site.
- Baseline observations: HR, BP, SpO₂, RR, temperature.

**Investigations** — per NICE NG45, stratified by ASA grade × surgery grade
(minor / intermediate / major-complex):

| Test | Minor | Intermediate | Major/complex |
|---|---|---|---|
| FBC | Not routine (all ASA) | ASA 3–4: consider (CV/renal disease) | **Yes, all ASA grades** |
| Kidney function | ASA 3–4: consider if AKI risk | ASA 2: consider if AKI risk; ASA 3–4: **yes** | ASA 1: consider if AKI risk; ASA 2–4: **yes** |
| ECG | ASA 3–4: consider if none in 12 months | ASA 2: consider (CV/renal/diabetes); ASA 3–4: **yes** | ASA 1: consider if > 65 and none in 12 months; ASA 2–4: **yes** |
| Haemostasis | Not routine | ASA 3–4: consider in chronic liver disease | Same as intermediate |
| Lung function / ABG | Not routine | ASA 3–4 respiratory: seek senior anaesthetist advice | Same |

All grades: do **not** routinely order chest X-ray, echocardiography
(consider only for murmur + cardiac symptom, or heart-failure signs —
ECG first, discuss with anaesthetist), urine dipstick, sickle testing, or
HbA1c in people without diabetes. For known diabetes: HbA1c within the
last 3 months. Pregnancy: sensitively ask all women of childbearing
potential on the day of surgery; test with consent if any doubt.
Also per local policy: group & save / crossmatch per the Maximum Surgical
Blood Ordering Schedule (MSBOS), MRSA screening swabs, NT-proBNP/BNP for
elevated cardiac risk (2024 ACC/AHA), CPET for high-risk major surgery.

**Outputs of the visit:** ASA grade, risk scores, SORT mortality estimate,
optimisation actions and referrals, anaesthetic alerts and plan, post-op
destination and expected length of stay, day-case suitability decision,
patient information, and the basis for informed consent.

### 5. The key grading and scoring instruments

**ASA Physical Status (amended 2020):**
- **I** — normal healthy patient (non-smoking, minimal alcohol).
- **II** — mild systemic disease, no substantive functional limitation
  (current smoker, pregnancy, BMI 30–40, well-controlled DM/HTN, mild lung
  disease).
- **III** — severe systemic disease with functional limitation (poorly
  controlled DM/HTN, COPD, BMI ≥ 40, pacemaker, moderate EF reduction,
  ESRD on scheduled dialysis, MI/CVA/TIA/stents > 3 months ago).
- **IV** — severe systemic disease that is a constant threat to life
  (MI/CVA/TIA/stents < 3 months, ongoing ischaemia, severe valve
  dysfunction, severe EF reduction, sepsis, DIC, ESRD not on scheduled
  dialysis).
- **V** — moribund, not expected to survive without the operation
  (ruptured aneurysm, massive trauma, intracranial bleed with mass effect).
- **VI** — declared brain-dead organ donor.
- **E** suffix — emergency surgery.

**RCRI (Lee Revised Cardiac Risk Index)** — 1 point each, 0–6:
high-risk surgery (intraperitoneal/intrathoracic/suprainguinal vascular);
ischaemic heart disease; congestive heart failure; cerebrovascular disease;
insulin-treated diabetes; creatinine > 2 mg/dL (> 177 µmol/L).
30-day major cardiac event risk (revalidated): ~3.9% (0), 6.0% (1),
10.1% (2), 15% (≥3). RCRI ≥ 2 = high risk; ≥ 1 often triggers BNP testing.

**STOP-BANG (OSA screen)** — 1 point each, 0–8: Snoring, Tiredness,
Observed apnoea, Pressure (hypertension), BMI > 35, Age > 50,
Neck > 40 cm, Gender male. 0–2 low, 3–4 intermediate, 5–8 high OSA risk.
High risk → sleep studies / OSA precautions; CPAP 4–6 weeks pre-op if
started; do not delay urgent surgery for diagnosis (CPOC OSA 2025).

**Mallampati class** (seated, mouth open, tongue out): I — pillars, uvula,
soft palate all visible; II — uvula and soft palate; III — soft palate and
base of uvula only; IV — soft palate not visible. III–IV predicts
difficult laryngoscopy (combine with thyromental distance, mouth opening,
neck movement, jaw protrusion).

**Clinical Frailty Scale (Rockwood)** 1–9: 1 very fit … 4 vulnerable,
5 mildly frail, 6 moderately frail, 7 severely frail, 8 very severely
frail, 9 terminally ill. CFS ≥ 5 = frail → comprehensive geriatric
assessment; CFS ≥ 7 is a common high-risk safety-flag threshold.

**Functional capacity:** < 4 METs = poor (cannot climb two flights of
stairs). Subjective MET estimation is unreliable (METS trial 2018), so
CPOC recommends the **DASI** questionnaire (0–58.2; < 34 → elevated risk)
for all surgical candidates, with **CPET** as the objective gold standard
where fitness is reduced.

**Mortality calculators:** **SORT** (ASA, urgency, specialty, severity,
malignancy, age band) — CPOC's recommended model; > 1% predicted 30-day
mortality → enhanced care, > 5% → critical care. **NSQIP MICA** (5
variables → MI/cardiac arrest risk) and the **ACS NSQIP** procedure-
specific calculator are the main US tools.

### 6. Fasting

- Classic **2-4-6 rule**: clear fluids up to 2 h, breast milk 4 h,
  formula/solids 6 h (large/fatty meal 8 h per ASA). Chewing gum is no
  longer a reason to cancel.
- **Sip Til Send** (CPOC SOP 2025): patients sip clear water (commonly
  ≤ 170 ml/h) until called to theatre; audits show much shorter fluid
  fasts with no increase in aspiration. Solid-food rules unchanged.
- ERAS adds **carbohydrate loading**: complex-carb clear drink evening
  before + up to 2 h pre-op (non-diabetic patients).

### 7. Medication management (UKCPA handbook conventions)

- **Warfarin**: stop 5 days pre-op; check INR the day before (vitamin K if
  ≥ 1.5); LMWH bridging only for high thrombotic risk (mechanical valve,
  VTE < 3 months, high-risk thrombophilia).
- **DOACs**: no bridging. Standard risk: last dose 24 h pre-op; high
  bleeding risk / renal impairment: 48 h; dabigatran with CrCl < 30:
  up to 72–96 h. Neuraxial blocks need the longer intervals.
- **Antiplatelets**: continue aspirin for most surgery; clopidogrel /
  prasugrel stop 7 days; ticagrelor 5 days. After stents, defer elective
  surgery (≈ ≥ 6 months post-DES; ≥ 12 months post-ACS) and involve
  cardiology; continue aspirin where possible.
- **ACE inhibitors / ARBs**: omit on the day (intra-op hypotension);
  continue beta-blockers and statins.
- **Diabetes**: SGLT2 inhibitors — omit day before + day of surgery
  (euglycaemic DKA risk; check ketones); metformin — continue if only one
  missed meal, eGFR ≥ 30, no contrast; sulfonylureas — omit on the day;
  basal insulin — continue at ~80%, never stop in type 1; VRIII if more
  than one missed meal; target CBG 6–10 (acceptable 4–12) mmol/L.
- **GLP-1 agonists** (semaglutide etc.): delayed gastric emptying →
  aspiration risk. Hold (day of surgery for daily agents; 1 week for
  weekly) or proceed with mitigation (24 h clear-liquid diet, gastric
  ultrasound, rapid-sequence induction); don't routinely cancel.
- **Steroids**: ≥ 5 mg prednisolone/day for ≥ 4 weeks → adrenal
  suppression risk → usual dose plus hydrocortisone cover at induction
  (25–100 mg IV) and post-op taper.
- **Oestrogens** (COCP/HRT): stop 4 weeks before major surgery with
  expected immobilisation (VTE); restart after full mobilisation.
- Continue anticonvulsants, Parkinson's drugs, antipsychotics (timing
  critical); lithium stop 24 h before major surgery; MAOIs need a
  specialist plan; DMARDs/biologics per rheumatology.

### 8. Optimisation targets and thresholds

- **Anaemia** (CPOC 2025): Hb < 130 g/L (men) / < 120–130 g/L (women)
  triggers investigation (exclude malignancy) whenever expected blood loss
  > 500 ml or > 10% blood volume. Oral iron first-line; IV iron for
  intolerance, inflammation/functional deficiency, or urgent surgery.
  Avoid elective major surgery with uncorrected significant anaemia.
- **Diabetes** (CPOC 2023): HbA1c > 69 mmol/mol (8.5%) → refer for
  optimisation before elective surgery; minimise starvation, first on list.
- **Hypertension** (AAGBI/BHS 2016): proceed if primary-care BP < 160/100
  documented in the past 12 months; without that, proceed if clinic BP
  < 180/110; defer only at ≥ 180/110 (refer to GP — do not treat-to-cancel
  on a single reading).
- **Smoking**: quit ≥ 4 weeks (ideally 8) pre-op; even 48 h helps
  (carboxyhaemoglobin); offer NRT at every contact.
- **Alcohol**: screen (AUDIT-C); > 2 units/day raises complications;
  4+ weeks abstinence helps; manage withdrawal risk.
- **COVID-19** (2023 consensus): no elective surgery within 2 weeks of
  infection; between 2 and 7 weeks proceed only if both patient and
  surgery are low risk (structured risk assessment otherwise); beyond
  7 weeks proceed unless ongoing symptoms.
- **Prehabilitation**: aerobic exercise priority, resistance training,
  protein/nutrition, psychological preparation; "surgery school" for all
  major elective patients (DrEaMing: Drinking, Eating, Mobilising).

### 9. Gates — criteria that postpone or cancel surgery

- Clinic BP ≥ 180/110 (Grade 3 hypertension).
- HbA1c > 69 mmol/mol awaiting optimisation; day-of CBG grossly out of
  range or ketosis.
- Recent MI/ACS/stroke (elective: ≥ 6 months post-elective-PCI, ≥ 12
  months post-ACS; risk elevated up to ~9 months post-stroke).
- COVID-19 infection < 2 weeks (and 2–7 weeks unless low-risk).
- Active infection (febrile illness, UTI before implant surgery, surgical
  site skin infection).
- Significant uncorrected anaemia.
- Missing or invalid consent; missing group & save for MSBOS procedures;
  INR out of range / anticoagulant hold missed.
- Inadequate fasting (solids < 6 h).
- No responsible adult escort/home support for day surgery under GA.
- Positive pregnancy test (defer unless emergency).
- POA validity expired or patient deteriorated since assessment.

### 10. Day-of-surgery verification and the WHO checklist

**Ward pre-op checklist:** positive ID (two bands; red = allergy), consent
present/signed/matching (confirmation-of-consent stage if signed earlier),
site marked by the operating surgeon (indelible, visible after draping),
fasting confirmed, VTE risk assessment done + prophylaxis, capillary
glucose for diabetics, pregnancy test where applicable, baseline
observations (NEWS2), allergies re-verified, MRSA status, jewellery/
prostheses/dentures removed or documented, medications given/held as
instructed, results and G&S validity checked, escort confirmed, documents
travel with the patient.

**WHO Surgical Safety Checklist** (NatSSIPs 2 in England):
- **Sign In** (before induction; anaesthetist + nurse): patient confirms
  identity/site/procedure/consent; site marked; anaesthesia machine and
  medication check complete; pulse oximeter on; allergies; difficult
  airway/aspiration risk; risk of > 500 ml blood loss (7 ml/kg children)
  with access/fluids planned.
- **Time Out** (before incision; whole team): introductions by name and
  role; verbal confirmation of patient/site/procedure; antibiotic
  prophylaxis within the last 60 minutes; anticipated critical events
  (surgeon: steps/duration/blood loss; anaesthetist: patient concerns;
  nursing: sterility, equipment); essential imaging displayed.
- **Sign Out** (before leaving theatre): procedure recorded; instrument,
  swab, and needle counts correct; specimens labelled and read aloud;
  equipment problems flagged; recovery concerns handed over.
- Plus team brief before the list and debrief after.

### 11. Documentation by stage

1. GP referral letter (fitness-for-surgery detail).
2. Surgical clinic letter + waiting-list/DTT form.
3. Patient health screening questionnaire (paper or ePOA digital).
4. POA clinic record (history, exam, observations, ASA, risk scores,
   investigations, referrals, alerts, post-op plan).
5. Investigation requests incl. group & save / crossmatch per MSBOS.
6. Consent forms (UK DH model set): Form 1 adult with capacity; Form 2
   parental for a child; Form 3 procedures without impaired consciousness;
   Form 4 adult lacking capacity (best interests, MCA 2005). Two-stage
   consent: discussion well before the day, confirmation on the day.
7. Anaesthetic chart (pre-op section at review; intra-op record on the day).
8. Ward pre-operative checklist.
9. National VTE risk assessment tool.
10. WHO Surgical Safety Checklist (trust-adapted).
11. Drug chart: pre-meds, VTE and antibiotic prophylaxis, perioperative
    diabetes plan.

### 12. Roles and timing windows

**Roles:** perioperative care coordinator (non-clinical triage/chasing),
POA nurse (runs clinics, escalates per criteria), pharmacist (medicines
reconciliation, high-risk drug plans), anaesthetist/perioperative physician
(high-risk clinic, plan, sign-off), surgeon (DTT, consent, site marking),
geriatrician (POPS), dietitian, psychologist, physiotherapist, intensivist.

**Timing:** screening at referral/DTT, at latest on joining the waiting
list; POA before a date is offered (Scotland: ≥ 12 weeks before TCI);
POA validity traditionally 12 weeks, extending to 6 months where safe
(shorter for unstable patients); bloods commonly valid 3 months, ECG 6–12
months if stable; waiting-list contact at least every 3 months; antibiotic
prophylaxis within 60 min of incision; US: H&P ≤ 30 days + 24 h update,
pre-anesthesia evaluation ≤ 48 h.

### 13. Day surgery and ERAS

Day surgery is the default; there are **no absolute exclusions** — suitability
is decided by functional status, not ASA grade, age, or BMI per se (stable
ASA 3 patients often do better as day cases). Social criteria: responsible
adult escort and 24 h home support, telephone access, reasonable travel
time. Surgical criteria: oral/regional-controllable pain, no major blood
loss, early oral intake, rapid recovery. OSA does not preclude day surgery
with opioid-sparing technique. ERAS pre-op elements: counselling/surgery
school, smoking and alcohol cessation ≥ 4 weeks, anaemia correction,
prehabilitation, minimal fasting + carb loading, no routine bowel prep or
sedative premedication, VTE and antibiotic prophylaxis, PONV risk scoring.

### 14. How this repo models the POA pathway

The monorepo implements the pathway as four linked forms:

- **`pre-operative-assessment-by-patient`** — 16-step patient self-report
  questionnaire; 42-rule declarative ASA engine (max-grade, ASA I–IV
  auto-assigned) plus ~20 safety flags (difficult airway, anaphylaxis,
  anticoagulants, malignant hyperthermia, pacemaker, pregnancy…).
- **`pre-anaesthesia-assessment`** / **`pre-operative-assessment-by-clinician`**
  (clinically identical) — 16-step clinician wizard recording objective
  findings; computes ASA I–VI, Mallampati, RCRI, STOP-BANG, Clinical
  Frailty Scale, and a composite Low/Moderate/High/Critical risk;
  clinician override with mandatory reason, both computed and final grades
  persisted. Hard gates: Mallampati required, fasting confirmation
  required for elective/urgent, NHS number required, anticoagulant hold
  plan flagged if missing. Maps explicitly to CPOC 2021 sections and the
  NICE NG45 test matrix; hazard log per DCB0129/0160; MDCG Class IIa.
- **`who-surgical-safety-checklist`** — the three-phase theatre checklist
  with team roster, completion states, and independent safety flags
  (Class I — process record, not decision support).
- **`anaesthetic-record`** — the intra-operative chart downstream of POA;
  carries ASA, airway findings, and WHO checklist status forward; grades
  record *completeness* rather than clinical severity.

Pathway: patient questionnaire → clinician POA (ASA + composite risk +
flags + anaesthesia plan) → WHO checklist handover → anaesthetic record.

## Part 2 — Step-by-step workflow checklist

### Stage A — Referral and listing

- [ ] GP referral includes diagnoses, medications, recent results, BP
      reading from the last 12 months, HbA1c if diabetic, COVID status,
      and optimisation opportunities ("fitness for surgery" referral).
- [ ] Shared decision making started (BRAN: Benefits, Risks, Alternatives,
      do Nothing); capacity considered (MCA 2005).
- [ ] Surgeon confirms indication; Decision To Treat recorded; patient
      listed with procedure, laterality, urgency, and surgery grade
      (minor / intermediate / major-complex).
- [ ] Screening at DTT: comorbidities, frailty, BMI, functional capacity,
      procedure severity, polypharmacy.
- [ ] "Waiting well" measures started: smoking, alcohol, activity, diet,
      psychological preparation.

### Stage B — Screening questionnaire and triage

- [ ] Patient completes the self-assessment health questionnaire (digital
      ePOA where possible; assisted completion offered).
- [ ] Desk triage summarises DASI, SORT, and Clinical Frailty Scale for
      the POA practitioner.
- [ ] Stream assigned: remote / nurse-led face-to-face / anaesthetist-led
      high-risk clinic.
- [ ] Escalate to high-risk clinic if any of: ASA ≥ 3 with major surgery,
      < 4 METs, SORT 30-day mortality > 1%, EF < 40%, symptomatic murmur,
      severe respiratory disease, CFS ≥ 5, STOP-BANG ≥ 5, difficult-airway
      history, high-dose opioids.

### Stage C — POA clinic visit

- [ ] Confirm identity (name, DOB, NHS number) and planned procedure,
      side, urgency.
- [ ] Full systems history; previous surgical and **anaesthetic history**
      (difficult intubation, awareness, PONV).
- [ ] Family anaesthetic history: malignant hyperthermia, suxamethonium
      apnoea.
- [ ] Medicines reconciliation (anticoagulants, antiplatelets, insulin,
      SGLT2i, GLP-1, ACEi/ARB, steroids, oestrogens, herbal); pharmacist
      review for high-risk drugs.
- [ ] Allergies documented with reaction nature (drug, latex,
      chlorhexidine, plaster).
- [ ] Social history: smoking, alcohol (AUDIT-C), drugs, home support,
      escort availability, interpreter needs, safeguarding.
- [ ] Airway exam: Mallampati, thyromental distance, mouth opening, neck
      movement, jaw protrusion, dentition, prior difficult airway.
- [ ] Cardiovascular and respiratory exam; SpO₂ on air; measured height,
      weight, BMI; baseline observations.
- [ ] Scores recorded: ASA grade, RCRI, STOP-BANG, CFS (≥ 65s), DASI/METs,
      SORT mortality estimate; cognition and malnutrition screens.
- [ ] Investigations ordered per NICE NG45 (ASA × surgery grade): FBC,
      renal function, ECG, ± haemostasis/HbA1c/pregnancy policy/G&S per
      MSBOS/MRSA swabs; echo only for murmur + symptoms or heart-failure
      signs; no routine CXR.
- [ ] Day-case suitability decided (functional status + social criteria,
      not ASA/age/BMI alone).

### Stage D — Optimisation (act on findings)

- [ ] Anaemia: Hb below threshold → investigate cause, start oral iron
      (IV if intolerant/inflammation/urgent).
- [ ] Diabetes: HbA1c > 69 mmol/mol → refer for optimisation; write the
      perioperative diabetes plan; schedule first on list.
- [ ] BP ≥ 180/110 in clinic (and no compliant primary-care reading) →
      refer to GP; otherwise proceed.
- [ ] Suspected OSA (STOP-BANG high) → sleep referral; CPAP 4–6 weeks if
      started; don't delay urgent surgery.
- [ ] Cardiac red flags (new murmur + symptoms, RCRI ≥ 2, EF < 40%) →
      ECG/echo/BNP per pathway, cardiology as needed.
- [ ] Frailty CFS ≥ 5 → comprehensive geriatric assessment (POPS).
- [ ] Smoking/alcohol cessation support; prehabilitation prescription;
      surgery-school invitation for major elective surgery.
- [ ] COVID timing checked: defer if < 2 weeks post-infection; 2–7 weeks
      → structured risk assessment unless low-risk.
- [ ] Anaesthetist sign-off for high-risk patients: anaesthetic plan,
      post-op destination (> 1% mortality → enhanced care; > 5% →
      critical care), treatment escalation plan.

### Stage E — Scheduling and pre-admission

- [ ] TCI date offered only once "fit or ready for surgery"; POA validity
      window checked (typically 12 weeks–6 months; shorter if unstable).
- [ ] Stage-1 consent taken at clinic (material risks, alternatives,
      written information; correct form 1–4).
- [ ] Written fasting instructions given (6 h solids; clear fluids to 2 h
      or sip-til-send per local SOP; carb drink if ERAS).
- [ ] Written medication instructions: warfarin stop day −5 (INR day −1);
      DOAC stop 24–48 h (dabigatran + renal impairment up to 96 h);
      clopidogrel/prasugrel day −7, ticagrelor day −5; SGLT2i omit day −1
      and day 0; GLP-1 hold per agent; ACEi/ARB omit day 0; steroids —
      plan hydrocortisone cover; COCP/HRT stopped 4 weeks before major
      surgery.
- [ ] MRSA-positive patients complete decolonisation.
- [ ] Day-case escort and 24 h home support confirmed.
- [ ] Waiting patients re-contacted at least every 3 months; health-change
      re-alert process in place.

### Stage F — Day of surgery (ward / day unit)

- [ ] Positive ID; two ID bands (red if allergy); notes match listing.
- [ ] Consent form present and signed; confirmation of consent completed;
      procedure and side match.
- [ ] Surgical site marked by the operating surgeon, visible after draping.
- [ ] Fasting confirmed and recorded (last solids, last clear fluids).
- [ ] Medication compliance verified (anticoagulant hold, SGLT2i held,
      usual drugs taken as instructed); INR checked if on warfarin.
- [ ] VTE risk assessment completed; stockings applied; pharmacological
      prophylaxis prescribed.
- [ ] Capillary glucose checked in diabetics (act if > 12 mmol/L; check
      ketones if SGLT2i involved).
- [ ] Pregnancy status asked; urine test with consent if any doubt.
- [ ] Baseline observations (NEWS2); allergies re-verified; MRSA status
      noted.
- [ ] Jewellery, prostheses, dentures, contact lenses removed/documented;
      hearing aids noted.
- [ ] Blood results and group & save validity confirmed (crossmatch for
      high-blood-loss procedures per MSBOS).
- [ ] Notes, drug chart, consent, POA record, ECG, and imaging travel
      with the patient.

### Stage G — Theatre (WHO Surgical Safety Checklist / NatSSIPs)

- [ ] **Team brief** at the start of the list: cases, roles, equipment.
- [ ] **Sign In** (before induction): patient confirms identity, site,
      procedure, consent; site marked; anaesthesia machine and medication
      check complete; pulse oximeter on and working; allergies declared;
      difficult airway / aspiration risk plan; > 500 ml blood-loss risk →
      two IVs and fluids planned.
- [ ] **Time Out** (before incision): all team members introduced by name
      and role; patient/site/procedure verbally confirmed by the whole
      team; antibiotic prophylaxis within the last 60 minutes; surgeon
      states critical steps, duration, expected blood loss; anaesthetist
      states patient-specific concerns; nursing confirms sterility and
      equipment; essential imaging displayed.
- [ ] **Sign Out** (before the patient leaves theatre): procedure name
      recorded; instrument, swab, and needle counts correct; specimens
      labelled and read aloud with patient name; equipment problems
      flagged; recovery and post-op concerns handed over.
- [ ] **Debrief** at the end of the list.

### Stop / defer triggers (any stage)

- [ ] BP ≥ 180/110 → defer elective, refer to GP.
- [ ] Ketosis or grossly deranged glucose → defer, treat.
- [ ] MI/ACS/stroke within deferral window → defer elective.
- [ ] COVID-19 < 2 weeks (or 2–7 weeks without low-risk justification).
- [ ] Active infection at the surgical site or systemic febrile illness.
- [ ] Fasting violation (solids < 6 h) → delay or cancel.
- [ ] Missing/invalid consent, missing G&S, INR out of range.
- [ ] Positive pregnancy test → defer unless emergency.
- [ ] No escort for day-case GA → convert to overnight or postpone.
- [ ] POA expired or significant deterioration → reassess.
