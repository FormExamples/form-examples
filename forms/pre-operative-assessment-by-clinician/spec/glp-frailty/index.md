# GLP frailty

During a preoperative assessment, combining GLP-1 receptor agonist (GLP-1 RA) management with a frailty assessment is crucial to prevent severe surgical complications like pulmonary aspiration, postoperative delirium, and profound functional decline. [1, 2] 
------------------------------
## 1. GLP-1 Receptor Agonist Management
GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., semaglutide, tirzepatide, dulaglutide) significantly delay gastric emptying. This requires specific perioperative adjustments to reduce the risk of pulmonary aspiration under anesthesia. [1, 3, 4, 5, 6] 

* Preoperative Fasting Options:
* Extended Fasting: High-quality guidelines suggest implementing a 24-hour solid food fast combined with clear liquids for 4 to 8 hours prior to the procedure.
   * Holding the Medication: Alternatively, clinical teams may hold daily formulations on the day of surgery, and weekly formulations exactly one week before the procedure. [7, 8, 9] 
* Symptom Screening: Check for active gastrointestinal symptoms (severe nausea, vomiting, bloating, or abdominal pain). If present, discuss delaying elective surgery due to a significantly elevated risk of a "full stomach". [4, 9, 10, 11] 
* Anesthetic Strategy: If medications were not held or the patient remains symptomatic, teams utilize full-stomach precautions, consider point-of-care gastric ultrasound, or opt for regional anesthesia where appropriate. [9, 12] 

------------------------------
## 2. Preoperative Frailty Assessment
Frailty reflects a decline in physical and physiological reserves, making patients exceptionally vulnerable to surgical stressors. Guidelines from the [British Geriatrics Society (BGS)](https://www.bgs.org.uk/guideline-for-the-care-of-people-living-with-frailty-undergoing-elective-and-emergency-surgery) recommend systematic screening for all surgical patients over 65. [13, 14, 15] 

* Screening Tools:
* Clinical Frailty Scale (CFS): A quick, highly predictive 9-point clinician-rated scale evaluating baseline functional status and independence.
   * Fried Phenotype Scale: Measures 5 objective physical criteria (weakness, slowness, low activity, exhaustion, and unintentional weight loss).
   * Risk Analysis Index (RAI): A 14-variable deficit accumulation tool widely validated for predicting postoperative complications. [2, 14, 16, 17, 18] 
* Triggering Interventions:
* Score of CFS ≥ 5: Directly triggers a formal cognitive assessment (e.g., Mini-Cog) to evaluate delirium risk, alongside a Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment (CGA).
   * Prehabilitation: Multimodal programs focusing on high-protein dietetic optimization, aerobic training, and resistance exercises should be initiated 2 to 6 weeks before surgery to mitigate functional decline. [2, 15, 19, 20] 

------------------------------
## Intersecting Risks: The Fragile Patient on GLP-1s
When a frail patient is also taking a GLP-1 RA, special precautions are required: [21] 

   1. Accelerated Sarcopenia: GLP-1 RAs can induce muscle mass loss alongside fat loss. Frail patients require intensive protein supplementation and resistance prehabilitation to avoid worsening baseline weakness. [2, 8, 20, 22, 23] 
   2. Dehydration and Electrolyte Shift: Prolonged pre-op fasting or active GLP-1 side effects (nausea/diarrhoea) can rapidly trigger acute kidney injury (AKI) or delirium in a frail system. [24, 25, 26] 
   3. Glycaemic Management: If a GLP-1 RA is held or fasting is extended, strict blood glucose tracking is required to prevent rebound hyperglycaemia or hypoglycaemia if combined with insulin. [24, 27, 28]
