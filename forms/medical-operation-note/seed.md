# Medical operation note

A medical operation note (or "op note") is a critical legal document written by the surgical team immediately following a procedure. It ensures continuity of care and guides post-operative management. According to the Royal College of Surgeons, it should be clear, legible, and ideally typed.Essential Components of an Op NoteA properly structured operation note should clearly document the following:Patient Details: Name, hospital number, date of birth, and date/time of the surgery.Surgical Team: Name of the primary surgeon, assistant(s), and the anesthetist.Procedure Names: The exact intended procedure and the principal procedure performed.Incisions & Approaches: The type, location, and length of any incisions.Operative Findings: A detailed, step-by-step account of what was discovered and done inside.Materials & Devices: Any sutures, ligatures, implants, or prostheses used.Drains & Specimens: Any drains, catheters, or packs left in situ, and any tissue samples or specimens sent to pathology.Complications & Difficulties: Documentation of any unexpected events, blood loss, or anatomical anomalies.Safety & Verification: Confirmation of the "WHO Surgical Safety Checklist" (e.g., swab, needle, and instrument counts).Post-Operative Plan: Specific instructions regarding monitoring, medications, diet, and DVT prophylaxis.Typical Op Note TemplateMany hospitals utilize electronic or standardized templates (such as those discussed on MyOpNotes) to ensure all clinical and legal requirements are met.A standard structure often looks like this:Pre-Operative
