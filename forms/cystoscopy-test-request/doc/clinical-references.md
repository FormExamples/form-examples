# Clinical references

Authoritative guidance underpinning the four-axis grading engine.

## Cancer-pathway thresholds (NICE NG12)

| Pathway | Threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Two-week-wait (visible haematuria) | Aged ≥ 45 with unexplained visible haematuria without UTI, or visible haematuria that persists / recurs after UTI treatment |
| Two-week-wait (non-visible haematuria) | Aged ≥ 60 with non-visible haematuria and either dysuria or a raised white-cell count |

## Procedure selection (BAUS haematuria guidance)

Flexible cystoscopy under local anaesthesia is the preferred first-line
investigation of haematuria unless patient choice or other factors make it
inappropriate. Any single episode of visible haematuria is considered
significant and warrants urological assessment, which in almost all cases
includes cystoscopy. Where imaging and cystoscopy find no cause for non-visible
haematuria, the patient may be discharged from secondary-care follow-up.

## Appropriateness (Axis A)

Ordinal 1–9 scale in three bands: usually-appropriate (7–9),
may-be-appropriate (4–6), usually-not-appropriate (1–3). Mapped per indication,
with visible haematuria, suspected bladder tumour, and bladder-cancer
surveillance scoring highest.

## Cancer-pathway urgency (Axis B)

| Tier | Target | Triggers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency | same day | acute clot retention, haemodynamic compromise |
| Two-week-wait | ≤ 14 days | meets a NICE NG12 suspected-cancer threshold |
| Urgent | ≤ 2–4 weeks | symptomatic but below the 2WW threshold |
| Routine | standard booking | surveillance, recurrent UTI, LUTS |

## Pre-procedure risk (Axis D)

| Band | Triggers |
| --- | --- |
| High | anticoagulant with no peri-procedural plan; active UTI present |
| Moderate | antiplatelet therapy; controlled bleeding-risk factors |
| Low | none of the above |

Active UTI defers the procedure (infection risk on instrumentation); high
bleeding risk on anticoagulation requires a documented peri-procedural plan.
