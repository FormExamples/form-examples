# Assessment protocol — DVLA M1 mental health screening

The M1 questionnaire captures psychiatric, behavioural, and substance-use
information needed by the DVLA Medical Adviser to decide fitness to drive.
This document maps each M1 section to the relevant chapter of the
"Assessing fitness to drive" guide and notes the standard time-bars.

Authoritative source: **"Assessing fitness to drive: a guide for medical
professionals"** — DVLA Drivers Medical Group. Current edition:
<https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/assessing-fitness-to-drive-a-guide-for-medical-professionals>

## Sections and corresponding guidance

### 1. Anxiety and depression

Guidance: Chapter "Psychiatric disorders — Anxiety and depression".

Differentiates mild/moderate (often compatible with driving) from severe
(memory/concentration impairment, agitation, suicidal ideation, behavioural
disturbance — must not drive).

### 2. Psychotic illness (including schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder,
delusional disorder)

Guidance: Chapter "Psychiatric disorders — Psychotic disorders".

Group 1: typically 3-month stability, insight maintained, compliant with
treatment, no side-effects affecting driving. Group 2: 3-year stability,
specialist report required.

### 3. Bipolar affective disorder

Guidance: Chapter "Psychiatric disorders — Bipolar disorder".

Time-bars during acute mood episodes (mania, hypomania, severe depression).
Driving permitted between episodes with insight.

### 4. Personality disorder

Guidance: Chapter "Psychiatric disorders — Personality disorder".

Driving permitted unless behaviour is associated with serious disturbance
likely to be a danger at the wheel.

### 5. Alcohol — misuse and dependence

Guidance: Chapter "Drug and substance misuse — Alcohol".

- **Persistent alcohol misuse**: 6 months off driving for Group 1, 1 year
  for Group 2, with evidence of cessation/controlled use.
- **Alcohol dependence**: 1 year off driving for Group 1, 3 years for
  Group 2, with confirmed abstinence/controlled drinking, no harmful end-
  organ effects.

### 6. Drug misuse and drug dependence

Guidance: Chapter "Drug and substance misuse — Drugs".

Separate criteria for cannabis, opioids (including methadone /
buprenorphine substitution), cocaine, amphetamines, benzodiazepines, and
"other" psychoactive substances. Time-bars range from 6 months to 3 years
depending on substance and group.

### 7. Learning disability, ADHD, autism spectrum disorder

Guidance: Chapter "Neurodevelopmental disorders".

Generally compatible with driving if behaviour is stable, no associated
condition (e.g. epilepsy) bars driving, and applicant can demonstrate
adequate driving ability. ADHD stimulant medication must not impair driving.

### 8. Dementia and cognitive impairment

Cross-referenced from B1 — Neurological / Dementia chapter.

## Reporting outcome

The DVLA Medical Adviser issues one of:

- Licence retained / re-issued (with or without time limit).
- Short-period licence (typically 1, 2, 3, or 5 years).
- Refusal / revocation under Road Traffic Act 1988 s.93.

## Appeal

A driver may appeal a refusal or revocation to a magistrates' court (Road
Traffic Act 1988 s.100); time limit 6 months from notification.

## Sources of corroborating information

- General practitioner medical records.
- Treating consultant psychiatrist / community mental health team report.
- Substance-use service (NHS or third-sector) report.
- Police records (alcohol-related driving convictions: see RTA 1988 s.5
  and ss.6–11A).
