# Regulatory framework — DVLA M1 (mental health and behaviour)

The M1 questionnaire collects psychiatric and behavioural information used by
the Driver & Vehicle Licensing Agency (DVLA, Swansea) to assess fitness to
drive Group 1 (cars and motorcycles) and Group 2 (large goods vehicles and
passenger carrying vehicles) licences.

## Primary statute

- **Road Traffic Act 1988**, sections 92–94 (notification of relevant or
  prospective disability; refusal/revocation on medical grounds).
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/52/contents>
- **Motor Vehicles (Driving Licences) Regulations 1999** (SI 1999/2864),
  Schedule 8. <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1999/2864/contents>
- EU Directive **2006/126/EC**, Annex III §13–§14 (mental disorders;
  alcohol; drugs and medicinal products).

## DVLA medical standards

Clinical thresholds are published in **"Assessing fitness to drive: a guide
for medical professionals"** — DVLA Drivers Medical Group, current edition:
<https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/assessing-fitness-to-drive-a-guide-for-medical-professionals>

Relevant chapters for M1:

1. Psychiatric disorders — anxiety and depression, psychotic illness,
   personality disorder, bipolar affective disorder.
2. Drug and substance misuse — alcohol dependence, alcohol misuse,
   cannabis, opioids, cocaine and amphetamines, benzodiazepines.
3. Sleep disorders (cross-references B1 chapter).
4. Learning disability, ADHD, autism spectrum disorder.
5. Dementia and cognitive impairment (cross-references B1 chapter).

## Typical thresholds (current guide editions)

- **Anxiety / depression — mild to moderate**: may continue to drive Group 1
  if maintained at a stable level and side effects of medication do not
  impair driving.
- **Severe anxiety / depression with significant memory or concentration
  problems, agitation, behavioural disturbance, or suicidal thoughts**:
  must not drive; must notify DVLA.
- **Psychotic disorders**: Group 1 — driving permitted after stable
  recovery (typically 3 months), insight regained, complying with
  treatment, no medication side effects affecting driving. Group 2 — 3-year
  stability period; specialist report required.
- **Bipolar affective disorder**: Group 1 — driving permitted between
  episodes with insight and stability; specific time-bars during acute
  episodes.
- **Alcohol dependence**: Group 1 — minimum 1-year off driving with
  confirmed abstinence/controlled drinking; Group 2 — 3 years.

The current guide is the authoritative source for exact periods — figures
are kept in step with the guide rather than restated here.

## Driver duty to notify

Drivers must notify DVLA of any condition that affects, or may affect, safe
driving (Road Traffic Act 1988 s.94). Failure to do so is an offence
punishable by a fine up to £1,000 and may invalidate motor insurance.

- Driver-facing notification: <https://www.gov.uk/driving-medical-conditions>
- Online service: <https://www.gov.uk/health-conditions-and-driving>

## Data protection

- UK GDPR Article 9(2)(g) — substantial public interest (driver licensing
  safety).
- Data Protection Act 2018, Schedule 1, Part 2, paragraph 6.
- DVLA processing notice:
  <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dvla-privacy-policy>

Mental-health data is "special category" data under UK GDPR Article 9.
Storage and transmission of M1 responses must use the same controls as
NHS-grade patient data (DCB0129/0160, DSPT).
