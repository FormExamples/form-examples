# Audit Trail Rules

The Medical Records Release Permission form produces an authorization
record and (separately) a disclosure log. This document specifies the
retention, accounting, and access rules that satisfy UK GDPR Article 5(2)
and HIPAA § 164.528 accounting-of-disclosures.

## Two records, distinct purposes

| Record | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Authorization record | Evidences the patient's signed authorization and its scope. |
| Disclosure log | Evidences each actual disclosure: when, what, to whom. |

The form captures the authorization record. The disclosure log is
generated by the system that actually fulfils the release; both must be
retained.

## Retention

### UK / NHS

- NHS Records Management Code of Practice 2021: GP electronic patient
  record retained for lifetime of patient plus 10 years.
- Authorization records form part of the patient record and inherit this
  retention.

NHS England Records Management Code:
<https://www.nhsx.nhs.uk/information-governance/guidance/records-management-code/>

### US / HIPAA

- 45 CFR § 164.530(j) — covered entity must retain the authorization for
  at least 6 years from the date it was created or last in effect,
  whichever is later.
- Disclosure accounting under § 164.528 must be available for the 6 years
  preceding the request.

45 CFR § 164.530:
<https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-E/section-164.530>

## Disclosure log fields

Every actual disclosure must be logged with:

- Patient identifier.
- Recipient identity and contact.
- Date of disclosure.
- Records / data items disclosed.
- Purpose of disclosure (must match the authorization).
- Operator identity (the user who effected the disclosure).
- Authorization reference (which signed authorization supports it).

## Accounting of disclosures

Under HIPAA § 164.528 a patient may request an accounting of disclosures.
The dashboard supports an export of:

- All disclosures made under the named authorization.
- All other disclosures of the patient's records during the period (e.g.
  legal-obligation disclosures, court orders) so far as § 164.528
  exemptions do not exclude them.

§ 164.528 exclusions include disclosures for treatment, payment, and
healthcare operations.

## Withdrawal handling

When a patient withdraws an authorization:

- The authorization status changes to `withdrawn`.
- The dashboard emits `flag_authorisation_withdrawn`.
- The disclosure log records the withdrawal as a state change with
  timestamp.
- Further disclosures must not occur under that authorization.
- Disclosures already made remain in the disclosure log; withdrawal is
  not retrospective.

## Re-disclosure warning

The authorization record explicitly warns the patient that information
disclosed under the authorization may be re-disclosed by the recipient
and no longer protected by UK GDPR (where the recipient is outside the
controller's scope) or by HIPAA (where the recipient is not a covered
entity).

This warning is in Step 7 of the wizard, satisfying:

- ICO consent guidance — informed consent.
- 45 CFR § 164.508(c)(2)(iii) — required statement on re-disclosure.

## Access controls

The authorization record is part of the patient's clinical record and
inherits its RBAC. Read access is restricted to:

- The patient's registered clinician and clinical staff with a legitimate
  relationship.
- The records-release administrator who fulfils the release.
- The compliance / privacy officer for audit.
- The patient (via Subject Access / HIPAA right of access).

The patient is entitled to a copy of their own authorization on request.
