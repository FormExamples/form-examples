# Inter-Facility Transfer Protocol

This document describes the bedside protocol for completing the WHO
Acute Referral Form during an inter-facility transfer.

## When to use

The form is used whenever a patient is transferred from one healthcare
facility to another for higher-acuity or specialty care. Typical
triggers:

- Stroke patient needs thrombolysis-capable centre.
- Trauma patient needs surgical haemostasis.
- Obstetric emergency (eclampsia, obstructed labour, PPH).
- Paediatric emergency requiring tertiary paediatric input.
- Neonatal emergency.
- Acute coronary syndrome needing primary PCI.
- Severe sepsis or septic shock needing ICU.
- Acute respiratory failure needing ventilatory support.

WHO IMAI uses the term *upward referral* for these moves. The matching
*downward referral* (after definitive care, returning the patient to
their primary facility) uses the Counter-Referral Form.

## Pre-transfer preparation

The initiating clinician must:

1. **Stabilize** — airway secure, breathing supported, circulation
   restored, immediate threats addressed. ABCDE complete.
2. **Communicate** — telephone or radio contact with the receiving
   facility focal point; agreement to accept the patient. Tick the
   "Referral facility contacted" checkbox only after this conversation.
3. **Arrange transport** — appropriate mode (ground / air / sea);
   appropriate escort skill level; appropriate equipment.
4. **Document** — complete the form's Situation, Background,
   Assessment, Recommendations sections.
5. **Pack** — patient notes, medications administered, blood products,
   imaging on disk or shared link, ID documents, valuables list, any
   specimen samples in transit.

## Escort selection

Skill level of the escort should match the patient acuity:

| Patient acuity | Minimum escort skill |
| --- | --- |
| Stable, low risk | Paramedic or experienced nurse |
| Moderately unstable | Critical-care paramedic or anaesthesia-trained nurse |
| Unstable / ventilated / multiple infusions | Doctor (anaesthesia or critical care) plus nurse |
| Paediatric / neonatal | Paediatrician or paediatric trained nurse |
| Obstetric emergency | Obstetrician or trained midwife |

Reference: Intensive Care Society / Faculty of Intensive Care Medicine.
*Guidelines for the transport of the critically ill adult*, 4th edition,
2019. <https://www.ficm.ac.uk/standards/guidelines-transport-critically-ill-adult>

## Mode of transfer

- **Ground (ambulance)** — most common. Suitable for journeys <2 hours
  and stable or moderately unstable patients.
- **Helicopter** — for time-critical transfers, road impassable, long
  ground time. Consider altitude effects: at 1500 m FiO2 must rise to
  maintain SpO2. ETT cuff pressure rises with altitude — set to 20-25
  cmH2O at sea level and re-check at altitude.
- **Fixed-wing** — for long-distance transfers; usually pressurized cabin
  (8000 ft equivalent).
- **Sea** — coastal and island contexts; motion sickness; saline-spray
  exposure on equipment.

## Equipment checklist

The Recommendations section should reference the standard equipment
inventory verified before departure:

- Airway: BVM, OPA / NPA range, suction (battery and manual back-up),
  ETT if intubated.
- Breathing: oxygen cylinders with ≥30 minutes reserve beyond expected
  journey; ventilator if intubated; SpO2 monitor.
- Circulation: at least 2 patent IV / IO access points; blood pressure
  monitor (preferably non-invasive automated); ECG monitor; infusion
  pumps with battery backup.
- Disability: blood glucose meter; pupil torch; thermometer.
- Drugs: standard resuscitation drug box; specific drugs for the
  patient's condition.
- Documentation: paper copy of the referral form; copy retained at
  initiating facility.

## During-transport reassessment

Vital signs should be repeated and recorded:

- Every 5-10 minutes if unstable.
- Every 15 minutes if stable.
- Immediately before any intervention and 5 minutes after.

Any deterioration triggers a divert decision — return to initiating
facility or proceed to nearest emergency facility.

## Handover at receiving facility

The receiving provider:

1. Receives a verbal SBAR handover from the escort.
2. Reviews the completed form.
3. Confirms arrival time on the form.
4. Signs the receipt section.
5. Returns a copy of the form (or a summary) to the initiating facility
   within 24 hours where possible.

## Quality indicators

WHO IMAI-aligned quality indicators for referral systems:

- Proportion of upward referrals with completed Acute Referral Form.
- Proportion of referrals where receiving facility was contacted before
  departure.
- Mean transfer time door-to-door.
- Proportion of patients arriving with deteriorated vital signs vs
  pre-transfer.
- Proportion of inappropriate referrals (rejected, sent back, or
  diagnosed with non-referrable condition).

Reference: WHO. *Strengthening referral systems in community health
care: a tool for managers*. Geneva: WHO; 2020.
<https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240003972>

## Common deterioration pathways and pre-prepared responses

| Deterioration | Pre-prepared response |
| --- | --- |
| Airway loss | Airway adjunct, BVM, intubation if trained |
| Hypoxia | Increase O2, check ETT/circuit, reassess |
| Hypotension | IV fluid bolus, vasopressor if available |
| Arrhythmia | ALS algorithm; defibrillation if VF/pulseless VT |
| Seizure | Midazolam IM/buccal; check glucose |
| Hypoglycaemia | IV dextrose 10% 100-150 mL |
| Cardiac arrest | Stop, start CPR; consider divert |
