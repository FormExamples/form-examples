# Clinical guideline alignment — acute stroke

## NICE NG128 — Stroke and transient ischaemic attack in over 16s

Source: National Institute for Health and Care Excellence.
Guideline ID: **NG128** (published May 2019; updated April 2022).
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng128

NG128 is the principal NICE guideline for acute stroke and TIA.
Alignments encoded by the form:

- **§1.1.1** — recognise stroke with FAST (face, arm, speech, time) in
  pre-hospital settings.
- **§1.1.4** — use ROSIER in the emergency department.
- **§1.4.1** — brain imaging immediately for patients eligible for
  thrombolysis or thrombectomy, on anticoagulants, with depressed
  consciousness or progressive symptoms.
- **§1.4.7–§1.4.10** — intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase within
  4.5 hours of onset for adults whose disabling symptoms are not
  contraindicated.
- **§1.4.17–§1.4.22** — mechanical thrombectomy for patients with
  proven large-vessel occlusion within the time window (up to 6 hours
  routinely; up to 24 hours with advanced imaging).
- **§1.5–§1.7** — admission to acute stroke unit; antiplatelet,
  antithrombotic, statin therapy; carotid imaging; secondary prevention.

## Royal College of Physicians 2023 National Clinical Guideline for Stroke

Source: Royal College of Physicians, Intercollegiate Stroke Working
Party (2023). *National Clinical Guideline for Stroke for the UK and
Ireland*.
URL: https://www.strokeguideline.org

The 2023 RCP guideline is the principal UK clinical reference. It
contains detailed recommendations on:

- Hyperacute assessment and reperfusion.
- Imaging selection (CT, CTA, CT perfusion, MRI).
- Tenecteplase as an alternative thrombolytic.
- Extended-window thrombolysis with MRI mismatch selection.
- Thrombectomy eligibility, including posterior-circulation extension.
- Secondary prevention and rehabilitation.

The 2023 guideline supersedes the RCP 5th edition (2016) and is
endorsed by NHS England, NHS Scotland, NHS Wales, and the Department
of Health (Northern Ireland).

## NICE TA264 — Alteplase for treating acute ischaemic stroke

Source: NICE Technology Appraisal TA264 (2012).
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ta264

TA264 covers alteplase use for acute ischaemic stroke within 4.5 hours.
Replaced in some respects by NG128.

## SSNAP — Sentinel Stroke National Audit Programme

Source: Royal College of Physicians SSNAP.
URL: https://www.strokeaudit.org

SSNAP is the national audit for stroke care in England, Wales and
Northern Ireland. The form's data structure supports SSNAP key
performance indicators including door-to-needle time, door-to-groin time,
and admission to a stroke unit within 4 hours.

## Contraindication checklist for thrombolysis

The form's Risk Factors and Current Medications steps prompt
documentation of:

- Time from LKW > 4.5 hours (relative; extended windows possible).
- Active internal bleeding.
- Recent major surgery (within 14 days).
- Recent stroke or head injury (within 3 months).
- Intracranial haemorrhage history.
- Aortic dissection.
- Active malignancy with bleeding risk.
- INR > 1.7 OR direct oral anticoagulant taken within 48 hours.
- Platelet count < 100 × 10⁹/L.
- Blood pressure > 185/110 mmHg unable to be lowered.
- Blood glucose < 2.7 or > 22 mmol/L.
- Pregnancy (relative).
