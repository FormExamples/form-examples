# Safety case notes — acute stroke assessment

Stroke is a time-critical emergency. Each minute of delay in reperfusion
costs approximately 1.9 million neurons (Saver, 2006). This form is
designed to make the hyperacute pathway data explicit.

## Time-critical flags

| Flag                                              | Trigger                                              | Action |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| Possible large-vessel occlusion                   | NIHSS ≥ 6 with cortical signs (gaze deviation, neglect, aphasia) | Immediate CTA; thrombectomy team |
| Thrombolysis window                               | LKW < 4.5 hours; no contraindication                 | Bloods, CT, alteplase / tenecteplase prep |
| Wake-up stroke                                    | LKW = previous evening; symptoms on waking          | MRI DWI/FLAIR mismatch for extended thrombolysis |
| Pre-existing anticoagulation                      | DOAC within 48 hours OR warfarin INR > 1.7           | Thrombolysis usually contraindicated; consider andexanet/idarucizumab + thrombectomy |
| Hypertensive crisis                               | BP > 185/110 mmHg                                    | IV labetalol / nicardipine to reduce; recheck before thrombolysis |
| Hypoglycaemia                                     | BG < 2.7 mmol/L                                      | Treat hypoglycaemia; reassess for stroke mimic |
| Hyperglycaemia                                    | BG > 22 mmol/L                                       | Treat hyperglycaemia; reassess |
| Pregnancy                                         | Confirmed pregnancy                                  | Multi-disciplinary discussion of risk/benefit |
| Massive stroke / depressed GCS                    | GCS ≤ 8 OR NIHSS > 25                                | Airway protection; neurosurgical consult; ICU |
| Possible haemorrhagic transformation              | Sudden deterioration post-thrombolysis               | Stop thrombolysis; urgent CT; tranexamic acid |
| Stroke mimic                                      | Migraine, seizure with Todd paresis, hypoglycaemia, functional | Treat underlying cause; do not give thrombolysis if confident |

## Differential diagnosis (stroke mimic exclusion)

The clinician must consider:

- **Hypoglycaemia** — point-of-care glucose immediately.
- **Seizure with Todd paresis** — postictal weakness usually resolves over
  minutes to hours.
- **Migraine with aura** — particularly young patients; positive symptoms
  marching over minutes.
- **Functional neurological symptoms** — give-way weakness, inconsistent
  signs, Hoover's sign.
- **Acute encephalopathy / sepsis** — fluctuating consciousness, fever,
  raised inflammatory markers.
- **Drug intoxication** — opioid, benzodiazepine, alcohol.

## Stroke chameleons (under-recognized stroke)

- **Acute confusion / encephalopathy** — particularly in older patients;
  large MCA stroke can present as delirium.
- **Vertigo** — posterior-circulation stroke; HINTS exam aids
  differentiation from peripheral vertigo.
- **Cortical blindness** — bilateral occipital stroke.
- **Acute aphasia mistaken for psychiatric presentation**.
- **Locked-in syndrome** — pontine stroke.

## Posterior-circulation stroke

Posterior-circulation strokes (basilar artery, vertebral artery, posterior
cerebral artery) may have a low NIHSS despite severe disability. Red
flags include vertigo + cranial nerve signs, dysarthria with limb
incoordination, bilateral signs, and vertical gaze palsy. The HINTS
examination (Head Impulse, Nystagmus, Test of Skew) supports
differentiation.

## Subarachnoid haemorrhage red flags

- **Thunderclap headache** — sudden, maximum severity within minutes.
- **Meningism**.
- **Focal neurological signs**.
- **Reduced consciousness**.

Immediate CT; if CT negative and time < 6 hours from onset, CT is
~100% sensitive; > 6 hours, perform lumbar puncture for xanthochromia
unless CTA confirms aneurysm.

## Mental Capacity Act

In acute stroke, patients may lack capacity for treatment decisions
because of aphasia, depressed consciousness, or neglect. The form
prompts:

- Capacity assessment for the specific decision (thrombolysis,
  thrombectomy, advance care planning).
- Best-interests decision-making where capacity is lacking, involving
  next of kin where possible.
- Documentation of LPA for Health and Care Decisions if in place.
- Use of telephone Section 12 doctor where Mental Health Act issues
  coexist.

## Safeguarding considerations

- **Falls / assault as cause** — record mechanism; consider safeguarding
  if intentional injury is possible.
- **Vulnerable adult discharge planning** — Care Act 2014 consideration.

## Post-thrombolysis monitoring

After thrombolysis, the form prompts:

- Neurological observations every 15 minutes for 2 hours, then every 30
  minutes for 6 hours, then hourly.
- Blood-pressure target < 180/105 mmHg.
- 24-hour CT to assess for haemorrhagic transformation.
- Avoidance of antiplatelet / anticoagulant therapy for 24 hours
  (unless atrial fibrillation cardioembolic stroke and 24-hour CT
  shows no haemorrhage).

## Decision support

The form does not replace local stroke pathway protocols. Where local
protocol differs from the recommendations encoded here, the local
protocol takes precedence and should be cited in the assessment record.
