# Safety case notes — hospital discharge

The discharge summary is a *transfer of care* artefact and is the
most common point at which medication and follow-up errors occur. The
notes below capture the principal safety pathways.

## Medication safety at the interface of care

- **Royal Pharmaceutical Society.** *Keeping patients safe when they
  transfer between care providers — getting the medicines right.*
  Part 1 (commissioning) and Part 2 (practice), 2012.
  <https://www.rpharms.com/resources/reports/getting-the-medicines-right>
- **WHO.** *Medication Without Harm — WHO Global Patient Safety
  Challenge.* 2017. Targets a 50% reduction in severe avoidable
  medication-related harm; transitions of care are one of three named
  priority areas. <https://www.who.int/initiatives/medication-without-harm>
- **NICE NG5.** *Medicines optimisation.* §1.5 covers medicines
  reconciliation at transfer of care.
  <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng5>

The *Discharge Medications (reconciled)* step in this form implements
the NG5 reconciliation requirement: the clinician must confirm each
medication is intended, indication is documented, and any change from
admission is noted.

## Incident reporting on failed discharge

Failed or unsafe discharges that reach the patient are reportable
patient-safety events under the **Learn from patient safety events
(LFPSE) service**, which replaced the NRLS in 2023.

- NHS England. *Learn from patient safety events service.*
  <https://www.england.nhs.uk/patient-safety/learn-from-patient-safety-events-service/>
- WHO. *Conceptual framework for the international classification for
  patient safety.* Final technical report, 2009.
  <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/conceptual-framework-for-the-international-classification-for-patient-safety>

## Safeguarding on discharge

- **Care Act 2014** — duty of safeguarding enquiry where an adult with
  care and support needs is at risk of abuse or neglect.
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2014/23/contents>
- **NHS England safeguarding adults and children index.**
  <https://www.england.nhs.uk/safeguarding/>
- **Mental Capacity Act 2005** — capacity to consent to discharge plan
  must be assessed where doubt exists.
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/contents>

The *Patient / Carer Acknowledgement* step records the discharge
recipient (patient or, where capacity is impaired, a carer or
authorized representative).

## Equality Act 2010 / Accessible Information Standard

- *Accessible Information Standard (SCCI1605 / DCB1605)* — patients
  with communication needs must be offered the discharge summary in an
  accessible format.
  <https://www.england.nhs.uk/about/equality/equality-hub/patient-equalities-programme/equality-frameworks-and-information-standards/accessibleinfo/>

## Data protection

- Lawful basis under UK GDPR: Article 6(1)(e) public task and Article
  9(2)(h) provision of health care.
- *NHS Records Management Code of Practice.*
  <https://www.nhsx.nhs.uk/information-governance/guidance/records-management-code/>

## Out of scope

- Continuing Healthcare (CHC) eligibility — recorded as a pathway
  flag only; CHC assessment uses the *National Framework for NHS
  Continuing Healthcare* and is performed separately.
  <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-framework-for-nhs-continuing-healthcare-and-nhs-funded-nursing-care>
