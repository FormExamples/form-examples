# Hip replacement surgery evaluation

A hip replacement surgery evaluation is an in-depth clinical and lifestyle review by an [orthopedic surgeon](https://www.topdoctors.co.uk/doctor/gareth-llewelyn-roberts) or multidisciplinary team to determine if total joint arthroplasty is the most effective intervention for chronic hip pain and functional decline. It builds upon initial clinical screenings by quantifying how significantly joint degeneration impacts your daily independence and overall quality of life. [1, 2, 3]

## Core Evaluative Components

- Patient-Reported Outcome Measures (PROMs): Standardized questionnaires like the [Oxford Hip Score (OHS)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666776222001715) or HOOS are administered to score your pain levels, functional restrictions, and difficulty with everyday movements like putting on socks or walking. [3, 4]
- Gait and Biomechanical Analysis: Direct observation of your walking pattern in the clinic to identify compensatory limping, load-bearing limitations, or structural leg-length discrepancies. [5, 6]
- Advanced Radiographic Review: Detailed analysis of weight-bearing pelvic and hip X-rays to grade the precise degree of joint space narrowing, subchondral sclerosis, or bone-on-bone deterioration. [6, 7]
- Conservative Treatment Audit: A formal review of non-surgical methods already attempted—such as physiotherapy regimens, weight management, targeted steroid injections, and analgesics—to confirm they no longer provide adequate relief. [3, 8]
- Risk-Benefit and Frailty Stratification: Assessment of chronological health versus biological fitness, ensuring that comorbidities are optimized and that expectations align with realistic post-surgical recovery milestones. [1, 3]
