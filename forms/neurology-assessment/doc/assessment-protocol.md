# Neurology assessment protocol

This document describes the recommended sequence and content of the
neurological assessment in an acute or outpatient setting.

## Time-critical elements first

For any patient presenting with possible acute stroke:

1. **Last known well** time — record with timezone. Determines
   thrombolysis / thrombectomy eligibility.
2. **FAST** — face, arm, speech, time. Pre-hospital screen.
3. **ROSIER** — Recognition Of Stroke In the Emergency Room. Validated
   for ED triage.
4. **NIHSS** — once stroke is suspected, complete a structured NIHSS to
   quantify severity.
5. **Blood glucose** — exclude hypoglycaemia.
6. **CT brain** — immediate if thrombolysis/thrombectomy candidate; within
   24 hours otherwise.

## History taking — symptom-led structure

The Chief Complaint step prompts the clinician to record the presenting
symptom against the following structured framework:

| Feature                | Note                                              |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| Onset                  | Sudden / gradual / progressive / stuttering       |
| Duration               | Resolved / ongoing / fluctuating                  |
| Distribution           | Focal / multifocal / generalised / bilateral      |
| Quality                | Pain (severity, character), weakness, numbness, visual, speech, balance |
| Severity               | Functional impact                                 |
| Aggravators / relievers | Posture, exertion, time of day, medications     |
| Associated symptoms    | Nausea, photophobia, fever, weight loss, malaise  |

## NIHSS administration sequence

Administer items in the published order — see scoring-rules.md.
The NINDS training and certification programme should be completed by
all clinicians performing NIHSS in acute settings.

## Cranial nerve and motor/sensory examination

Captured in the Motor & Sensory Exam step. The form prompts the clinician
to record findings for:

- Cranial nerves I–XII.
- Motor — tone, power (MRC grade 0–5), reflexes (graded 0–4+), plantar
  responses.
- Sensory — light touch, pinprick, vibration, joint position sense,
  two-point discrimination.
- Cerebellar — coordination, gait, Romberg.

## Headache assessment

Captured in the Headache Assessment step. The clinician records:

- Frequency and duration.
- Severity (0–10) and time-to-peak.
- Character (throbbing, tight, sharp, electric).
- Site and laterality.
- Triggers (food, sleep deprivation, hormonal, stress, alcohol,
  medications).
- Associated features (aura, photophobia, phonophobia, nausea, autonomic
  features).
- Red flags — see clinical-guideline-alignment.md (SNOOP4).
- Current and previous treatment, including over-the-counter analgesia
  frequency (for medication-overuse headache identification).

## Seizure history

Captured in the Seizure History step. The clinician records:

- Witnessed account.
- Pre-ictal aura.
- Ictal phenomena (focal aware, focal impaired awareness, tonic-clonic,
  absence, myoclonic, atonic).
- Duration.
- Post-ictal phase (Todd paresis, confusion, headache, sleep).
- Tongue biting, incontinence, injury.
- Triggers (sleep deprivation, alcohol, photic, fever, illness).

## Cognitive screen

Captured in the Cognitive Assessment step. A brief screen (AMT-4, 6CIT,
or MoCA depending on setting) is appropriate. Where stroke is the
context, the **Oxfordshire Community Stroke Project (OCSP)
classification** or the **Bamford classification** correlates with
prognosis.

## Diagnostic results

Captured in the Diagnostic Results step. The form prompts entry of:

- Brain imaging (CT, CTA, MRI, MRA, perfusion).
- Vascular imaging (carotid duplex, transcranial Doppler).
- Cardiac (ECG, 24-hour Holter, echocardiogram, prolonged loop monitor).
- Bloods (FBC, U&E, glucose, lipid panel, troponin, ESR/CRP, coag).
- Lumbar puncture results (CSF opening pressure, cell count, protein,
  glucose, xanthochromia if SAH suspected).
- EEG findings if available.

## Differential diagnosis prompts

The clinician documents reasoning around:

- Stroke vs stroke mimic (seizure with Todd paresis, migraine with aura,
  functional symptoms, hypoglycaemia, encephalopathy).
- Headache subtype (see above).
- Demyelinating disease (MS, NMOSD).
- Movement disorder (Parkinson, essential tremor, dystonia).
- Peripheral nerve and neuromuscular conditions.
- Functional neurological disorder.
