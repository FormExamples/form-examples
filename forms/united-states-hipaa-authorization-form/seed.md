# United States (US) Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) authorization form

A United States HIPAA authorization form is a legal document that gives your healthcare provider or health plan explicit permission to share your Protected Health Information (PHI) with third parties outside of routine treatment, payment, or operations.Key RequirementsA valid HIPAA authorization form must contain specific, detailed information:Patient Details: Your full name and date of birth.Recipient Details: The name or specific organization designated to receive your records.Information to Disclose: A precise description of the medical information to be released (e.g., "lab results from March 2026"). Vague phrases like "all my records" are usually rejected.Purpose: The exact reason for the disclosure (e.g., legal proceedings, insurance application, or personal use).Expiration Date: A defined end date or event (e.g., "12 months from signature" or "upon conclusion of my claim").Your RightsUnder the HIPAA Privacy Rule, you have the right to:Revoke your authorization: You can cancel the release of information at any time, usually by submitting a written request to your provider.Decline to sign: Healthcare providers cannot refuse treatment, payment, or enrollment if you do not sign the authorization.Receive a copy: You are entitled to a copy of the signed form once it is completed.Where to Find FormsBecause forms can vary slightly depending on your state and the exact entity holding your records:Your Healthcare Provider: Always check directly with your doctor's office or hospital's medical records department, as they generally have their own pre-approved HIPAA forms.State Department of Health: State government websites offer standardized templates. For example, you can review state-specific models like the Pennsylvania Department of Human Services HIPAA Authorization Form or the Tennessee Department of Human Services Release of Medical/Health Information.General Templates: If you need a draft for personal or legal planning, standard samples are available for review through resources like the Scribd HIPAA Journal Sample Form.
