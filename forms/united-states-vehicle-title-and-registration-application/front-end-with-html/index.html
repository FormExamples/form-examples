Complete the eight sections below to prepare an application for a certificate of title and registration. The engine does not decide whether a title is issued. It checks that the application is complete and consistent with the federal rules (the VIN format and check digit in 49 CFR 565.15, the odometer disclosure in 49 CFR Part 580, and the National Motor Vehicle Title Information System) and the rules of the state you choose, and classifies it as ready to submit, incomplete, or title brand or lien issue, raising prioritized flags. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

State law varies. Nine states (California, New York, Texas, Florida, Illinois, Pennsylvania, Ohio, Washington, and Arizona) carry encoded rules; any other state uses a uniform default and a "verify the state rule" flag. This tool is documentation support, not legal advice. The issuing state agency decides.

Vehicle titling is governed by each state's certificate-of-title law, with federal overlays: the VIN standard (49 CFR 565.15), the odometer disclosure requirements (49 U.S.C. 32705 and 49 CFR Part 580), and the National Motor Vehicle Title Information System (49 U.S.C. chapter 305 and 28 CFR part 25). This tool is documentation support, not legal advice. Fees, deadlines, and tax rates change; the issuing state agency decides.
