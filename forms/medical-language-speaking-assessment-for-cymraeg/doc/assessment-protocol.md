# Assessment protocol — Welsh clinical speaking

## Two-role-play structure

The form uses two role-plays based on the OET *Speaking sub-test*
two-role-play structure, adapted for the Welsh-language clinical
setting.

- Cambridge Boxhill Language Assessment Trust. *OET Speaking sub-test
  format — Medicine.*
  <https://www.occupationalenglishtest.org/speaking-test/>

The two role-plays are designed to elicit:

1. **Sgwrs gyda Chlaf** (*Patient Conversation*) — opening
   consultation, history taking, rapport building.
2. **Esboniad Clinigol** (*Clinical Explanation*) — explaining a
   diagnosis, management plan, or procedure to a patient in
   plain-language Welsh.

## Examiner training

Examiners should be:

- Registered NHS Wales staff with a Welsh-language proficiency at or
  above CEFR C1, **and**
- Trained in role-play-based speaking assessment (an OET examiner
  qualification or equivalent is recommended).

The Welsh Language Skills Strategy and the NHS Wales *Welsh in the
Workplace* programme run examiner-training sessions.

- Welsh Government. *Welsh in the Workplace.*
  <https://www.gov.wales/welsh-in-the-workplace>

## Criterion-based scoring

The four assessment criteria are rated on a 6-point band scale
aligned to CEFR A1 → C2:

| Criterion | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Fluency / Llifenrwydd | Pace, hesitation, repair strategies |
| Grammar / Gramadeg | Mutation, verb forms, register |
| Pronunciation / Ynganiad | Phonology, intonation, comprehensibility |
| Clinical appropriateness / Priodoldeb clinigol | Use of clinical-Welsh terminology, plain-language explanation, cultural appropriateness |

The overall CEFR level is the *worst-band* of the four criteria —
this is consistent with OET's *lowest-of* aggregation logic.

## Standards and treatment effect

The *active offer* principle requires that a Welsh-speaking patient
should not have to explicitly ask for Welsh-language care. The
practical effect on assessment design is that:

- Even *limited* Welsh capacity (A1/A2) should be recorded — staff at
  this level can deliver greeting, signposting, and basic comfort
  language even if they hand over clinical content to a colleague.
- A *recommended deployment* (rather than pass/fail) framing is more
  useful than a binary registration decision.

This is why the form outputs a CEFR level rather than a pass/fail
result.
