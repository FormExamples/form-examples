# Safety case notes — PTSD assessment

PTSD assessment involves direct discussion of traumatic events. The
following safety considerations apply.

## Pre-assessment safety

- **Setting** — assess in a private, quiet room with no risk of
  interruption.
- **Time** — allow at least 60 minutes; an interrupted trauma history
  can re-traumatize.
- **Support person** — offer the patient the opportunity to bring a
  trusted person.
- **Grounding plan** — agree before starting how the patient will signal
  if they need a break and what grounding techniques they will use.
- **Post-assessment plan** — confirm the patient has safe transport home
  and at least one source of support after the appointment.

## Suicide and self-harm risk

PTSD substantially increases suicide risk. The form's Risk Assessment
step should:

- Ask directly about suicidal thoughts, plans, intent, and means.
- Review past attempts and self-harm history.
- Capture protective factors.
- Document modifiable risk factors (substance use, social isolation,
  recent loss).

A non-zero score on PCL-5 items related to negative mood and self-blame
(items 9, 10, 14) does not in itself indicate suicidality but should
trigger explicit enquiry.

## Dissociation risk during assessment

Patients with severe PTSD or CPTSD may dissociate (depersonalization,
derealization, flashbacks) when describing the trauma. The clinician
should:

- Pause and ground the patient (5-4-3-2-1 sensory grounding, orientation
  questions, ice-pack or strong sensation).
- Confirm the patient is fully oriented before continuing.
- Stop the assessment if dissociation persists.

## Trauma-event re-disclosure

Patients may disclose abuse or violence for the first time. The
clinician should:

- Validate the disclosure.
- Avoid pressing for graphic detail beyond what is needed for diagnosis.
- Document factually and proportionately.
- Discuss confidentiality limits (safeguarding duties, criminal-justice
  implications).
- Offer signposting to specialist services.

## Safeguarding

Active safeguarding considerations include:

- **Adult safeguarding** — ongoing intimate-partner violence, sexual
  exploitation, financial abuse, modern slavery / trafficking. Care Act
  2014 duty to investigate.
- **Child safeguarding** — children in the patient's care exposed to
  domestic abuse or living with a person whose untreated PTSD affects
  parenting. Working Together to Safeguard Children duty.
- **Disclosure of historical abuse** — discuss reporting options;
  patient's choice unless others remain at risk.

## Specific high-risk populations

- **Military veterans** — direct enquiry about service history; refer to
  Op COURAGE (NHS Veterans' Mental Health and Wellbeing Service) in
  England, equivalent services in Wales / Scotland / Northern Ireland.
- **Asylum seekers and refugees** — interpreter required; assess for
  pre-arrival torture exposure; consider Helen Bamber Foundation or
  Freedom from Torture referral.
- **Survivors of sexual violence** — refer to Sexual Assault Referral
  Centres (SARCs) where appropriate; respect timing and consent
  preferences.
- **Healthcare workers and first responders** — secondary or vicarious
  traumatization; occupational-health pathway.
- **Perinatal women** — co-existing perinatal depression / anxiety;
  consider Perinatal Mental Health Team referral.

## Acute Stress Disorder vs PTSD

If the trauma occurred < 1 month ago, the relevant diagnosis is
**Acute Stress Disorder** (DSM-5 308.3 / F43.0; ICD-11 6B41 maps
differently — see ICD-11 6E40 Acute Stress Reaction). NICE NG116
recommends active monitoring for the first month; trauma-focused CBT
within the first month for symptomatic patients; debriefing is **not**
recommended.

## Substance use comorbidity

Substance use, particularly alcohol, often masks and complicates PTSD.
The clinician should ask about substance use and consider integrated
treatment where both are present.

## Pharmacological cautions

- Benzodiazepines should generally be avoided in PTSD; they impair
  trauma-processing and risk dependence.
- SSRIs and venlafaxine are first-line where pharmacotherapy is needed.
- Prazosin is sometimes used adjunctively for trauma nightmares.

## Crisis pathway

When acute risk emerges during assessment:

1. Confirm safety in the room; stay with the patient.
2. Contact the local Crisis Resolution Home Treatment Team or
   Liaison Psychiatry.
3. Where there is immediate intent or means, consider 999 and Mental
   Health Act assessment.
4. Document the assessment, plan, and patient's understanding.
5. Provide written contact details for Samaritans, NHS 111 (option 2),
   local crisis team.
