# Complete Blood Count (CBC / FBC) interpretation

Reference framework for interpreting the full blood count in the
haematology assessment.

## Reference intervals (adult, typical UK values)

Reference intervals vary by laboratory and analyser. Typical UK adult
intervals (source: British Society for Haematology / Royal College of
Pathologists guidance):

| Parameter | Female | Male | Units |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Haemoglobin (Hb) | 120–160 | 130–170 | g/L |
| Red blood cells (RBC) | 3.8–4.8 | 4.5–5.5 | × 10¹²/L |
| Mean cell volume (MCV) | 80–100 | 80–100 | fL |
| Mean cell haemoglobin (MCH) | 27–32 | 27–32 | pg |
| White blood cells (WBC) | 4.0–11.0 | 4.0–11.0 | × 10⁹/L |
| Neutrophils | 2.0–7.5 | 2.0–7.5 | × 10⁹/L |
| Lymphocytes | 1.0–4.5 | 1.0–4.5 | × 10⁹/L |
| Platelets | 150–400 | 150–400 | × 10⁹/L |
| Reticulocytes | 25–100 | 25–100 | × 10⁹/L |

Pregnancy, ethnicity (e.g. benign ethnic neutropenia), age, altitude, and
smoking shift these intervals.

## WHO definition of anaemia

WHO *Haemoglobin concentrations for the diagnosis of anaemia and
assessment of severity* (2011) and the 2024 update define anaemia by Hb
threshold:

- Non-pregnant women: Hb < 120 g/L.
- Pregnant women (first / third trimester): Hb < 110 g/L; second
  trimester: Hb < 105 g/L.
- Men: Hb < 130 g/L.
- Children 6–59 months: Hb < 110 g/L.

Reference: <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240088542>

## Anaemia classification by MCV

- **Microcytic** (MCV < 80 fL): iron deficiency, thalassaemia, anaemia of
  chronic disease (late), sideroblastic.
- **Normocytic** (MCV 80–100): acute blood loss, anaemia of chronic
  disease (early), renal failure, marrow infiltration.
- **Macrocytic** (MCV > 100): megaloblastic (B12/folate), hypothyroidism,
  liver disease, alcohol, drugs (methotrexate, azathioprine), MDS,
  reticulocytosis.

## Cytopenia thresholds (BSH guidance)

- Anaemia (above).
- Leukopenia: WBC < 4.0 × 10⁹/L.
- Neutropenia: neutrophils < 1.5 × 10⁹/L (severe < 0.5).
- Lymphopenia: lymphocytes < 1.0 × 10⁹/L.
- Thrombocytopenia: platelets < 150 × 10⁹/L (severe < 50).
- Pancytopenia: simultaneous low Hb, WBC, and platelets.

## Cytosis thresholds

- Leukocytosis: WBC > 11 × 10⁹/L.
- Neutrophilia: neutrophils > 7.5 × 10⁹/L.
- Lymphocytosis: lymphocytes > 4.5 × 10⁹/L (consider CLL in older adults).
- Eosinophilia: eosinophils > 0.5 × 10⁹/L.
- Thrombocytosis: platelets > 400 × 10⁹/L.
- Erythrocytosis: Hb > 165 g/L (women) / > 175 g/L (men).

## Severity rule of thumb (this form)

- **Normal**: all indices within reference range.
- **Mild**: isolated abnormality, non-urgent follow-up.
- **Moderate**: multi-line abnormality or coagulation disturbance
  requiring specialist review.
- **Severe**: pancytopenia, active bleeding diathesis, haemolysis, blast
  cells or other features of acute leukaemia — urgent haematology
  referral.

## Coagulation reference intervals

- PT (INR): 0.9–1.2 (off anticoagulant).
- APTT ratio: 0.8–1.2.
- Fibrinogen: 1.5–4.0 g/L.
- D-dimer: < 500 ng/mL (lab-dependent; age-adjusted thresholds for
  PE/VTE per NICE NG158).
