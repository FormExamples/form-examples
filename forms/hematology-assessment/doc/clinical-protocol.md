# Clinical protocol

Operational protocol for the haematology assessment.

## Indications

- New haematology referral for anaemia, cytopenia, lymphadenopathy,
  splenomegaly, or paraproteinaemia.
- Suspected acute leukaemia (urgent same-day pathway).
- Follow-up of chronic haematological condition (MDS, CML, CLL, ITP,
  haemoglobinopathy, polycythaemia vera, essential thrombocythaemia).
- Pre-transfusion review.
- VTE / thrombophilia workup.

## Preconditions

- Recent (≤ 7 days) full blood count.
- Coagulation screen if abnormal bleeding history or anticoagulant use.
- Reticulocyte count, ferritin, B12, folate as indicated by FBC pattern.
- Peripheral blood film reviewed by laboratory haematologist.

## Step-by-step

1. **Patient Information.** Identifiers; symptom timeline; family history.
2. **Blood Count Analysis.** Hb, MCV, MCH, WBC differential, platelets,
   reticulocytes.
3. **Coagulation Studies.** PT/INR, APTT, fibrinogen, D-dimer (with
   pretest probability per NG158 if VTE-suspected).
4. **Peripheral Blood Film.** Morphology — anisopoikilocytosis, blast
   cells, sickling, hypersegmented neutrophils, Howell-Jolly bodies,
   schistocytes.
5. **Iron Studies.** Ferritin, transferrin, transferrin saturation,
   serum iron; vitamin B12, folate where macrocytic.
6. **Haemoglobinopathy Screening.** HPLC / electrophoresis (when
   indicated); HbA, HbA2, HbF, HbS, HbC.
7. **Bone Marrow Assessment.** Aspirate and trephine if indicated for
   unexplained cytopenia, paraproteinaemia, or suspected marrow
   infiltration.
8. **Transfusion History.** Past transfusions, reactions, alloantibodies,
   irradiated component requirements, refusal of blood products
   (Jehovah's Witness considerations per NICE NG24).
9. **Treatment & Medications.** Current anti-thrombotic / anticoagulant,
   chemotherapy, immunomodulator, immunosuppressant, iron therapy,
   erythropoietin.
10. **Clinical Review.** Composite severity, onward referral, monitoring
    plan.

## Composite severity rules

- **Normal**: all indices within reference range.
- **Mild**: isolated mild abnormality (e.g. Hb 110–120 g/L in a non-
  pregnant woman, isolated mild eosinophilia).
- **Moderate**: multi-line abnormality or coagulation disturbance
  requiring specialist review (e.g. bicytopenia, INR > 1.5 off
  anticoagulant, persistent monocytosis).
- **Severe**: pancytopenia, blast cells, active bleeding diathesis,
  haemolysis, suspected acute leukaemia — same-day haematology referral.

## Red flags requiring same-day haematology

- Blast cells on peripheral film.
- Hb < 70 g/L with symptoms or < 80 g/L with ACS.
- Platelets < 20 × 10⁹/L.
- Neutrophils < 0.5 × 10⁹/L with fever (neutropenic sepsis).
- Hyperviscosity symptoms (visual blur, headache, mucosal bleeding) with
  raised paraprotein.
- Confirmed haemolysis with raised LDH and reticulocytosis.

## Anticoagulation review

- DOAC dose-adjustment per NICE NG196 (AF) and SPC; check eGFR and
  weight.
- Warfarin INR target reviewed against indication.
- Bridging strategy for procedures per BSH 2020.
