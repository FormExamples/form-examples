Complete the seven sections below to document a private vehicle sale. The engine does not give legal advice. It checks that the bill of sale is complete and free of risk: both parties and the vehicle are identified, the federal odometer disclosure (49 CFR Part 580) is made unless the vehicle is exempt, the VIN is well-formed, no lien is outstanding, the seller holds the title, and any notarization the state requires is done. It classifies the sale as complete, incomplete, or risk flagged, and raises flags for the filings that remain. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

State law varies. The engine applies a small state-rule table and falls back to a uniform default, with a “verify state rule” flag, for any other state. This is documentation support, not legal advice; the issuing state agency decides what it accepts.

Federal law (49 CFR Part 580, under the Truth in Mileage Act) requires the seller to disclose the odometer reading in writing on transfer, unless the vehicle is exempt, and both parties to keep the statement for five years. Notarization, release of liability, and title-transfer deadlines are set by each state. This tool documents and checks the bill of sale; it is not legal advice, and the state motor vehicle agency, not this tool, decides what it accepts.
