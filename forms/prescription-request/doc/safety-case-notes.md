# Safety case notes — prescription request

## Medication safety framework

- WHO. *Medication Without Harm — Global Patient Safety Challenge.*
  2017. <https://www.who.int/initiatives/medication-without-harm>
- NICE NG5. *Medicines optimisation.*
  <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng5>
- Royal Pharmaceutical Society. *Professional standards for hospital
  pharmacy services.*
  <https://www.rpharms.com/standards>

The three WHO priority areas (high-risk situations, polypharmacy,
transitions of care) are all relevant; transition-of-care
prescribing is the highest-risk context.

## Allergy and contraindication checks

The form does not perform an automated allergy check; the
prescribing system is responsible. Reference:

- NICE CG183. *Drug allergy: diagnosis and management.*
  <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg183>
- BNF *Drug allergy and cross-sensitivity.*
  <https://bnf.nice.org.uk/>

The receiving pharmacy is expected to perform a clinical check
against the patient's medication history before dispensing — per
GPhC standards.

## Controlled drugs

- *Misuse of Drugs Regulations 2001.*
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/3998/contents>
- NICE NG46. *Controlled drugs: safe use and management.*
  <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng46>

Operational safety expectations:

- Schedule 2 and 3 drugs require a private CD prescription form
  (FP10PCD or hospital equivalent) and adherence to the
  *Handwriting Requirements* (which the BNF lists in full).
- Quantity must be in words and figures.
- 28-day maximum supply (with documented exceptions).

## Error reporting

Medication errors detected at prescription, transcription, dispensing,
or administration are reportable via:

- NHS England *Learn from patient safety events (LFPSE) service*.
  <https://www.england.nhs.uk/patient-safety/learn-from-patient-safety-events-service/>
- MHRA *Yellow Card scheme* for adverse drug reactions.
  <https://yellowcard.mhra.gov.uk/>

## Verification of prescriber

In the UK, the prescriber's registration must be verified at the
receiving pharmacy. Independent prescribers (nurses, pharmacists,
optometrists, physiotherapists, podiatrists, paramedics) have
separate registration registers; the form's *Clinician Information*
step captures registration body and number.

- General Medical Council registration check.
  <https://www.gmc-uk.org/registration-and-licensing/the-medical-register>
- Nursing and Midwifery Council register search.
  <https://www.nmc.org.uk/registration/search-the-register/>
- General Pharmaceutical Council register search.
  <https://www.pharmacyregulation.org/registers>

## Data protection

- Lawful basis (UK GDPR): Article 6(1)(e) public task; Article
  9(2)(h) provision of health care.
- Prescription records form part of the patient's health record and
  are retained per the *NHS Records Management Code of Practice*.

## Out of scope

- Automated dose calculation — not implemented; prescriber retains
  responsibility.
- Drug-drug interaction checking — receiving pharmacy system
  responsibility.
- Cost / formulary check — practice formulary responsibility.
