# Consent and preferences

The *Consent & Preferences* step (step 10) captures the lawful basis
for processing and the patient's communication preferences. The
authoritative frameworks are:

## Lawful basis

- **UK GDPR Article 6** — lawful basis for processing personal data.
  <https://gdpr-info.eu/art-6-gdpr/>
- **UK GDPR Article 9** — lawful basis for special category data
  (health). <https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/>
- For NHS providers: Article 6(1)(e) public task + Article 9(2)(h)
  provision of health or social care is the typical pair.
- For private US providers: HIPAA Privacy Rule §164.506 *Permitted
  uses* covers treatment, payment, and healthcare operations.

## Consent vs lawful basis

The intake form should distinguish *lawful basis* (which is rarely
consent for direct care) from *positive choices* the patient makes:

- Consent to share with named third parties (e.g. spouse, carer).
- Consent to receive reminders by SMS or email.
- Consent to be contacted about research.
- Consent to be entered in the *National Data Opt-out* (UK).

UK National Data Opt-out:
<https://digital.nhs.uk/services/national-data-opt-out>

## Accessible Information Standard (UK)

- NHS England. *Accessible Information Standard (DCB1605).*
  <https://www.england.nhs.uk/about/equality/equality-hub/patient-equalities-programme/equality-frameworks-and-information-standards/accessibleinfo/>

The standard requires capture of:

- Preferred language and interpreter need.
- Communication needs (BSL, lip-reading, easy read, large print,
  braille, audio).
- Information format preference (paper, email, secure portal).

## Mental capacity

Where the patient lacks capacity to consent to the intake (UK Mental
Capacity Act 2005 / equivalent), a *best interests* decision is taken
and the *Consent* fields record the proxy decision-maker.

- *Mental Capacity Act 2005.*
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/contents>
- Social Care Institute for Excellence. *Mental Capacity Act
  resources.* <https://www.scie.org.uk/mca/>

## Children and young people

- UK: Children aged 16+ may consent under the *Family Law Reform Act
  1969 s8*; younger children may consent if *Gillick competent*.
- US: state-specific minor-consent laws apply; see state-level
  professional society guidance.

## Equality

- *Equality Act 2010 (UK).*
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/contents>
- HHS Office for Civil Rights. *Section 1557 (US Affordable Care Act
  non-discrimination).*
  <https://www.hhs.gov/civil-rights/for-individuals/section-1557/index.html>
