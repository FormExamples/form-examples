Recognition Of Stroke In the Emergency Room (ROSIER)

Bedside stroke-recognition screen: two mimic exclusions and five acute-onset neurological signs, scored -2 to +5.

Complete the ROSIER assessment below for an adult (16 years or over) presenting with acute suspected stroke or transient ischaemic attack. Measure blood glucose first and correct any hypoglycaemia before interpreting the score. Two mimic criteria subtract a point each and five acute-onset neurological signs add a point each, for a signed total of -2 to +5. A total greater than 0 is a positive screen (stroke likely) that activates the acute stroke pathway. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

ROSIER is a screening prompt, not a diagnosis of stroke. A total of 0 or below does not exclude stroke: if clinical suspicion remains, escalate regardless of the score, and remember that imaging is required for a definitive diagnosis.

The ROSIER (Recognition Of Stroke In the Emergency Room) scale was derived and validated by Nor et al. (Lancet Neurology 2005) and is referenced in NICE NG128 and the Royal College of Physicians National Clinical Guideline for Stroke (2023). This screening tool is for information only and is not a diagnosis; activate the stroke pathway, escalate, and treat in line with local policy and clinical judgement.
