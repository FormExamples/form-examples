# Safety case notes — patient satisfaction survey

## Free-text disclosure pathway

The free-text *Comments & Suggestions* field may surface disclosures
that require escalation outside the experience-improvement pathway:

- **Safeguarding** — NHS England *Safeguarding adults and children*
  policy index.
  <https://www.england.nhs.uk/safeguarding/>
- **Active suicidal ideation / self-harm** — NICE NG225.
  <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng225>
- **Patient safety incidents** — NHS England *Learn from patient
  safety events (LFPSE) service*.
  <https://www.england.nhs.uk/patient-safety/learn-from-patient-safety-events-service/>
- **Formal complaints** — *Local Authority Social Services and NHS
  Complaints (England) Regulations 2009*.
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/309/contents/made>

## Risk pathway — bottom-band composite

A composite ≤ 24 (Very Poor) signals an experience that is
significantly worse than ordinary variation. Operational expectation:

- Practice manager review within 1 working day.
- Free-text passage redacted of third-party identifiers and routed
  per the disclosure pathway above.
- Trend monitoring at sub-cohort level (specialty, time of day, day
  of week) to identify systemic issues.

## Selection bias

Voluntary post-encounter surveys over-represent both extremes
(highly satisfied and highly dissatisfied). Mitigations:

- Compute response rate (responses ÷ eligible encounters).
- Apply minimum sample threshold before publication (HCAHPS uses
  n ≥ 100 per quarter).
- Recognize that this form is *internal improvement* output — not a
  research-grade survey.

## Equality Act 2010

Demographic capture in Step 1 is *protected characteristic* data
under the Equality Act 2010 and *special category* under UK GDPR.

- *Equality Act 2010.*
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/contents>

Operational expectation: demographic capture is optional and
respondents may select *prefer not to say* on every demographic
field.

## Data protection

- Lawful basis (UK GDPR): Article 6(1)(e) public task; Article
  9(2)(h) provision of health care (where the survey is linked to an
  identifiable encounter).
- If the survey is administered anonymously and not linked to an
  identifiable patient, Article 6(1)(e) alone is sufficient and the
  data is not personal data after submission.
- *NHS Records Management Code of Practice.*
  <https://www.nhsx.nhs.uk/information-governance/guidance/records-management-code/>

## Limitations on use

The survey output is **not**:

- A clinical outcome measure (use a PROM).
- A regulatory submission (use FFT / CQC / HCAHPS as required).
- A staff performance metric without case-mix adjustment.
