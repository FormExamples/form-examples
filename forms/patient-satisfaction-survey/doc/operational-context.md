# Operational context — running a patient satisfaction survey

## Mode and timing

Patient-experience surveys exhibit known mode and timing effects.
Reference guidance:

- AHRQ. *CAHPS — Survey administration guidance.*
  <https://www.ahrq.gov/cahps/surveys-guidance/helpful-resources/administration/index.html>
- CMS. *HCAHPS Quality Assurance Guidelines* (current version).
  <https://hcahpsonline.org/>

Typical operational windows:

- HCAHPS: 48 hours to 6 weeks post-discharge.
- CG-CAHPS: within 4 weeks of visit.
- This form is mode-agnostic; the *Visit Details* step captures
  visit date so the analyst can compute response latency.

## Response rate and case-mix adjustment

- AHRQ. *CAHPS Analysis Program.*
  <https://www.ahrq.gov/cahps/surveys-guidance/helpful-resources/analysis/index.html>

Case-mix adjustment is recommended when comparing services with
materially different patient populations. Adjuster variables
typically include age, education, self-rated general health, and
language of survey.

## Free-text triage

The *Comments & Suggestions* free text may contain:

- Safeguarding disclosures — route per local safeguarding policy.
- Complaints — route per *Complaints (England) Regulations 2009*.
- Patient-safety incidents — route to LFPSE.
- Compliments and improvement ideas — route to local quality team.

Operational policy: the survey response with composite ≤ 24 and a
non-empty free-text comment must be reviewed by the practice
manager within one working day.

## Comparability cautions

This form's bands and composite score are **not** comparable to
public HCAHPS / CG-CAHPS / CQC survey publications, which:

- Use different scales (frequency rather than satisfaction).
- Report top-box percentages, not means.
- Apply case-mix adjustment.
- Use independent administration (mail / telephone) rather than
  point-of-encounter capture.

Local trend monitoring and *internal* improvement comparisons are
the appropriate uses. Quoting this form's outputs alongside published
HCAHPS or CQC scores is misleading without explicit caveats.

## Equity and Equality Act considerations

- *Equality Act 2010* — protected characteristics must not be used
  to weight or filter responses in a way that produces unlawful
  discrimination.
- Sub-group analysis is permitted and encouraged where there is a
  health-inequality improvement purpose; see *Core20PLUS5*.
  <https://www.england.nhs.uk/about/equality/equality-hub/national-healthcare-inequalities-improvement-programme/core20plus5/>

## Data retention

- Lawful basis: UK GDPR Article 6(1)(e) / Article 9(2)(h).
- Retention: align with the *NHS Records Management Code of
  Practice* — patient-experience data is typically retained for the
  shorter of the active improvement cycle or the underlying record
  retention period.
- Pseudonymisation: identifiable demographic data should be
  separated from response data at the earliest opportunity that
  preserves the analytic linkage.
