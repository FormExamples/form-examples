# Cardiac Stress Test Request — clinical references

Reference notes grounding the four-axis grading engine. These inform the
appropriateness scale, the safety / contraindication screen, and the triage
rules; they are not patient-facing.

## Appropriateness (Axis A)

- **ACC/AHA/.../STS 2023 Multimodality Appropriate Use Criteria for the
  Detection and Risk Assessment of Chronic Coronary Disease** — rates each
  testing modality 1–9 by clinical scenario: usually-appropriate (7–9),
  may-be-appropriate (4–6), usually-not-appropriate (1–3).
  <https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2023.03.410>
- **2024 ESC Guidelines for the management of chronic coronary syndromes** —
  selection of exercise ECG vs stress imaging vs CCTA by pre-test clinical
  likelihood; a negative exercise ECG can reclassify low-likelihood patients.
  <https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/45/36/3415/7743115>

## Safety / contraindication (Axis B)

- **ACC/AHA 2002 Guideline Update for Exercise Testing** — absolute
  contraindications include acute myocardial infarction (within 2 days),
  ongoing / high-risk unstable angina, uncontrolled symptomatic arrhythmia,
  decompensated heart failure, and **symptomatic severe aortic stenosis**;
  uncontrolled hypertension is a relative contraindication.
  <https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/01.cir.0000034670.06526.15>
- **Severe aortic stenosis** — in symptomatic patients, exercise testing is
  contraindicated; coronary angiography is the standard for assessing CAD.
  <https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8961810/>
- **Inability to exercise** — an exercise treadmill ECG requires the patient to
  exercise; redirect to a pharmacological / imaging stress modality (e.g.
  dobutamine stress echo, perfusion imaging) when the patient cannot exercise.

## Triage (Axis D)

- Recent acute coronary syndrome, severe symptomatic aortic stenosis, and an
  acute / emergency setting escalate the triage tier (routine → urgent →
  emergency) with an associated target timeframe.

## Completeness (Axis C)

- Mandatory-field checklist; the primary indication and the specific clinical
  question are weighted highest because they are the most commonly omitted,
  highest-value fields for safe and appropriate vetting.
