# WHO Emergency Unit Standardized Clinical Form: Trauma

This form implements the WHO standard clinical-encounter form for
trauma adult and paediatric presentations to a hospital emergency unit
(EU). It is the trauma-specific complement to the WHO Emergency Unit
Form: General and forms part of the WHO Emergency Care Toolkit family.

## ABCDEF primary survey

The trauma form adds two extensions over the General form's ABCD:

| Letter | Section in the form | Trauma-specific content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A — Airway | Step 5 | Same as general; cervical-spine stabilization always considered |
| B — Breathing | Step 6 | Tension/open pneumothorax flags; chest needle/tube |
| C — Circulation | Step 7 | External / cavity haemorrhage; tranexamic acid; massive-transfusion |
| D — Disability | Step 8 | GCS / AVPU; pupils; glucose |
| E — Exposure | Step 9 | Undress, log roll, prevent hypothermia |
| F — FAST | Step 9 | Focused Assessment with Sonography for Trauma (4 views: pericardium, RUQ Morison's, LUQ splenorenal, suprapubic) |

## High-risk red signs and high-risk trauma indicators

The trauma form's Step 3 captures two panels:

### High-risk red signs (mirror the general form)

- A/B — abnormal airway / breathing
- C — abnormal perfusion / catastrophic bleeding
- D — abnormal mental status / blood glucose
- Other — abnormal vital signs / pregnancy / extremes of age

### High-risk trauma indicators

Drawn from the *American College of Surgeons National Trauma Triage
Protocol* (ACS-COT 2021) and adopted into WHO BEC:

- Penetrating injury to head, neck, torso, or proximal extremity
- Chest wall instability or deformity (e.g. flail chest)
- Two or more proximal long-bone fractures
- Crushed, degloved, mangled, or pulseless extremity
- Amputation proximal to wrist or ankle
- Pelvic fracture
- Open or depressed skull fracture
- Paralysis
- Falls — adults >6 m, children >3 m or 2-3× their height
- Road-traffic crash — high-energy: ejection, death in same vehicle,
  intrusion >30 cm at occupant site, auto vs pedestrian thrown / run
  over / struck at >32 km/h, motorcycle crash >32 km/h

Reference: ACS Committee on Trauma. *National Guideline for the Field
Triage of Injured Patients*. JAMA Surg. 2022;157(7):e221150.
doi:10.1001/jamasurg.2022.1150
<https://www.facs.org/quality-programs/trauma/systems/national-trauma-triage-protocol/>

## Injury history

Step 10 captures four classes of detail required for trauma:

- **Mechanism** — blunt, penetrating, blast, burn, drowning, fall, RTC,
  assault, animal bite, other.
- **Intent** — accidental, self-inflicted, assault, intentional unknown.
- **Prehospital care** — interventions performed before arrival.
- **Substance use** — explicit tobacco / alcohol / drugs / IV drugs.

Intent capture is required for WHO injury surveillance and is mandated
by the WHO Injury Surveillance Guidelines 2001.

Reference: Holder Y, Peden M, Krug E, Lund J, Gururaj G, Kobusingye O
(eds). *Injury surveillance guidelines*. Geneva: WHO and CDC; 2001.
ISBN 92-4-159133-1.
<https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9241591331>

## Body diagram

The trauma form includes an anterior / posterior body diagram on Step 12
for marking injury locations. WHO recommends the Abbreviated Injury Scale
(AIS) and Injury Severity Score (ISS) be calculated downstream for
trauma-registry submission, but the form itself only documents the
anatomical map.

Reference: Baker SP, O'Neill B, Haddon W Jr, Long WB. The injury
severity score: a method for describing patients with multiple injuries
and evaluating emergency care. *J Trauma.* 1974;14(3):187-96.
PMID: 4814394.

## Diagnostics layout (trauma-specific)

| Lab | Trauma relevance |
| --- | --- |
| Hgb | Haemorrhage trend |
| Blood Type | Crossmatch for massive transfusion |
| Chemistry | Renal function, electrolytes |
| Hepatic | Liver enzymes for blunt abdominal trauma |
| UPT | Pregnancy status before imaging |

| Imaging | Trauma relevance |
| --- | --- |
| CXR | Pneumothorax, haemothorax, widened mediastinum |
| Pelvis XR | Pelvic ring injury (if FAST unavailable) |
| Head CT | Traumatic brain injury — Canadian CT Head Rule / NICE NG232 thresholds |
| C-spine | NEXUS / Canadian C-spine Rule application |
| Chest / Abd CT | Polytrauma evaluation |
| Extremity XR | Long-bone fracture |

## Tranexamic acid (TXA)

WHO BEC trauma module recommends TXA 1 g IV within 3 hours of injury for
adults with significant haemorrhage, following CRASH-2 evidence:

Reference: CRASH-2 trial collaborators. Effects of tranexamic acid on
death, vascular occlusive events, and blood transfusion in trauma
patients with significant haemorrhage (CRASH-2): a randomized,
placebo-controlled trial. *Lancet.* 2010;376(9734):23-32.
doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(10)60835-5
