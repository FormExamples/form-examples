# Trauma Triage and Primary Survey

This document describes how the trauma triage category and ABCDEF primary
survey are intended to be used in the resuscitation bay.

## Trauma triage (RED / YELLOW / GREEN)

The trauma SCF supports a three-tier triage scheme. RED is mandatory for
any high-risk red sign OR any high-risk trauma indicator. The categories
are:

- **RED** — immediate, life-threatening: ABCDE compromise, GCS ≤8,
  systolic BP <90, RR <10 or >29, penetrating torso/proximal-extremity
  wound, two or more proximal long-bone fractures, pelvic fracture,
  amputation, paralysis, or any of the high-risk trauma indicators.
- **YELLOW** — urgent, potentially serious: vital signs stable but
  abnormal, single long-bone fracture, open fracture without
  haemorrhagic shock, head injury without GCS drop, moderate burns.
- **GREEN** — non-urgent: walking wounded, minor lacerations, isolated
  closed-extremity injury, no abnormal vital signs.

This three-tier scheme aligns with the WHO Interagency Integrated Triage
Tool and the South African Triage Scale's collapsed three-band view.

## ABCDEF primary survey

### C₀ — Catastrophic haemorrhage (prefix, not a row)

While not a separate row on the form, WHO BEC and the *Joint Royal
Colleges Ambulance Liaison Committee* trauma guidelines recommend
addressing exsanguinating external bleeding before A in the trauma
context (the "<C>ABCDE" approach). On this form the catastrophic
haemorrhage is captured under Step 7 (Circulation) with explicit
intervention checkboxes.

### A — Airway (Step 5)

- Patency — angioedema, stridor, voice changes, oral/airway burns,
  obstruction by tongue/blood/secretions/vomit/foreign body.
- Interventions — repositioning, suction, OPA, NPA, LMA, BVM, ETT.
- **Cervical-spine precautions are maintained throughout** in any blunt
  trauma until clinically cleared by Canadian C-spine rule or imaging.

### B — Breathing (Step 6)

- Spontaneous respiratory rate.
- Chest rise — shallow, retractions, paradoxical.
- Trachea — midline or deviated.
- Breath sounds — left and right.
- Oxygen — flow rate and device.
- Chest needle or tube — laterality, size, depth.

Tension pneumothorax → immediate needle decompression
(4th-5th intercostal space anterior axillary line preferred per ATLS
10th edition; or 2nd ICS midclavicular line).

### C — Circulation (Step 7)

- Skin — warm, dry, pale, cyanotic, moist, cool.
- Capillary refill.
- Pulses — weak, asymmetric.
- JVD.
- Access — IV / CVL / IO with location and gauge.
- IVF — type and volume.
- Blood ordered.
- TXA / massive-transfusion protocol activation if shock and
  significant haemorrhage.

### D — Disability (Step 8)

- GCS or AVPU.
- Moves all extremities or deficit.
- Pupils — size and reactivity bilateral.
- Blood glucose — abnormal if <3.5 mmol/L.
- Interventions — glucose, antiepileptic, naloxone.

GCS is the canonical trauma metric (over AVPU) once a clinician is
available. The form captures GCS via the qualified-GCS field if the
patient is sedated, intubated, or has vision obstruction.

### E — Exposure (Step 9)

- Undress patient fully for inspection.
- Log roll to inspect back, perineum, gluteal cleft.
- Prevent hypothermia — warm IV fluids, blankets, warm room.

### F — FAST (Step 9)

FAST is **F**ocused **A**ssessment with **S**onography for **T**rauma —
a four-view bedside ultrasound:

1. **Pericardial** — sub-xiphoid; detects pericardial effusion /
   tamponade.
2. **RUQ — Morison's pouch** — hepatorenal recess; detects free fluid.
3. **LUQ — splenorenal recess**; detects free fluid.
4. **Suprapubic / pelvic** — detects free fluid in pelvis.

eFAST adds two thoracic views to detect pneumothorax.

Reference: ATLS Subcommittee. *Advanced Trauma Life Support Student
Course Manual*, 10th edition. American College of Surgeons; 2018.

## Secondary survey: head-to-toe physical exam

Step 12 captures an 11-body-system physical exam with an injury body
diagram. The exam is performed only after the primary survey is
stabilised. AMPLE history is collected in parallel where possible:

- **A**llergies
- **M**edications
- **P**ast medical history
- **L**ast meal
- **E**vents leading to injury

## Disposition

Six options captured at Step 17:

- Admit to Ward
- Admit to ICU
- Admit to Operating Theatre — triggers WHO Surgical Safety Checklist
- Transfer to other facility — triggers WHO Acute Referral Form
- Discharge
- Died — cause specified (not "cardiopulmonary arrest")
- Left without being seen
