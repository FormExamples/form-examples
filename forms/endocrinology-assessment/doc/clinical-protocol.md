# Clinical protocol

Operational protocol the endocrinology assessment encodes.

## Indications

- First endocrinology outpatient consultation.
- Abnormal screening result (e.g. raised TSH, hypertension with
  hypokalaemia, hyperprolactinaemia).
- Annual review for chronic endocrine disease (e.g. treated Graves',
  Addison's, acromegaly).

## Preconditions

- Recent (≤ 6 weeks) thyroid function (TSH, free T4, ± free T3).
- Recent fasting biochemistry: glucose / HbA1c, U&E, calcium, phosphate.
- Targeted hormones based on presenting complaint (see below).

## Step-by-step

1. **Demographics.** Identifiers and ethnicity (some hormone reference
   intervals are ethnicity-stratified).
2. **Presenting Symptoms.** Targeted ROS by axis:
   - Thyroid: heat / cold intolerance, weight change, palpitations,
     tremor, lethargy, mood.
   - Adrenal: weight gain, striae, easy bruising, hypertension, fatigue,
     hyperpigmentation.
   - Glucose: polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, blurred vision.
   - Reproductive: menstrual disturbance, libido, hirsutism, galactorrhoea,
     erectile dysfunction.
   - Pituitary: headache, visual-field defect, polyuria, growth changes.
   - Bone/calcium: bone pain, fragility fracture, renal stones.
3. **Thyroid Axis.** TSH, free T4, free T3, anti-TPO, TRAb if Graves'
   suspected.
4. **Adrenal Axis.** 9 am cortisol; if hypoadrenal pattern, short
   Synacthen. If hypertensive + hypokalaemic, renin/aldosterone.
5. **Glucose Metabolism.** HbA1c, fasting glucose (OGTT if indicated).
6. **Reproductive Axis.** FSH, LH, oestradiol or testosterone, SHBG,
   prolactin; AMH for ovarian reserve where indicated.
7. **Pituitary Function.** Prolactin, IGF-1, ACTH, cortisol, dynamic
   function tests if pituitary mass suspected.
8. **Bone & Calcium.** Corrected calcium, phosphate, PTH, 25-OH vitamin D,
   ALP, eGFR; FRAX score where age ≥ 50 y.
9. **Medications & Lifestyle Review.** Endocrine-active drugs (steroids,
   amiodarone, lithium, opioids, antipsychotics), supplements
   (iodine, biotin which can falsely lower TSH).
10. **Clinical Impression & Management Plan.** Per-axis categorization
    (Normal / Subclinical / Mild / Moderate / Severe) and onward plan.

## Per-axis categorization rule of thumb

- **Normal**: clinically and biochemically within reference range.
- **Subclinical**: biochemical abnormality without symptoms (e.g.
  subclinical hypothyroidism: TSH > upper limit, free T4 within range).
- **Mild**: symptomatic biochemical abnormality without end-organ
  complications.
- **Moderate**: symptomatic with single-organ complication or persistent
  biochemical disturbance despite first-line therapy.
- **Severe**: multi-organ involvement, emergency (e.g. Addisonian crisis,
  thyroid storm, pituitary apoplexy), or refractory to treatment.

## Onward referrals

- Thyroid eye disease → orbital MDT (NICE NG145 §1.5).
- Acromegaly → pituitary MDT and pituitary surgical centre.
- Cushing's syndrome → tertiary endocrinology with adrenal MDT.
- Primary hyperparathyroidism with calcium > 2.85 mmol/L or
  symptomatic → parathyroid surgery referral.
- Phaeochromocytoma → urgent adrenal MDT.
