# Federal law and uniform acts

- **Uniform Health-Care Decisions Act (1993)**, Uniform Law Commission. Written
  power of attorney for health care; agent authority begins on signing or on
  incapacity; agent restrictions for owners, operators, and employees of
  residential long-term care institutions, with a relatives exception, appear in
  state variants. Superseded for new enactments by the 2023 Uniform
  Health-Care Decisions Act. The 1993 text is used here only as the default rule
  frame.
- **Patient Self-Determination Act of 1990**, 42 U.S.C. 1395cc(f); 42 CFR
  489.100 (definition) and 489.102 (requirements): participating providers give
  written information, document whether a directive exists, do not condition
  care on a directive, and educate staff. Drives flag `F-HCPOA-013` and the
  "copy to facility" question.
- **HIPAA** privacy rule, 45 CFR 164.502(g) (personal representatives) and
  164.508 (authorizations). A HIPAA release lets the agent obtain records before
  incapacity is certified. Drives flag `F-HCPOA-008`.
- **Revised Uniform Anatomical Gift Act (2006)**: a person may make a gift, and
  an agent may do so when authorized. Drives flag `F-HCPOA-011`.
- **POLST**: a portable medical order for people with serious illness or
  frailty, signed by a clinician; followed by emergency responders; cannot
  appoint an agent. Advance directives are open to all adults and generally are
  not followed by emergency responders. Sources: polst.org (who is a POLST for),
  CaringInfo (POLSTs are portable medical orders), NCOA guide. Drives flag
  `F-HCPOA-010`.
