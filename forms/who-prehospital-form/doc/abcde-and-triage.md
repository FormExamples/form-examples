# Prehospital ABCDE Primary Survey and Triage

This document describes the bedside use of the WHO Prehospital SCF
primary-survey rows and the triage category.

## Triage categories

The form supports a three-tier triage scheme aligned with the WHO
Interagency Integrated Triage Tool (IITT):

- **RED** — Immediate / life-threatening: ABCDE compromise; airway
  threat; SpO2 <90% on RA; systolic BP <90 mmHg in adult; GCS ≤8;
  major haemorrhage; high-risk trauma.
- **YELLOW** — Urgent: stable but abnormal vital signs; moderate pain;
  isolated long-bone fracture; head injury without GCS drop.
- **GREEN** — Non-urgent: minor injury; stable vital signs; ambulatory.

In a mass-casualty incident the form supports an additional triage tag
workflow (the "Mass Casualty" checkbox at the header), typically:

- **BLACK** — deceased / expectant
- **RED** — immediate
- **YELLOW** — delayed
- **GREEN** — minor / walking wounded

Reference: WHO. *Interagency Integrated Triage Tool*. Geneva: WHO;
2020. ISBN 978-92-4-001734-4.
<https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240017344>

## High-risk signs (Step 3)

The form's four high-risk panels are aligned with WHO BEC:

| Panel | Indicators |
| --- | --- |
| A / B | Stridor, voice change, unable to swallow, oral burns, accessory muscle use, SpO2 <90% RA, RR <10 or >29 |
| C | Capillary refill >3 s, weak/asymmetric pulses, JVD, systolic BP <90, HR <55 or >130 |
| D | AVPU = V/P/U; GCS ≤13; pupillary asymmetry; new motor deficit; blood glucose <3.5 mmol/L |
| Other | Pregnancy in shock; extremes of age; severe pain; hypothermia / hyperthermia |

Any high-risk sign immediately upgrades the triage to RED.

## ABCDE primary survey

### A — Airway (Step 5)

- Patency check: clear / threatened / obstructed.
- Cause of obstruction: tongue, blood, secretions, vomit, foreign body,
  swelling, burn.
- Interventions: positioning, suctioning, OPA, NPA, LMA, BVM, ETT.
- Cervical spine: maintained throughout suspected trauma until cleared.

### B — Breathing (Step 6)

- Respiratory rate.
- Chest rise — symmetry, retractions, paradoxical movement.
- Tracheal position — midline or deviated.
- Breath sounds — bilateral comparison.
- Oxygen — flow rate and delivery device:
  - Nasal cannula 1-6 L/min for mild hypoxia.
  - Simple face mask 6-10 L/min for moderate.
  - Non-rebreather 10-15 L/min for severe.
  - BVM with O2 for inadequate ventilation.
  - CPAP / BiPAP for pulmonary oedema, COPD, asthma.
- Bronchodilator if wheeze (salbutamol 5 mg nebulized, repeat as needed).
- Chest needle decompression for suspected tension pneumothorax.

### C — Circulation (Step 7)

- Skin signs — colour, temperature, moisture.
- Capillary refill — central and peripheral.
- Pulses — rate, rhythm, strength, symmetry.
- JVD — assessed at 45° head-up.
- Access — IV (location, gauge), IO (location, age-appropriate).
- IV fluids — type (NS, LR / Hartmann's, Ringer's) and volume.
- Major external haemorrhage — direct pressure, packing, tourniquet
  (with time of application).
- Pelvic binder for suspected pelvic ring injury.
- TXA 1 g IV within 3 hours of trauma with significant haemorrhage.

### D — Disability (Step 8)

- GCS (E + V + M) or AVPU.
- Pupils — size in mm, reactivity.
- Lateralizing signs — facial droop, arm drift, leg weakness.
- Blood glucose — abnormal if <3.5 mmol/L.
- Interventions — IV/IM/IO dextrose for hypoglycaemia; midazolam or
  diazepam for active seizure; naloxone for suspected opioid overdose.

### E — Exposure (Step 9)

- Expose to identify hidden injuries; preserve dignity.
- Inspect back, axillae, perineum during log roll.
- Prevent hypothermia — warm blankets, warmed IV fluids, environmental
  control.
- Burn first aid — cool burn under cool water for 20 minutes if
  practical; clean cling-film cover.

## Drug doses commonly required

WHO BEC and JRCALC reference doses for prehospital use:

| Drug | Indication | Adult dose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Adrenaline | Anaphylaxis | 0.5 mg IM (1:1000) |
| Adrenaline | Cardiac arrest | 1 mg IV every 3-5 min |
| Salbutamol | Asthma, COPD | 5 mg nebulized |
| Hydrocortisone | Asthma, anaphylaxis | 200 mg IV/IM |
| Midazolam | Seizure | 10 mg IM/buccal |
| Glucose 10% | Hypoglycaemia | 100-150 mL IV |
| Naloxone | Opioid overdose | 400 mcg IM/IV titrated |
| Tranexamic acid | Trauma haemorrhage | 1 g IV over 10 min |
| Aspirin | ACS | 300 mg chewed PO |
| GTN spray | ACS, LVF | 400-800 mcg SL |
| Morphine | Severe pain | 5-10 mg IV titrated |

Reference: JRCALC / AACE. *UK Ambulance Services Clinical Practice
Guidelines*. Class Publishing; updated annually.

## Handover at hospital

The MIST handover format is supported by Step 16:

- **M**echanism / chief complaint
- **I**njuries / interventions and exam findings
- **S**igns / vital signs and trends
- **T**reatment given and response

Reference: Iedema R, Ball C, Daly B, et al. Design and trial of a new
ambulance-to-emergency department handover protocol: 'IMIST-AMBO'. *BMJ
Qual Saf.* 2012;21:627-33. doi:10.1136/bmjqs-2011-000766
