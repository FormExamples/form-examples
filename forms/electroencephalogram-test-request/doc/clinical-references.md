# Clinical references

Authoritative guidance underpinning the four-axis grading engine.

## Role and limitations of EEG (NICE NG217 / ILAE)

- A routine EEG carried out while awake should be **considered to support a
  diagnosis** of epilepsy and to provide information about seizure type or
  epilepsy syndrome, when an epileptic seizure is suspected.
- **Do not use EEG to exclude a diagnosis of epilepsy** — a normal or
  non-diagnostic EEG does not rule out epilepsy when clinical suspicion remains.
- EEG must **not** be used in isolation; it complements history and examination.
- Sleep-deprived and ambulatory EEG increase the yield of interictal
  epileptiform discharges when a routine EEG is non-diagnostic.
- Long-term video-EEG monitoring (LTVEM) classifies patients in whom the seizure
  type or epilepsy syndrome is undetermined.

## EEG types

| EEG type | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Routine awake | First-line; supports diagnosis and seizure classification |
| Sleep-deprived | Activation to increase yield of interictal discharges |
| Ambulatory 24-hour | Outpatient capture over a longer window |
| Video-telemetry | Inpatient LTVEM to characterize events and classify syndrome |

## Appropriateness (NICE NG217 / ILAE)

Ordinal 1–9 scale in three bands: usually-appropriate (7–9), may-be-appropriate
(4–6), usually-not-appropriate (1–3). Mapped per (indication × EEG type).
Requests whose clinical question implies EEG is being used to *exclude* epilepsy
are graded down and flagged.

## Triage tiers

| Tier | Target | Triggers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency | same hour / same day | suspected status epilepticus, encephalopathy with ongoing seizures |
| Urgent | 24–72 h / 1–2 weeks | recent first seizure, frequent seizures, inpatient work-up |
| Routine | standard booking | seizure classification, medication review, follow-up |
