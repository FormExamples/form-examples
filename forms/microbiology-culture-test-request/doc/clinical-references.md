# Clinical references

Authoritative guidance underpinning the four-axis grading engine.

## Appropriateness (Axis A — UKHSA SMI specimen / indication match)

Ordinal 1–9 scale in three bands: usually-appropriate (7–9),
may-be-appropriate (4–6), usually-not-appropriate (1–3). There is **no single
published 1–9 microbiology appropriateness score**; this axis anchors on
specimen-and-indication appropriateness (analogous to the ACR ordinal scale)
using UKHSA / RCPath SMI specimen-selection guidance — e.g. blood cultures for
suspected sepsis, midstream urine for suspected UTI, wound swab for wound
infection, sputum for lower respiratory infection, CSF for suspected meningitis.

## Pre-analytical specimen safety (Axis B — SMI)

| Band | Triggers |
| --- | --- |
| reject-risk | no specimen collected; blood culture taken after antibiotics started |
| caution | current antibiotics on board; missing collection date/time; long transport delay |
| ok | specimen collected, sampled before antibiotics, correct specimen for indication |

SMI and the NHS England blood-culture pathway recommend inoculated blood-culture
bottles reach an automated analyser without delay, ideally within ~4 hours of
collection. Blood cultures should be taken **before** the first antibiotic dose.

## Triage tiers (Axis D — NICE NG51)

| Tier | Target | Triggers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| stat | within the hour | suspected sepsis; suspected meningitis (CSF) |
| urgent | same day | febrile inpatient; immunocompromised with infection |
| routine | standard turnaround | screening, routine MC&S, non-urgent indications |

NICE NG51: take blood cultures (and other relevant cultures) before
administering antibiotics, and give a broad-spectrum antimicrobial within 1 hour
when high-risk criteria are met — but do not let culture sampling delay
antibiotics.
