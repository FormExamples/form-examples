Wells Score for Pulmonary Embolism (PE)

Pre-test probability rule: seven weighted criteria scored 0 to 12.5, stratifying PE likely (> 4) versus PE unlikely (≤ 4).

Complete the Wells PE criteria below for an adult (16 years or over) with clinically suspected acute pulmonary embolism. Each present criterion contributes its weighted points — clinical signs of DVT and "PE most likely" score +3; heart rate > 100, immobilisation or recent surgery, and previous DVT/PE score +1.5; haemoptysis and malignancy score +1 — for a total of 0 to 12.5. A score of more than 4 means PE likely (immediate CTPA); 4 or less means PE unlikely (D-dimer). Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

The Wells score directs the diagnostic pathway; it does not diagnose or exclude PE on its own. It does not override clinical judgement, and it is not for a haemodynamically unstable patient — suspected massive PE requires immediate resuscitation and imaging or empirical treatment, not scoring.

The Wells PE score was derived and validated by Wells et al. (Thromb Haemost 2000; Ann Intern Med 2001) and is embedded in the NICE NG158 two-level pathway. This tool is for information only and is not a diagnosis; investigate, anticoagulate, and escalate in line with local policy and clinical judgement.
