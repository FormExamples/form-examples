Complete the seven sections below to check a will plan. The tool does not give legal advice and does not draft the will. It checks that the plan is complete and meets the formalities of the testator's state, based on the Uniform Probate Code and a table of state rules: the testator is an adult of sound mind, the will is signed at the end in front of two witnesses, no witness loses a gift under the interested-witness rule, the residuary shares add up to 100%, a spouse is not left below the elective share, and a self-proving affidavit is planned. It classifies the plan as valid, ready to execute, incomplete, or formality risk, and raises flags. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

A classification of valid, ready to execute does not guarantee that a court will admit the will. State law changes and has exceptions; the probate court decides, and a licensed attorney in the testator's state should review the will before signing.
