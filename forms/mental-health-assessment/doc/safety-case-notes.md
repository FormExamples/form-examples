# Safety case notes — mental health assessment

This form is a **screening instrument** in a primary-care or community
mental-health setting. The safety-critical pathway covers suicidality,
acute psychiatric emergency, and safeguarding.

## PHQ-9 item 9 — suicidality safety flag

PHQ-9 item 9 — "Thoughts that you would be better off dead, or of hurting
yourself in some way" — is treated as an **independent flag** regardless
of total PHQ-9 score.

| Item 9 response             | Action                                                    |
| --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0 — Not at all              | Note in record; routine follow-up                         |
| 1 — Several days            | Conduct full risk assessment in the Risk Assessment step  |
| 2 — More than half the days | Same-day clinician review; consider urgent referral       |
| 3 — Nearly every day        | Same-day clinician review; do not leave patient unattended; consider Mental Health Act assessment if intent + means present |

## Crisis pathway

When the patient discloses immediate intent or plan during the assessment:

1. **Stay with the patient.** Do not let them leave the consultation
   unaccompanied while at acute risk.
2. **Contact the crisis team** (Crisis Resolution and Home Treatment
   Team) for the local catchment.
3. **Consider 999 / A&E referral** for medical clearance if an act of
   self-harm has occurred.
4. **Consider Mental Health Act assessment** if the patient lacks capacity
   to consent to admission and meets criteria for compulsory admission
   under the Mental Health Act 1983 (England and Wales) or equivalent
   legislation.
5. **Document** the risk assessment, clinical reasoning, plan, and
   safety-planning conversation.

## Risk assessment principles

NICE NG225 §1.3.2 — risk-assessment tools must **not** be used as
standalone decision-makers. The Risk Assessment step prompts the
clinician to record:

- **Current ideation** — frequency, intensity, duration, control.
- **Plan** — specificity, lethality.
- **Means** — access to firearms, medication stockpiles, hanging points,
  preferred method.
- **Intent** — strength and timing of stated intent.
- **History** — previous attempts, prior method, severity, intent.
- **Modifiable risk factors** — substance use, recent loss, separation,
  job loss, social isolation, hopelessness.
- **Static risk factors** — male sex, prior attempt, family history of
  suicide, chronic pain, recent inpatient discharge.
- **Protective factors** — children at home, religious beliefs,
  therapeutic alliance, future-orientation.

## Safety planning

When risk is identified but immediate admission is not required, the
clinician collaboratively develops a **safety plan** with the patient.
The Stanley-Brown Safety Planning Intervention (Stanley & Brown, 2012) is
the recommended structure:

1. Recognizing warning signs and triggers.
2. Internal coping strategies.
3. Social contacts and settings for distraction.
4. People who can be contacted for help.
5. Professional and emergency contacts (including 999, NHS 111 option
   2 / 988 in the US, Samaritans 116 123, crisis text lines).
6. Restricting access to lethal means (e.g. removing medication
   stockpiles, locking firearms).

## Substance use safety considerations

The Substance Use step prompts review of:

- Alcohol-dependence withdrawal risk (CIWA-Ar / SADQ assessment if
  indicated).
- Benzodiazepine and opioid use — overdose risk.
- Stimulant or hallucinogen use — psychosis risk.

Combined depression + substance use increases the risk of completed
suicide and warrants closer monitoring.

## Safeguarding

The clinician records concerns under:

- **Adult safeguarding** — Care Act 2014 (England) or equivalent;
  identifies the local safeguarding authority and trigger criteria
  (abuse, neglect, financial exploitation).
- **Child safeguarding** — Working Together to Safeguard Children
  (HM Government, current edition). Records concerns about children
  in the patient's care.
- **Domestic abuse** — direct enquiry, signposting to refuges and
  helplines, MARAC referral where appropriate.

## Capacity, consent, and confidentiality

- Capacity is decision-specific; routine capacity for sharing GP records
  is generally assumed unless contrary evidence.
- Confidentiality may be breached where required to prevent serious
  harm to the patient or others (GMC *Confidentiality* guidance).
  Document the reasoning.

## Indicators for inpatient referral

- Active suicidal intent with plan and means.
- Severe symptoms with breakdown of community support.
- Psychotic depression or melancholic depression with significant
  agitation.
- High-risk perinatal depression (postpartum psychosis, severe PND).
- Comorbid uncontrolled medical condition (e.g. severe self-neglect,
  dehydration).
