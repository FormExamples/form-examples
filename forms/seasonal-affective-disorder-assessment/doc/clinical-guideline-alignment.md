# Clinical guideline alignment — seasonal affective disorder

## NICE NG222 — Depression in adults: treatment and management

Source: National Institute for Health and Care Excellence.
Guideline ID: **NG222** (published June 2022).
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng222

NG222 treats SAD as a presentation of recurrent depressive disorder with
a seasonal pattern. The recommended treatment hierarchy is the same as
for non-seasonal depression: psychological therapy, antidepressant
medication, or combined therapy depending on severity and preference.

NICE has historically advised that **light therapy** may be considered as
an adjunctive treatment for SAD, but evidence quality is moderate and
light therapy is not formally recommended as a first-line stand-alone
treatment. The form's Treatment Plan step prompts the clinician to
discuss light therapy alongside conventional options.

## DSM-5-TR — Major Depressive Disorder with Seasonal Pattern specifier

American Psychiatric Association (2022). DSM-5-TR.

The "with seasonal pattern" specifier may be applied to:

- Major Depressive Disorder, Recurrent (296.3x / F33.x).
- Bipolar I Disorder (with seasonal pattern of depressive episodes).
- Bipolar II Disorder (with seasonal pattern of depressive episodes).

The specifier requires:

- A regular temporal relationship between onset and a particular time of
  year (most commonly autumn or winter onset).
- Full remissions or change to non-depressed phase at a characteristic
  time of year (most commonly spring).
- At least two episodes in the past 2 years demonstrating the seasonal
  relationship, and no non-seasonal episodes in that period.
- Seasonal episodes substantially outnumber non-seasonal episodes over a
  lifetime.

## ICD-11

World Health Organization. ICD-11 MMS.
URL: https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en

ICD-11 does not provide a separate code for SAD. The seasonal pattern is
recorded under:

- **6A71** — Recurrent depressive disorder, with the **seasonal pattern**
  qualifier.
- **6A60–6A61** — Bipolar disorder, with seasonal pattern of depressive
  episodes.

## Light-therapy evidence base

- The CANMAT 2016 clinical guidelines for the management of adults with
  major depressive disorder include light therapy as a first-line
  treatment for SAD and a second-line adjunct for non-seasonal depression.
  Source: Lam, R. W., et al. (2016). Canadian Network for Mood and
  Anxiety Treatments (CANMAT) 2016 Clinical Guidelines for the Management
  of Adults with Major Depressive Disorder: Section 1. Disease Burden
  and Principles of Care. *Canadian Journal of Psychiatry*, 61(9),
  510–523.
  DOI: https://doi.org/10.1177/0706743716659416

- A typical light-therapy regimen is 10,000 lux white light for 30
  minutes per morning, started in early autumn and continued through the
  symptomatic season.

## Differential diagnosis

The clinician should consider:

- **Non-seasonal recurrent major depressive disorder** — episodes occur
  outside the seasonal pattern.
- **Bipolar disorder** — winter depression with summer hypomania.
- **Hypothyroidism** — can mimic atypical depression; check TFTs.
- **Vitamin D deficiency** — frequently coexisting in northern latitudes;
  check 25(OH)D.
- **Sleep-wake disorders** — delayed sleep phase disorder.
- **Substance-induced depression**.
- **Adjustment disorder** with seasonal life events (e.g. anniversary
  reactions).
