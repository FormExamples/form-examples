# Safety case notes — SAD assessment

SAD is a recurrent depressive presentation. The same suicide-risk
considerations apply as for any depressive disorder, with some
seasonal-specific factors.

## Suicidality

PHQ-9 item 9 ("Thoughts that you would be better off dead, or of hurting
yourself in some way") triggers the safety pathway regardless of total
score:

| Item 9 response | Action                                                  |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0               | Routine follow-up                                       |
| 1               | Full risk assessment                                    |
| 2               | Same-day clinician review                               |
| 3               | Same-day review; do not leave patient unattended; consider Mental Health Act |

## Seasonal risk profile

Epidemiological studies indicate that suicide rates show a spring peak
in northern hemisphere populations, not coincident with the maximal
symptom intensity of winter-pattern SAD. The clinician should not assume
that suicide risk falls as the season improves; the **transition out of
winter depression**, with returning energy in spring, may coincide with
increased risk in patients still cognitively/affectively low.

## Bipolar diathesis

A small proportion of patients with winter depression have a bipolar
diathesis with summer hypomania or mania. The Treatment Plan step
prompts review of:

- Previous summer episodes of elevated mood, decreased sleep need,
  pressured speech.
- Family history of bipolar disorder.
- Antidepressant-induced mood switch.

If a bipolar diathesis is suspected, the patient should be referred for
psychiatric assessment before starting antidepressant monotherapy.

## Light therapy safety

When light therapy is recommended, the form prompts the clinician to
discuss:

- **Mood switch risk** in patients with bipolar diathesis (rare but
  documented).
- **Ophthalmological screening** if pre-existing retinal disease,
  diabetes mellitus, photosensitizing medications (e.g. lithium,
  amiodarone, certain antibiotics, isotretinoin, St John's wort).
- **Eye strain, headache** — common, usually mild and self-limiting.
- **Skin photosensitivity** — usually not relevant for visible-light
  boxes without UV emission, but check device specification.

## Vitamin D

Vitamin D deficiency is common at high latitudes and is associated with
depressive symptoms. Routine checking of 25(OH)D and supplementation per
NICE NG228 (vitamin D, 2023) is reasonable but is not a treatment for
SAD per se.

## Substance use

Captured indirectly via the Previous Treatments and Risk Assessment
steps. Comorbid alcohol use increases suicide risk and complicates
treatment.

## Safeguarding

Standard depression safeguarding applies:

- Adult safeguarding (Care Act 2014).
- Children in the household.
- Domestic abuse direct enquiry.

## Crisis pathway

When acute risk emerges:

1. Stay with the patient.
2. Contact local Crisis Resolution Home Treatment Team or NHS 111 option
   2.
3. Consider 999 / A&E for acute intent or attempted self-harm.
4. Document the assessment and safety plan.
5. Provide crisis numbers (Samaritans 116 123 in UK/Ireland;
   988 Lifeline in US).

## Re-screening cadence

- Symptomatic patients: every 2 weeks during active treatment.
- Recovered patients: re-screen at the start of the typical at-risk
  season and quarterly thereafter.
- Encourage the patient to maintain a mood diary across the year to
  evidence the seasonal pattern over time.
