# Glossary

Alphabetized reference of terms used in the LP1F form, the LP12 Guide,
the LPA validation engine, and the on-screen help.

## A

### Applicant

The person who applies to the Office of the Public Guardian (OPG) to
register the LPA. The applicant must be the donor or one (or more) of
the attorneys (LPA Regs 2007 reg. 11). Captured in LP1F section 12.

### Attorney

A person (or, exceptionally, a trust corporation) appointed by the
donor to make decisions about the donor's property and financial
affairs once the LPA is registered. Must be ≥ 18, must have mental
capacity, and (for a property-and-financial LPA) must not be bankrupt
or subject to a debt relief order (MCA 2005 s. 10).

## B

### Best interests

The decision-making standard imposed on an attorney by MCA 2005 s. 1(5)
and s. 4. When acting under the LPA, the attorney must do what is in
the donor's best interests, taking into account the donor's past and
present wishes, beliefs, values, and the views of relevant others
(s. 4(6)–(7)). The MCA Code of Practice chapter 5 expands the test.

### Bankruptcy

A person who is bankrupt or subject to a debt relief order (DRO)
cannot be appointed as an attorney for property and financial affairs
(MCA 2005 s. 10(2)). If an attorney becomes bankrupt after appointment
the LPA terminates as to that person (s. 13(8)).

## C

### Certificate provider

The independent person who signs section 10 of LP1F to certify that
the donor (a) understands the purpose of the LPA and the powers
conferred, (b) has not been subject to fraud or undue pressure, and
(c) is not making the LPA because of any other concern (Sch. 1 para.
2(1)(e) MCA 2005). May qualify by knowledge ("known the donor
personally for ≥ 2 years") or by professional skill (e.g. GP,
solicitor, registered healthcare professional). Eligibility is
restricted by LPA Regs 2007 reg. 8.

### Continuation sheet

A LPC form attached to LP1F when the standard space is exceeded:

- **LPC1** — additional attorneys, replacement attorneys, or
  people-to-notify.
- **LPC2** — additional decisions / instructions / preferences.
- **LPC3** — signed by another person at the donor's direction
  (the donor cannot physically sign).
- **LPC4** — trust corporation acting as attorney.

### Court of Protection

The specialist senior court (in England and Wales) established by MCA
2005 s. 45 to make decisions about the property, affairs, health, and
welfare of people who lack capacity. The court can revoke or vary an
LPA, appoint deputies, and resolve disputes about an attorney's
conduct.

## D

### Deputyship

A court-appointed alternative to an LPA: where someone has already
lost capacity without making a valid LPA, the Court of Protection may
appoint a **deputy** to make decisions on their behalf. Deputyships
are more burdensome and expensive than LPAs and are supervised by the
OPG.

### Donor

The person making the LPA — the individual whose property and
financial affairs the attorneys will be authorized to manage. Must be
≥ 18 and must have mental capacity at the time of signing (MCA 2005
s. 9(2)).

## E

### Enduring Power of Attorney (EPA)

The predecessor instrument under the Enduring Powers of Attorney Act
1985, abolished prospectively by MCA 2005 s. 66(1)(b). An EPA created
before 1 October 2007 remains valid and may still be registered with
the OPG (MCA 2005 Sch. 4); no new EPAs may be created.

## F

### Fee

The application fee paid to the OPG to register the LPA. As at
publication date the fee is £82 per LPA (set by the Public Guardian
(Fees, etc.) Regulations 2007 as amended). Reduced fees and
exemptions are available via form LPA120A — see
[opg-registration-workflow.md](./opg-registration-workflow.md).

## J

### Jointly

An appointment under which the attorneys must all agree on every
decision (MCA 2005 s. 10(4)(a)). If any one attorney can no longer
act, the entire LPA fails unless replacement attorneys are appointed.

### Jointly and severally

The default appointment mode under which any attorney may make any
decision alone (MCA 2005 s. 10(4)(b)). The most robust mode for
practical use.

### Jointly for some decisions, severally for other decisions

A "mixed" mode under MCA 2005 s. 10(4)(c). Requires LPC continuation
sheet 2 specifying which decisions are joint.

## L

### LPA

Lasting Power of Attorney. Statutory deed under MCA 2005 Part 1 by
which a donor confers on one or more attorneys the authority to make
decisions about either:

- **Property and financial affairs** (LP1F — this form), or
- **Health and welfare** (LP1H — separate form).

### LPC

The OPG's set of continuation sheets — see *Continuation sheet*
above.

### LPA120A

The OPG's reduced-fee / fee-exemption application form. Evidence
attached to LPA120A (e.g. proof of means-tested benefit or low income)
is required if the donor is asking for the fee to be reduced or
waived.

## M

### MCA

The Mental Capacity Act 2005 (c. 9). The primary statute governing
LPAs in England and Wales.

### MCA Code of Practice

The statutory code issued under MCA 2005 ss. 42–43 (TSO 2007).
Practitioners must "have regard" to the Code when making decisions for
people who lack capacity (s. 42(4)). The Code expands on the five
principles, the two-stage capacity test, the best-interests checklist,
and LPA operation.

### Mental capacity

The legal threshold defined by MCA 2005 s. 2(1): a person lacks
capacity if at the material time they are unable to make a decision
because of an impairment of, or disturbance in the functioning of, the
mind or brain. Inability to make a decision is further defined in s. 3
(understand, retain, use or weigh, communicate). Capacity is **issue-
specific** and **time-specific**.

## O

### OPG

The Office of the Public Guardian — the executive agency of the UK
Ministry of Justice that maintains the register of LPAs and EPAs,
supervises court-appointed deputies, and investigates concerns about
attorneys and deputies. Statutory basis: MCA 2005 ss. 57–60.

## P

### People to notify

Optional notice recipients listed in LP1F section 6. The OPG sends
each named person an LP3 form when the application to register is
made, giving them three weeks to object to the registration on
specified grounds (LPA Regs 2007 reg. 14). Maximum five.

### Property and financial affairs LPA

The category of LPA for which LP1F is the prescribed form: bank
accounts, investments, real property, debts, benefits, and tax. May be
used while the donor still has capacity, unless the donor restricts
its use to incapacity only (LP1F section 5).

### Public Guardian

The statutory officer who heads the OPG. Office created by MCA 2005
s. 57. Maintains the register of LPAs (s. 58), supervises deputies,
and refers concerns to the Court of Protection.

## R

### Registration

The process by which an LPA becomes legally usable: the OPG checks
the form, gives notice to people-to-notify, observes the four-week
waiting period, and (if no objection succeeds) records the LPA on the
public register and stamps every page (MCA 2005 Sch. 1). An
unregistered LPA has no legal effect.

### Replacement attorney

A person nominated to step in if one of the originally appointed
attorneys is unable or unwilling to continue acting (MCA 2005 s.
10(8)). Strongly recommended when attorneys are appointed jointly,
because otherwise the loss of any one attorney terminates the LPA.

## T

### Trust corporation

A company entitled by law to act as a trustee (e.g. a bank trustee
service or some solicitors' firms). May be appointed as the sole
attorney for property and financial affairs (MCA 2005 s. 10(1)),
provided LPC continuation sheet 4 is completed. Cannot be appointed
for health-and-welfare LPAs.

## W

### Witness

The person who watches the donor or an attorney sign LP1F. Must be
≥ 18 and is restricted by LPA Regs 2007 reg. 9(2):

- The donor's signature witness must not be one of the attorneys or
  replacement attorneys.
- An attorney's signature witness must not be the donor.

A witness signs and prints their name and address next to the
signature they have witnessed.
