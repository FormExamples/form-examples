# Mental Capacity Act 2005 — Legal Framework

This document summarizes the parts of the Mental Capacity Act 2005
(c. 9) ("MCA 2005") and the Lasting Powers of Attorney, Enduring
Powers of Attorney and Public Guardian Regulations 2007
(SI 2007/1253) ("LPA Regs 2007") that are relevant to the LP1F deed
and to the validation engine.

Where a citation is uncertain it is marked `(approximate)`. All other
citations are to the Act and Regulations as in force on the LP1F
October 2025 issue.

## 1. The five statutory principles (s. 1)

Section 1 sets out the five principles that govern every act and
decision under the Act, including those of an attorney under an LPA:

1. **Presumption of capacity** (s. 1(2)) — a person must be assumed
   to have capacity unless it is established that they lack capacity.
2. **Right to make unwise decisions** (s. 1(4)) — a person is not to
   be treated as unable to make a decision merely because they make
   an unwise one.
3. **All practicable steps** (s. 1(3)) — a person is not to be treated
   as unable to make a decision unless all practicable steps to help
   them have been taken without success.
4. **Best interests** (s. 1(5)) — an act done, or decision made,
   under the Act for or on behalf of a person who lacks capacity must
   be done, or made, in their best interests.
5. **Least restrictive option** (s. 1(6)) — the act or decision must
   be the least restrictive of the person's rights and freedom of
   action among the available options.

An attorney must apply these principles when acting under the LPA;
the certificate provider at section 10 of LP1F is confirming that the
donor understands they are conferring powers governed by these
principles.

## 2. The two-stage capacity test (ss. 2–3)

### Stage 1 — the diagnostic test (s. 2(1))

A person lacks capacity in relation to a matter if, at the material
time, they are unable to make a decision for themselves in relation
to that matter **because of** an impairment of, or a disturbance in
the functioning of, the mind or brain.

The impairment may be permanent or temporary (s. 2(2)).

Capacity is **decision-specific** and **time-specific** (s. 2(1)).
A person may have capacity to make some decisions and not others, and
capacity may fluctuate.

### Stage 2 — the functional test (s. 3(1))

A person is unable to make a decision for themselves if they are
unable:

- (a) to **understand** the information relevant to the decision,
- (b) to **retain** that information,
- (c) to **use or weigh** that information as part of the process of
  making the decision, or
- (d) to **communicate** the decision (by any means).

Both stages must be satisfied to conclude that a person lacks
capacity (s. 2(1)). The information relevant to the decision
includes the reasonably foreseeable consequences of deciding one way
or another or of failing to decide (s. 3(4)).

The donor of an LP1F must have capacity at the time of signing
(s. 9(2)(c)). This is what the certificate provider at section 10 of
LP1F confirms.

## 3. Best interests (s. 4)

When an attorney makes a decision under the LPA in respect of a donor
who has lost capacity, s. 4 imposes a structured best-interests
checklist:

- s. 4(2) — consider all relevant circumstances.
- s. 4(3) — consider whether the donor is likely to regain capacity.
- s. 4(4) — permit and encourage the donor to participate.
- s. 4(5) — the decision must not be motivated by a desire to bring
  about the donor's death (for life-sustaining treatment — health-
  and-welfare LPAs only).
- s. 4(6) — consider the donor's past and present wishes, feelings,
  beliefs and values.
- s. 4(7) — take into account the views of anyone named by the donor,
  carers, and any deputy.

The MCA Code of Practice chapter 5 expands the test.

## 4. LPAs under Part 1 (ss. 9–14)

### s. 9 — creation

- An LPA is a power of attorney under which the donor confers on the
  donee(s) authority to make decisions about the donor's personal
  welfare or property and affairs, when the donor no longer has
  capacity to make them.
- The donor must be **≥ 18** and have **capacity** to grant the LPA
  (s. 9(2)).
- The LPA must be made in the prescribed form and registered with
  the OPG (s. 9(2)(b), Sch. 1).
- It is not effective until registered (s. 9(3)).

### s. 10 — appointment of attorneys

- The donor may appoint a single attorney or several. The donor may
  also appoint a **trust corporation** as a single attorney for
  property and financial affairs (s. 10(1)(a)).
- An individual attorney must be **≥ 18** (s. 10(1)(a)).
- An individual attorney for property and financial affairs must not
  be **bankrupt or subject to a debt relief order** (s. 10(2)).
- Multiple attorneys may be appointed:
  - **Jointly** (s. 10(4)(a)) — must act together;
  - **Jointly and severally** (s. 10(4)(b)) — may act alone or
    together; or
  - **Jointly in respect of some matters and jointly and severally
    in others** (s. 10(4)(c)) — "mixed" mode.
- The donor may appoint **replacement attorneys** (s. 10(8)).

### s. 11 — restrictions on personal-welfare LPAs

Not directly relevant to LP1F (property and financial affairs).

### s. 12 — gifts

An attorney for property and financial affairs may make only limited
gifts (s. 12(2)): customary gifts to family / friends on
anniversaries or marriages, gifts to charities the donor used to
support, and only where reasonable having regard to all circumstances
and in particular the size of the donor's estate. Beyond this the
attorney must apply to the Court of Protection.

### s. 13 — termination

The LPA terminates on death, on the donor's loss of capacity (for
personal-welfare LPAs of a particular shape), on the attorney's
bankruptcy (s. 13(8) — for property-and-financial LPAs), on
disclaimer (s. 13(5)), or on revocation by the donor (s. 13(2), while
capacitous).

### s. 14 — protection where the LPA is invalid

A third party (e.g. a bank) who acts in good faith on what they
believe to be a valid LPA is protected from liability.

## 5. Statutory formal requirements (Schedule 1)

Schedule 1 of the Act, together with LPA Regs 2007 regs. 5–10,
prescribes the form of an LPA.

| Requirement | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Use of the prescribed form (currently LP1F) | Sch. 1 para. 1; reg. 5 |
| Donor's signed statement of understanding and intention | Sch. 1 para. 2(1)(b) |
| Statement by the certificate provider | Sch. 1 para. 2(1)(e); reg. 7 |
| Statement by each attorney accepting the appointment | Sch. 1 para. 2(1)(c) |
| Donor and attorney signatures must each be witnessed | reg. 9(2) |
| Signing order: donor (s. 9 of LP1F), certificate provider (s. 10), then each attorney (s. 11) | reg. 9(6) |
| Continuation sheets must be in the prescribed LPC form | reg. 5(2) |

A LPA that does not comply with these requirements is not a valid LPA
(MCA 2005 s. 9(3) read with Sch. 1 para. 1). The validator's blocker
rules implement these requirements.

## 6. Statutory restrictions

### On the certificate provider (LPA Regs 2007 reg. 8)

A person **must not** be a certificate provider if they are:

- An attorney or replacement attorney under the LPA (reg. 8(1)(c)),
  or under any other LPA / EPA made by the donor.
- A family member of the donor — by blood, marriage, civil
  partnership, or step-relationship (reg. 8(1)(d)).
- A business partner or paid employee of the donor (reg. 8(1)(d))
  *(approximate)*.
- A family member of any attorney or replacement attorney
  (reg. 8(1)(d)).
- An owner, manager, director, or employee of any care home in
  which the donor is living, or a family member of such a person
  (reg. 8(2)).

A certificate provider must also be ≥ 18 and competent to certify.

### On witnesses (LPA Regs 2007 reg. 9)

- The donor's signature witness must not be one of the attorneys or
  replacement attorneys (reg. 9(2)).
- An attorney's signature witness must not be the donor (reg. 9(2)).
- A witness must be ≥ 18.

### On attorneys (MCA 2005 s. 10)

- ≥ 18 (s. 10(1)(a)).
- Not bankrupt and not subject to a debt relief order for
  property-and-financial LPAs (s. 10(2)).
- Must have mental capacity (s. 10 implied; see also MCA Code
  ch. 7).

## 7. The Public Guardian and the OPG (ss. 57–60)

- s. 57 establishes the **Public Guardian**, a statutory officer
  supported by the Office of the Public Guardian (OPG).
- s. 58 sets out the Public Guardian's functions:
  - maintaining the register of LPAs and EPAs (s. 58(1)(a));
  - supervising deputies (s. 58(1)(c));
  - investigating concerns about attorneys and deputies (s. 58(1)(h));
  - publishing information about the Act (s. 58(1)(b)).
- s. 60 enables fees-and-regulations rules — the source of the
  current £82 application fee.

## 8. The Court of Protection (ss. 45–56)

The Court of Protection is the senior court that supervises operation
of the Act. In relation to LPAs it can:

- Determine whether an LPA is valid (s. 22(2)).
- Revoke an LPA on prescribed grounds (s. 22(4)(b)) — typically
  fraud, undue pressure, or the attorney acting outside their
  authority or contrary to the donor's best interests.
- Direct the OPG to refuse registration where a prescribed-ground
  objection succeeds.
- Appoint a **deputy** if no LPA has been made.

## 9. The MCA Code of Practice

Issued under MCA 2005 ss. 42–43 (TSO 2007). Statutory professionals
(including attorneys acting under a registered LPA) must "have
regard" to the Code (s. 42(4)). The Code is the operational manual
that expands ss. 1–4 and is referenced throughout the LP12 Guide.

## 10. Updates: Powers of Attorney Act 2023

The Powers of Attorney Act 2023 (c. 22) modernizes LPA registration
(digital first, photo-ID checks, online objection). It is being
phased in; the current LP1F October 2025 issue and the validator
implement the pre-existing paper-and-form workflow described in this
document.
