Parkland Formula for Burns

Fluid resuscitation for major thermal burns: total 24 h volume = 4 mL × weight (kg) × %TBSA, given as half in the first 8 h from injury and half over the next 16 h.

Enter the patient's body weight (kg), the percentage of total body surface area (%TBSA) with partial-thickness or deeper burns, and the time the burn occurred. The calculator applies the Parkland formula to estimate the first-24-hour crystalloid (Hartmann's / lactated Ringer's) volume, splits it into the mandated first-8-hour and next-16-hour phases measured from the time of injury, derives an hourly rate for each phase, and reports a urine-output titration target. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

The Parkland volume is a starting estimate only. Fluids must then be titrated to physiological endpoints — chiefly a urine output of 0.5–1.0 mL/kg/h in adults — and managed with a specialist burns service. Superficial (epidermal) burns are excluded from the %TBSA. This tool does not replace airway assessment, senior review, or specialist advice.

The Parkland (Baxter) formula is drawn from Baxter & Shires (Ann N Y Acad Sci 1968); %TBSA is estimated by the Wallace Rule of Nines or a Lund–Browder chart. This calculator produces a starting fluid estimate for a clinician to review and titrate; it is not a diagnosis and does not administer therapy. Interpret in the clinical context and follow local burns and major-trauma protocols.
