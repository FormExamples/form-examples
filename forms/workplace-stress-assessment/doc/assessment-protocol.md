# Assessment protocol — workplace stress

The assessment follows HSE's published five-step approach to managing
work-related stress, anchored on the six Management Standards and the
HSE Indicator Tool.

Authoritative source: HSE — *Tackling work-related stress using the
Management Standards approach* (WBK01).
<https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/wbk01.htm>

## HSE five steps

1. **Identify** the risk factors (use the Indicator Tool).
2. **Decide** who can be harmed and how (specific groups — managers,
   shift workers, lone workers, returning-to-work).
3. **Evaluate** the risks — explore problems with employees and develop
   solutions.
4. **Record** findings and develop and implement action plans.
5. **Monitor** and review.

The implementation operationalizes these five steps as the assessment
record's section structure.

## Indicator Tool

The HSE Indicator Tool is a 35-item validated questionnaire scored
1-5, mapped to the six Management Standards.

| Standard | Items in the tool |
| --- | --- |
| Demands | 8 items |
| Control | 6 items |
| Manager support | 5 items |
| Peer support | 4 items |
| Relationships | 4 items |
| Role | 5 items |
| Change | 3 items |

(Schedule per HSE Indicator Tool manual; manager support and peer
support are conventionally separated within the "Support" standard.)

Score interpretation against HSE benchmarks (from the Indicator Tool
user manual):

| Mean score range | Action |
| --- | --- |
| ≥ 4.0 (Demands reverse) / ≥ 4.0 others | doing very well; maintain |
| 3.5–3.9 | better than average; maintain |
| 3.0–3.4 | clear need for improvement |
| < 3.0 | urgent action required |

(The Indicator Tool manual is the authoritative source for exact
benchmark values; this table is illustrative of bands.)

## Cohort analysis

- Aggregate scores per Management Standard for the whole population
  and per cohort (team, location, role band).
- Minimum cohort size = 10 to preserve anonymity.
- Top-2-box and bottom-2-box percentages reported alongside means.
- Benchmark overlay against the HSE national benchmark dataset.

## Action plan

For each Management Standard scoring below benchmark, an action plan
record is opened, capturing:

- standard;
- problem statement (from employee discussion / focus group);
- proposed actions;
- named owner;
- target completion date;
- review date;
- expected outcome metric.

Action plans are persisted as linked Issues in the issue-tracker
model.

## Worker consultation

The HSE approach emphasizes that solutions must be co-designed with
workers, not imposed. The implementation persists:

- focus-group records with attendance and theme summaries;
- linked Meeting records (using the meeting form);
- consultation evidence under the Health and Safety (Consultation with
  Employees) Regulations 1996 (SI 1996/1513) or the Safety
  Representatives and Safety Committees Regulations 1977 (SI 1977/500).

## Individual stress risk assessment

Where an individual has been identified as at risk (e.g. through
sickness absence, a return-to-work meeting, or a self-referral), a
**personal stress risk assessment** is conducted, drawing on HSE Talking
Toolkit conversations:

- HSE — Talking Toolkit.
  <https://www.hse.gov.uk/stress/assets/docs/stress-talking-toolkit.pdf>

The personal assessment is treated as health data and is access-
restricted.

## Reasonable adjustments record

Where stress is associated with a disability under the Equality Act
2010, reasonable adjustments are recorded with:

- adjustment description;
- effective from / to dates;
- review date;
- owner;
- evidence of trial and impact.

## Monitor and review

Reviews are triggered by:

- annual cycle (minimum);
- significant change (organizational, role, premises);
- spikes in sickness absence;
- adverse engagement signals from the workplace-climate-assessment or
  employee-satisfaction-survey forms.
