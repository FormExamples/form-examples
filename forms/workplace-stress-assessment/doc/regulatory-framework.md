# Regulatory framework — workplace stress assessment

The Workplace Stress Assessment is the UK-domain instrument that
employers use to identify, assess, and act on psychosocial risk factors
that can lead to work-related stress, anxiety, and depression.

## Primary statute

- **Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974** (c. 37), s.2(1) — the
  general duty on employers extends to mental as well as physical
  health.
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1974/37/contents>
- **Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999**
  (SI 1999/3242), regulation 3 — duty to assess risks, including
  psychosocial risks.
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1999/3242/contents>
- **Equality Act 2010** (c. 15) — where stress-related illness amounts
  to a disability, the duty to make reasonable adjustments applies.
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/contents>

## HSE Management Standards for work-related stress

The **HSE Management Standards** are the published UK framework. They
identify **six primary sources of work-related stress** that, if not
managed properly, are associated with poor health, lower productivity,
and increased accident and sickness-absence rates:

1. **Demands** — workload, work patterns, work environment.
2. **Control** — how much say the person has in the way they do their
   work.
3. **Support** — encouragement, sponsorship, and resources provided by
   the organisation, line management, and colleagues.
4. **Relationships** — promoting positive working to avoid conflict and
   dealing with unacceptable behaviour.
5. **Role** — whether people understand their role within the
   organisation and whether the organisation ensures the person does
   not have conflicting roles.
6. **Change** — how organisational change is managed and communicated.

## Indicator Tool

The HSE **Indicator Tool** is a validated 35-item questionnaire that
maps to the six Management Standards. It produces a per-standard score
on a 1-5 scale, against benchmarks:

| Score band | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| ≥ Good (HSE benchmark) | doing very well — need to maintain performance |
| Good (better than average) | doing better than average; need to maintain |
| Below average / clear need | clear need for improvement |
| Urgent action needed | poor; urgent action required |

The benchmarks are published in the HSE Indicator Tool user manual.

Reference: HSE — Indicator Tool user manual.
<https://www.hse.gov.uk/stress/standards/pdfs/indicatortoolmanual.pdf>

## Related published scales (cross-references)

- **Job Content Questionnaire (JCQ)** — Karasek et al. 1998 (job-
  demand-control-support model).
- **Effort-Reward Imbalance (ERI)** scale — Siegrist 1996.
- **Copenhagen Psychosocial Questionnaire (COPSOQ III)** — Burr et al.
  2019.
- **Warwick-Edinburgh Mental Wellbeing Scale (WEMWBS)** — Tennant et
  al. 2007 (general wellbeing, not stress-specific).

This implementation uses the HSE Management Standards Indicator Tool
as the primary instrument because it is the UK regulator's published
methodology.

## ISO 45003

**ISO 45003:2021** "Occupational health and safety management —
Psychological health and safety at work — Guidelines for managing
psychosocial risks" sets out international guidance compatible with
HSE Management Standards.

- ISO 45003:2021. <https://www.iso.org/standard/64283.html>

## Reasonable adjustments

Where work-related stress is associated with a disability under
Equality Act 2010 (substantial and long-term adverse effect on
day-to-day activities), the employer must make reasonable adjustments
(ss.20-22). Adjustments may include workload changes, flexible
working, phased return, or changes to line management.

## Data protection

Workplace stress data is special-category data under UK GDPR Art. 9
(health data). Processing relies on:

- Art. 9(2)(b) — employment, social security, social protection
  obligations.
- Art. 9(2)(h) — provision of occupational medicine.
- DPA 2018 Sch.1 Pt.1 §1 (employment) and §2 (health and social care).
