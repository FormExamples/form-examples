# Psychiatry assessment protocol

This document describes the recommended interview structure, the mental
status examination procedure, and the use of corroborating sources.

## Interview structure

The 11 form steps follow the conventional ordering of a comprehensive
psychiatric assessment. Suggested time allocation for a 90-minute
initial assessment:

| Step                  | Approximate time |
| --------------------- | ----------------:|
| Demographics          |  5 min           |
| Presenting Complaint  | 15 min           |
| Psychiatric History   | 15 min           |
| Mental Status Exam    | 15 min           |
| Risk Assessment       | 10 min           |
| Mood & Anxiety        |  5 min           |
| Substance Use         |  5 min           |
| Current Medications   |  5 min           |
| Medical History       |  5 min           |
| Social History        |  5 min           |
| Capacity & Consent    |  5 min           |

The clinician adjusts allocation based on the presenting issue.

## Mental status examination — observation cues

The MSE is conducted throughout the interview. Specific cues to observe:

- **General appearance**: grooming, hygiene, dress appropriateness for
  weather and season, build, physical signs (tremor, scars, jaundice).
- **Behaviour**: rapport, eye contact, psychomotor activity, abnormal
  movements (tics, akathisia, parkinsonism, choreiform).
- **Speech**: rate (pressured, slowed), volume (loud, whispered),
  rhythm, fluency, prosody, articulation. Note any neologisms, perseveration,
  echolalia.
- **Mood**: ask "How are you feeling in yourself?" and use the patient's
  own words.
- **Affect**: range (full, restricted, blunted, flat), reactivity
  (responsive, fixed), intensity, congruence with mood and content,
  appropriateness.
- **Thought form**: linear, circumstantial, tangential, loose
  associations, flight of ideas, thought block, perseveration,
  derailment.
- **Thought content**: preoccupations, ruminations, obsessions, phobias,
  suicidal/homicidal ideation, delusions (persecutory, grandiose,
  jealous, somatic, religious, nihilistic, reference, control, thought
  insertion/broadcasting/withdrawal).
- **Perceptual abnormalities**: hallucinations (modality, command,
  third-person, second-person, running commentary, hypnagogic,
  hypnopompic), pseudohallucinations, illusions, derealization,
  depersonalization.
- **Cognition**: orientation, attention (digit span, serial 7s),
  short-term and long-term memory, abstract reasoning (similarities,
  proverbs), executive function (Frontal Assessment Battery).
- **Insight**: awareness of illness, attribution, agreement with
  proposed treatment.
- **Judgement**: practical scenarios (hypothetical and observed).

## Corroborating information

A psychiatric assessment must not rest on patient self-report alone. The
clinician should record information from:

- **Family / next of kin** — with the patient's consent unless capacity
  is impaired and consultation is in best interests.
- **GP records and letter** — past psychiatric history, medication
  history, physical health, blood and screening results.
- **Previous psychiatric records** — old notes, discharge summaries.
- **Care coordinator / care plan** — for established patients.
- **Multi-disciplinary team** — community mental health team, crisis
  team, liaison psychiatry, A&E records.
- **Section 12 doctor reports** for Mental Health Act assessments.

## Risk assessment principles

Captured in the Risk Assessment step. The clinician records:

- Risk to self — suicide, self-harm, self-neglect.
- Risk to others — violence, sexual offence, fire-setting, exploitation
  of others.
- Risk from others — abuse, exploitation, neglect.
- Risk to children / dependants.
- Risk in relation to reputation and finances.

NICE NG225 §1.3.2 — risk-stratification tools must not be used as
standalone decision tools; risk is a clinical formulation.

## Diagnostic formulation

At the end of the assessment, the clinician produces a five-part
formulation:

1. **Predisposing factors** — genetic, developmental, attachment,
   childhood adversity.
2. **Precipitating factors** — recent stressors, losses,
   substance use, medical illness.
3. **Perpetuating factors** — ongoing stressors, unhelpful
   beliefs, social isolation, dysfunctional family system.
4. **Protective factors** — supportive relationships, employment,
   engagement, insight.
5. **Provisional diagnosis** — ICD-11 and/or DSM-5-TR code(s).

## Plan and disposition

The Psychiatric Plan section records:

- Onward referral (CMHT, EIP, perinatal team, EUPD pathway, ED service,
  specialist clinic).
- Medication recommendations and rationale.
- Psychological therapy recommendations.
- Crisis plan and Wellness Recovery Action Plan (WRAP) where
  applicable.
- Physical health workup ordered.
- Follow-up timeframe.
- Communication with GP and patient.
