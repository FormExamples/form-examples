# Clinical guideline alignment — sundowner syndrome / BPSD

"Sundowner syndrome" (sundowning) is a descriptive clinical term for
late-day behavioural change in older people, most commonly in the
context of dementia. There is **no canonical guideline specific to
"sundowner syndrome" as a discrete entity**; the relevant authoritative
guidance is the literature on dementia care, behavioural and
psychological symptoms of dementia (BPSD), and delirium.

## NICE NG97 — Dementia: assessment, management and support

Source: National Institute for Health and Care Excellence.
Guideline ID: **NG97** (published June 2018).
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng97

NG97 §1.7 — Managing non-cognitive symptoms — recommends:

- **Initial non-pharmacological assessment** — explore reasons for
  behaviour, address pain, infection, hunger, thirst, overstimulation,
  understimulation, communication needs.
- **Personalised activities** — life-story-based, music, exercise,
  reminiscence.
- **Antipsychotics** — only after non-pharmacological strategies have been
  tried and only if the person is at risk of harm to self or others, or
  is severely distressed. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest
  time, with documented review every 6 weeks. Risperidone is the only UK-
  licensed antipsychotic for short-term (up to 6 weeks) management of
  persistent aggression in Alzheimer's dementia.

## NICE CG103 — Delirium: prevention, diagnosis and management

Source: NICE. Guideline ID: **CG103** (published July 2010; updated
March 2023 as NG223 in some scope).
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg103
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng223

Acute behavioural change in an older person must trigger delirium
workup:

- Identify and treat underlying causes (infection, dehydration,
  constipation, pain, medication, hypoxia, electrolyte imbalance).
- Use the 4AT or CAM (Confusion Assessment Method) to screen.
- Reorientate; ensure sensory aids; promote sleep hygiene.
- Avoid restraint and unnecessary moves.

## STOMP — Stopping over-medication in dementia

Although STOMP is principally aimed at people with a learning disability
or autism, the principle of avoiding inappropriate psychotropic
prescription applies equally in dementia care.

## The 2021 Banerjee NHS-commissioned review

Sube Banerjee's *The Use of Antipsychotic Medication for People with
Dementia: Time for Action* (2009) led to the NHS dementia action plan
and reduced antipsychotic prescription in care homes. The principles
remain relevant.
URL: search at https://www.gov.uk for the original review.

## Pharmacological cautions

- **Antipsychotics in dementia** — associated with increased risk of
  stroke and mortality (FDA black-box warning, MHRA Drug Safety Update).
  Use only when clinically essential and review every 6 weeks.
- **Antipsychotics in Lewy body dementia** — severe neuroleptic
  sensitivity in approximately 50%; avoid where possible.
- **Anticholinergic medication** — worsens cognition and may precipitate
  delirium. Review the patient's total anticholinergic burden (e.g.
  ACB scale).
- **Benzodiazepines** — increase fall risk, confusion, and dependence.
- **Trazodone, melatonin, mirtazapine** — sometimes used for night-time
  agitation; limited evidence.

## Environmental and non-pharmacological interventions

Recommended for first-line management of sundowning (NG97 §1.7):

- Light therapy in the afternoon (small RCT evidence base).
- Structured daytime activity and exercise.
- Limit afternoon naps.
- Reduce overstimulation at the symptom peak.
- Ensure adequate hydration and toileting.
- Provide reassurance, validation, redirection (not contradiction).
- Address sensory deficits (hearing aids, glasses).
- Music and reminiscence therapy.
