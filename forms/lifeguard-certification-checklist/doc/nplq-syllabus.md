# National Pool Lifeguard Qualification (NPLQ) Syllabus

This form implements the competency verification for pool and beach
lifeguards aligned with the Royal Life Saving Society UK (RLSS UK)
National Pool Lifeguard Qualification (NPLQ) and the International Life
Saving Federation (ILSF) competency framework.

## Step → syllabus alignment

| Form step | NPLQ syllabus unit |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Candidate Details | Identity and prerequisite checks (≥16 years; medical declaration) |
| 2. Physical Fitness & Swim | NPLQ Unit 1 — *Lifeguarding Theory*; timed 50 m swim in ≤60 s; jump-in then 100 m swim in ≤2 min 30 s; surface dive |
| 3. Supervision, Scanning & Zoning | NPLQ Unit 1 — *Pool supervision and surveillance*; 10/20 scanning rule; zone validity |
| 4. Conscious Casualty Rescue | NPLQ Unit 2 — *Intervention and rescue*; reach, throw, wade, swim rescue with float |
| 5. Unconscious Casualty Rescue | NPLQ Unit 2 — *Unconscious casualty extraction*; safe entry; tow; deep-water support |
| 6. Spinal Injury Management | NPLQ Unit 2 — *Suspected spinal injury management*; head-splint or vice-grip; spinal board extraction |
| 7. CPR & AED | NPLQ Unit 3 — *Cardiopulmonary resuscitation and AED* (aligned with RCUK 2021 BLS) |
| 8. First Aid & Oxygen Therapy | NPLQ Unit 3 — *First aid for the pool environment*; supplemental oxygen via constant-flow or demand-valve device |
| 9. Legal, Regulatory & Incident Reporting | HSG179 duties; RIDDOR; data protection |
| 10. Overall Result, Feedback & Signoff | Examiner signoff; certificate issuance |

## Critical competencies

The following are critical-fail items per the NPLQ specification — any
single failure means the candidate cannot pass even if all other items
are met:

1. **Timed swim** — 50 m in ≤60 s, or 100 m within 2 min 30 s including
   surface dive.
2. **Unconscious casualty rescue** — safe entry, controlled tow, safe
   extraction from deep water.
3. **Suspected spinal injury management** — correct head splint or
   vice-grip immobilization throughout.
4. **CPR** — compressions to RCUK depth and rate, with effective
   ventilations.
5. **AED delivery** — safe shock delivery without rescuer contact.
6. **Scanning effectiveness** — passes the 10/20 scanning rule test in
   the assessment scenario.

## 10/20 scanning rule

The most-cited surveillance benchmark in pool lifeguarding:

- **10 seconds** to detect a distressed swimmer.
- **20 seconds** to reach the casualty.

Source: RLSS UK pool surveillance guidance, derived from the early-2000s
research consolidated in HSG179 and ILSF position statements.

## Drowning sequence (recognition)

Drowning casualties follow a recognisable behaviour pattern documented by
Pia in 1971 and now standardized by ILSF:

1. Surprise — splashing or thrashing.
2. Instinctive drowning response — vertical body position, head tilted
   back, arms extended laterally.
3. Silent — drowning casualties cannot call for help.
4. Submersion — 20-60 seconds from instinctive response to submersion.

Reference: Pia F. *Observations on the drowning of non-swimmers.* Journal
of Physical Education. 1971;68:99-104. Standardized drowning definition
in: van Beeck EF, Branche CM, Szpilman D, Modell JH, Bierens JJ. *A new
definition of drowning: towards documentation and prevention of a global
public health problem.* Bull World Health Organ. 2005;83(11):853-6.
PMID: 16302042.
