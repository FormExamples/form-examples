# Assessment Protocol — Lifeguard Certification

This document describes how an RLSS UK NPLQ assessor (or international
equivalent) runs and scores each of the 10 form steps.

## Outcome categories

- **Pass** — all critical and non-critical competencies demonstrated;
  certificate valid for 24 months from the date of pass (NPLQ standard).
- **Needs Development** — minor deficiency in a non-critical competency;
  retraining and reassessment within 4 weeks; certificate withheld until
  reassessment.
- **Fail** — any critical-competency failure (timed swim, unconscious
  rescue, spinal handling, CPR, AED, scanning); full requalification
  course required.

## Step 2 — Physical Fitness and Swim Competency

NPLQ entry and assessment swim requirements (RLSS UK NPLQ 9th edition):

| Skill | Standard |
| --- | --- |
| 50 m swim | ≤60 seconds, any stroke other than backstroke; head above water for breathing acceptable |
| 100 m swim with surface dive | ≤2 min 30 s including a deep surface dive to recover a manikin or brick from ≥1.5 m |
| Tread water | 2 minutes without aids |
| Underwater swim | 10 m underwater |
| Manikin tow | 25 m surface tow of a manikin in water |

US Red Cross prerequisite swim (for comparison): 300 yards (≈275 m)
continuous swim, 2-minute tread water using legs only, timed recovery of
a 10 lb brick from a depth of 7-10 ft within 1 min 40 s.

## Step 3 — Supervision, Scanning and Zoning

The candidate is observed during a 20-30 minute pool scan in a simulated
or real surveillance scenario. The assessor measures:

- **Detection time**: when the planted distressed swimmer is identified
  (must be ≤10 seconds per RLSS UK 10/20 rule).
- **Reach time**: time from detection to first physical contact with
  the casualty (must be ≤20 seconds).
- **Scanning pattern**: sweep covers all four corners of the zone;
  candidate moves position; head movement evident.
- **Zone validity**: candidate maintains zone integrity and does not
  abandon supervision without handover.

## Step 4 — Conscious-casualty rescue

The candidate must demonstrate the rescue hierarchy:

1. **Reach** — pole, shepherd's crook, towel.
2. **Throw** — throwing aid (rope bag, ring buoy).
3. **Wade** — only in shallow, safe water, with a buoyant aid carried.
4. **Swim** — only when no other option; always with a buoyant aid
   between rescuer and casualty.

Pass criteria: rescuer maintains personal safety; casualty contact only
through aid; controlled tow to side; casualty exited safely.

## Step 5 — Unconscious-casualty rescue

The candidate must:

1. Recognise unconscious casualty (no response, abnormal breathing,
   submerged).
2. Enter safely — slide-in or step-in (never head-first in unknown
   depth).
3. Approach and turn casualty face-up.
4. Apply rescue tow (extended arm tow or vice-grip).
5. Maintain airway above water during tow.
6. Reach exit; transition to deep-water support or spinal-suspected
   extraction.
7. Extract or call for second lifeguard to assist with extraction.

Critical-fail conditions: failure to maintain airway above water;
unsafe entry; loss of casualty during tow; failure to call for help.

## Step 6 — Spinal injury management

Recognition: any diving incident, witnessed head-first impact, casualty
found in shallow water near the side, or casualty reporting neck pain
after impact.

Demonstrated technique:

1. Maintain manual in-line stabilisation (head splint or vice grip).
2. Float casualty to spinal board or extraction point.
3. Roll onto board with at least 3 rescuers if possible.
4. Secure head blocks and straps in order: chest → pelvis → legs → head.
5. Extract from water with team lift.

Critical-fail conditions: loss of head control at any point; rolling
without team co-ordination; head strap before body straps.

## Step 7 — CPR and AED

Aligned with the *cardiopulmonary-resuscitation-training* form. Pass
criteria are identical to RCUK 2021 Adult BLS:

- Rate 100-120/min
- Depth 5-6 cm
- Full recoil
- 30:2 ratio
- Visible chest rise on ventilation
- Safe AED delivery (no rescuer in contact during shock)
- Compressions resumed within 10 seconds of shock

## Step 8 — First aid and oxygen therapy

NPLQ Unit 3 first-aid topics:

- Drowning casualty post-rescue management (consider hospital referral
  even when apparently recovered — secondary drowning risk).
- Hypothermia and immersion injury.
- Hyperventilation; shallow-water blackout.
- Bleeding (especially scalp lacerations).
- Asthma and anaphylaxis.
- Spinal precautions out of water.
- Oxygen therapy: 15 L/min via non-rebreather mask for unconscious or
  significantly hypoxic casualty.

## Step 9 — Legal, regulatory and incident reporting

- HSE *HSG179* duties for the pool operator and lifeguard team.
- PWTAG Code of Practice — water quality and emergency-action plan
  knowledge.
- RIDDOR-reportable incidents:
  <https://www.hse.gov.uk/riddor/reportable-incidents.htm>
- Pool incident log entry.
- Safeguarding referral pathway for incidents involving children or
  vulnerable adults (HSE & RLSS UK aligned with the Children Act 1989
  and Care Act 2014).

## Step 10 — Overall result, feedback and signoff

- Assessor records each step's pass/fail status.
- Feedback delivered verbally and in writing.
- Certificate issued via the awarding body (RLSS UK or partner) on
  successful completion.
- Re-validation: NPLQ requires periodic monthly competence checks at
  the workplace; certificate renewed every 24 months.
