# Assessment Protocol — First Aid at Work Verification

This document describes how an assessor verifies each of the 10 form
steps in a structured workplace First Aid at Work (FAW) competency check.
The protocol follows HSE L74 and the Resuscitation Council UK 2021
guidelines.

## Outcome categories

- **Pass** — every required skill demonstrated to the competent standard;
  no life-saving deficiency; certificate valid for 3 years.
- **Needs Development** — minor deficiency in a non-life-saving topic;
  targeted retraining and reassessment within 4 weeks; certificate
  withheld until reassessment.
- **Fail** — critical deficiency in a life-saving skill (primary survey,
  CPR, AED, choking, anaphylaxis, severe bleeding); full requalification
  course (minimum 18 hours) required.

## Primary survey (DRABC)

The candidate must demonstrate the survey in sequence and verbalise each
step.

| Letter | Action | Pass criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D | Danger | Make scene safe before approaching; remove or remove yourself from the hazard |
| R | Response | Shout, shake shoulders gently; AVPU level documented |
| A | Airway | Head-tilt chin-lift (or jaw thrust if spinal injury suspected) |
| B | Breathing | Look, listen, feel for normal breathing for no more than 10 seconds |
| C | Circulation | Major bleeding identified and controlled before further care |

Failure to make the scene safe, failure to open the airway, or failure to
recognise absent or abnormal breathing each constitutes a critical
deficiency.

## CPR and AED

The candidate must perform a minimum 2-minute CPR cycle on a manikin
that measures compression rate and depth.

Resuscitation Council UK adult BLS pass criteria:

- **Compression rate**: 100-120 per minute
- **Compression depth**: 5-6 cm (one third of chest depth)
- **Recoil**: full chest recoil between compressions
- **Compression:ventilation ratio**: 30:2 with two effective rescue breaths
- **Hand position**: lower half of sternum
- **Interruptions**: minimised; <10 seconds between cycles

AED operation:

- Switch on AED before exposing the chest if helper available
- Attach pads in correct positions; do not delay shock for clothing
- Ensure no one is in contact during analysis and shock delivery
- Resume compressions immediately after the shock (or "no shock advised")

## Choking management

Adult and child sequence (RCUK):

1. Encourage coughing if mild airway obstruction.
2. If severe — up to 5 back blows between the shoulder blades.
3. If unresolved — up to 5 abdominal thrusts (chest thrusts for infants
   or pregnant patients).
4. Alternate cycles of 5 back blows and 5 thrusts.
5. If unresponsive — call 999, lower to ground, start CPR.

## Severe bleeding

Catastrophic-haemorrhage control sequence (in order):

1. Direct pressure with sterile dressing.
2. Apply additional dressings over the first; do not remove the first.
3. Elevation only if no suspected fracture.
4. If life-threatening limb bleed unresponsive to pressure — tourniquet
   (commercial CAT-style preferred; improvised acceptable only if no
   commercial device available). Record time of application.
5. Wound packing for junctional bleeds where tourniquet is not feasible.

## Burns and scalds

- Cool the burn under cool running water for at least **20 minutes**
  (NICE CG3 / RCUK / British Burn Association consensus).
- Cover with clingfilm (lengthwise, not circumferential) or a sterile
  non-adherent dressing.
- Do not apply ice, butter, oils, or toothpaste.
- Refer to hospital for: full-thickness burns; partial-thickness >5 % BSA
  in adults or any in children; burns to face, hands, feet, genitalia,
  major joint; circumferential burns; chemical or electrical burns;
  inhalation injury.

## Fractures, sprains, and spinal injury

- Fracture — immobilise with splint or sling in position found; do not
  attempt reduction.
- Suspected spinal injury — manual in-line stabilisation; do not move
  unless required for ABC.
- Sprain — RICE (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation) for first 48 hours.

## Medical emergencies

| Condition | Recognition | First-aid action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stroke | FAST: Face droop, Arm weakness, Speech slurred, Time to call 999 | Call 999; keep patient still; do not give food or drink |
| Seizure | Tonic-clonic activity, post-ictal | Protect head; do not restrain; do not put anything in mouth; time the seizure; recovery position when finished |
| Anaphylaxis | Sudden onset, airway/breathing/circulation compromise, skin signs | Call 999; lie flat with legs raised (or sit up if breathing difficulty); administer adrenaline auto-injector if prescribed; second dose after 5 minutes if no improvement |
| Asthma | Wheeze, breathlessness, accessory muscle use | Sit upright; reliever inhaler (10 puffs via spacer if available); call 999 if no improvement |
| Heart attack | Central crushing chest pain, sweating, nausea | Call 999; sit comfortably; 300 mg aspirin (chew) if not contraindicated; no food or drink |
| Hypoglycaemia | Pallor, sweating, confusion, behavioural change in a known diabetic | If conscious — 15-20 g fast-acting glucose; recheck after 15 minutes; if unconscious — recovery position, call 999 |

## Recording, reporting, and handover

- **Workplace first-aid record** — date, time, patient name, injury,
  first aid given, first aider's name (HSE recommended; not statutory).
- **RIDDOR** — Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences
  Regulations 2013 (SI 2013/1471). Reportable to HSE within 10 days for
  certain categories of injury, or within 15 days for over-7-day
  incapacitation.
  <https://www.hse.gov.uk/riddor/>
- **Handover to ambulance** — use the SBAR or ATMIST mnemonic.
