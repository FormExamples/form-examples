# First Aid at Work — UK HSE Framework

This form implements the workplace First Aid at Work (FAW) competency
verification used by UK employers to demonstrate compliance with the
Health and Safety (First-Aid) Regulations 1981.

## Statutory background

Regulation 3(1) of SI 1981/917 requires every employer to provide
"adequate and appropriate equipment, facilities and personnel to enable
first aid to be rendered to employees if they are injured or become ill
at work." Since the October 2013 deregulation, HSE no longer approves
first-aid training providers, but it sets out the syllabus expectations in
the L74 ACOP and INDG214 guidance.

The two recognised qualifications under L74 are:

- **Emergency First Aid at Work (EFAW)** — minimum 6 hours of contact
  training; suitable for low-hazard workplaces.
- **First Aid at Work (FAW)** — minimum 18 hours over 3 days; required
  for higher-hazard workplaces or larger workforces.

Both qualifications are valid for 3 years; a 3-yearly requalification
course is required.

## Awarding-organisation framework

The certificate is awarded by an Ofqual-regulated awarding organisation
(or SQA in Scotland; Qualifications Wales in Wales; CCEA in Northern
Ireland) using a recognised RQF (Regulated Qualifications Framework) unit:

- *Level 3 Award in First Aid at Work* — RQF code varies by awarding body
  (e.g. Highfield, Qualsafe, NUCO, Pearson Edexcel).
- Search the Ofqual Register of Regulated Qualifications:
  <https://register.ofqual.gov.uk/>

## Syllabus alignment

The L74 ACOP and HSE guidance describe the topics a competent first
aider must be able to manage. The form's 10 steps map directly to those
topics.

| Form step | HSE / L74 syllabus topic |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Trainee Details | Trainee identification and qualification level (EFAW / FAW) |
| 2. Scene Assessment & Safety | Assessing situation and circumstances safely; managing an incident |
| 3. Primary Survey (DRABC) | Danger, Response, Airway, Breathing, Circulation |
| 4. CPR & AED | Adult CPR and automated external defibrillation |
| 5. Choking Management | Foreign-body airway obstruction in adults |
| 6. Bleeding & Wound Care | Severe bleeding control, minor cuts, embedded objects |
| 7. Burns & Scalds | Thermal, chemical, and electrical burn first aid |
| 8. Fractures, Sprains & Spinal Injury | Suspected fracture, sprain/strain, suspected spinal injury |
| 9. Medical Emergencies | Stroke (FAST), seizure, anaphylaxis, asthma, heart attack, diabetes |
| 10. Recording, Reporting & Handover | First-aid record book; RIDDOR-reportable incidents |

DRABC is the UK survey acronym; the equivalent international BLS sequence
used by the Resuscitation Council UK (RCUK) is "Safety — Response —
Shout for help — Open airway — Check breathing — Call 999 — Start CPR".

## Resuscitation Council UK alignment

The CPR and AED component (step 4) must match the current Resuscitation
Council UK Adult Basic Life Support algorithm:

- Compression depth: 5-6 cm
- Compression rate: 100-120 per minute
- Compression:ventilation ratio: 30:2 (untrained-rescuer compression-only
  CPR is acceptable)
- Minimise interruptions; full chest recoil

Source: Resuscitation Council UK, *Adult Basic Life Support Guidelines*
(2021): <https://www.resus.org.uk/library/2021-resuscitation-guidelines/adult-basic-life-support-guidelines>

## St John Ambulance syllabus

St John Ambulance is the largest single FAW training provider in the UK.
Their syllabus mirrors the L74 topics and adds the FAST stroke mnemonic
and the use of an adrenaline auto-injector for anaphylaxis. Reference:

- St John Ambulance — *First Aid at Work* training overview:
  <https://www.sja.org.uk/courses/first-aid-at-work/>
- St John Ambulance — *First Aid Manual*, 11th edition (DK, 2021;
  endorsed by St John Ambulance, St Andrew's First Aid, and the British
  Red Cross): <https://www.dk.com/uk/book/9780241382318-first-aid-manual/>

## British Red Cross syllabus

The British Red Cross is also an Ofqual-recognised FAW provider. Syllabus
reference: <https://www.redcross.org.uk/first-aid/learn-first-aid>

## Versioning note

L74 was last revised in 2018; the Resuscitation Council UK guidelines
were updated in 2021 (with minor revisions tracked at
<https://www.resus.org.uk/library/2021-resuscitation-guidelines>).
Implementations should record the L74 revision and RCUK guideline year
in effect at the time of each assessment.
