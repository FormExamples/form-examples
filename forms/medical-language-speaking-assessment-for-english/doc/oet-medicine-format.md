# OET Speaking sub-test (Medicine profession)

The Occupational English Test (OET) is the English-language proficiency
test for healthcare professionals recognised by the UK General Medical
Council (GMC), the UK Nursing and Midwifery Council (NMC), and equivalent
regulators in Australia, Ireland, New Zealand, Singapore, and Dubai.

- OET. *Test overview.* <https://www.occupationalenglishtest.org/test-information/>
- OET. *Speaking sub-test.* <https://www.occupationalenglishtest.org/speaking-test/>
- OET. *Speaking criteria.* <https://www.occupationalenglishtest.org/speaking-criteria/>

## Speaking sub-test format

- Duration: approximately 20 minutes.
- Two role-plays with a professional interlocutor playing the patient
  (or in some scenarios, a carer or relative).
- Each role-play is preceded by a 2–3 minute card-reading and
  preparation period.

The two role-plays in this form mirror the OET structure exactly:

1. *Patient Interview* — opening consultation / history taking.
2. *Clinical Explanation* — explaining a diagnosis, management plan,
   or procedure.

## Scoring — linguistic criteria

OET Speaking is double-marked on **nine criteria** comprising four
*Linguistic* and five *Clinical Communication* criteria. Each is
banded 0–6 with the band descriptors published by OET.

### Linguistic criteria (four)

| Criterion | Range | What it measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intelligibility | 0–6 | Pronunciation, stress, intonation, comprehensibility |
| Fluency | 0–6 | Pace, hesitation, repair, sustained speech |
| Appropriateness of Language | 0–6 | Register, professional vocabulary, tone, politeness |
| Resources of Grammar and Expression | 0–6 | Range and accuracy of grammar; lexical range |

### Clinical Communication criteria (five)

| Criterion | Range | What it measures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relationship-building | 0–3 | Initiating, demonstrating empathy, non-judgemental |
| Understanding patient's perspective | 0–3 | Eliciting and acknowledging ideas, concerns, expectations |
| Providing structure | 0–3 | Sequencing, signposting, summarising |
| Information-gathering | 0–3 | Open / closed questioning, screening, clarifying |
| Information-giving | 0–3 | Chunking, checking understanding, plain language |

## Grade mapping

OET converts the raw scores into a letter grade with the published
mapping (current at time of writing — verify against the OET site):

| Grade | Numeric range | Equivalence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | 450–500 | High-level professional proficiency |
| B | 350–440 | Good professional proficiency |
| C+ | 300–340 | Functional proficiency |
| C | 200–290 | Lower-functional proficiency |
| D | 100–190 | Limited |
| E | 0–90 | Beginner |

## UK regulator equivalence

- UK General Medical Council. *Evidence of your knowledge of English.*
  <https://www.gmc-uk.org/registration-and-licensing/join-the-register/before-you-apply/evidence-of-english-language-skills>
- UK Nursing and Midwifery Council. *English language requirements.*
  <https://www.nmc.org.uk/registration/joining-the-register/trained-outside-the-eueea/english-language-requirements/>

Both GMC and NMC currently accept **Grade B** as the minimum
acceptable OET Speaking sub-test score.
