# Safety case notes — substance use disorder assessment

Substance use disorders carry acute medical risk (withdrawal, overdose),
chronic medical risk (organ damage, infectious disease), and
psychosocial risk (mental health, safeguarding). The following safety
considerations apply.

## Acute withdrawal risk

Some substance withdrawal syndromes are medical emergencies. The
Withdrawal Assessment step prompts the clinician to assess:

| Substance class      | Withdrawal danger                                    | Action |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------ |
| Alcohol              | Seizures, delirium tremens (mortality up to 5%)      | CIWA-Ar; inpatient detox if CIWA > 15, prior DTs/seizures, severe comorbidity; thiamine before glucose |
| Benzodiazepines      | Seizures, delirium                                   | Cautious tapered withdrawal; inpatient if high-dose, long-term, or comorbid |
| Opioids              | Severe distress; rarely fatal but dangerous in pregnancy and in the very ill | COWS; symptomatic management (lofexidine, antiemetics); consider OST |
| Stimulants           | Crash, severe depression, suicidality                | Monitor for suicide risk; supportive care |
| GHB / GBL            | Seizures, delirium                                   | Inpatient detox with benzodiazepines |
| Synthetic cannabinoids | Variable                                           | Symptomatic; monitor cardiovascular and renal function |

## Wernicke's encephalopathy prophylaxis

All patients with suspected alcohol dependence should receive parenteral
**thiamine** (Pabrinex) before any carbohydrate is administered.
Wernicke's encephalopathy presents with the classic triad (ophthalmoplegia,
ataxia, confusion) but is often incomplete; treat empirically.

NICE CG100 recommends:

- Intravenous Pabrinex 2 pairs t.d.s. for 3 days in suspected Wernicke's.
- 1 pair daily for 5 days as prophylaxis in alcohol dependence.

## Overdose risk

Overdose is the leading cause of death in opioid use disorder. The
Substance Use History step captures:

- Recent loss of tolerance (post-detox, post-prison release, post-acute
  illness).
- Polysubstance use (especially opioids + benzodiazepines + alcohol).
- High-potency opioids (heroin, fentanyl analogues, nitazenes).
- Route of administration (intravenous, smoked, intranasal).
- Use alone (no naloxone, no one to respond).

Naloxone (Prenoxad / Nyxoid) should be prescribed for all patients at
risk; carriers should include patient, family, and frontline workers.

## Hepatitis and HIV risk

For injecting drug users:

- Hepatitis B vaccination if non-immune (rapid schedule).
- Hepatitis C testing and treatment (direct-acting antivirals; achievable
  cure).
- HIV testing.
- Tuberculosis screening if homeless or recent travel.

## Pregnancy and substance use

Pregnancy is a critical safety context:

- Alcohol — no safe lower limit; risk of fetal alcohol spectrum disorder.
- Opioids — methadone or buprenorphine maintenance is preferred over
  detoxification because of overdose and miscarriage risk.
- Benzodiazepines — abrupt withdrawal is dangerous to fetus.
- Cocaine, stimulants — placental abruption, preterm birth.

Refer to specialist substance-use-in-pregnancy team.

## Mental health comorbidity (dual diagnosis)

NICE NG58 covers coexisting severe mental illness and substance misuse.
Suicide risk is markedly elevated. The Mental Health Comorbidities step
captures:

- PHQ-9 / GAD-7 screen.
- Suicidality.
- Psychotic symptoms (stimulant-induced or independent).
- Personality disorder.
- Trauma history (often unaddressed).

## Safeguarding

- **Children** — parental substance use is a recognised safeguarding
  trigger. Direct enquiry about children in the household and care
  arrangements.
- **Domestic abuse** — substance use intersects with intimate-partner
  violence; direct enquiry.
- **Modern slavery / county-lines exploitation** — particularly for
  young people with cannabis or stimulant use.
- **Adult safeguarding** — Care Act 2014 duty.

## Driving

DVLA guidance: dependence on alcohol or drugs requires the patient to
notify DVLA, and licence revocation is likely until evidence of
sustained recovery. The form prompts the clinician to discuss this with
the patient. See https://www.gov.uk/health-conditions-and-driving

## Crisis pathway

- **Overdose suspected** — 999; supportive care; naloxone for opioid
  overdose.
- **Acute withdrawal** — assess CIWA-Ar / COWS; consider inpatient
  admission.
- **Acute psychosis** — psychiatric liaison; consider Mental Health Act
  if risk and lack of capacity.
- **Suicidality** — same-day Crisis Team review; consider Mental Health
  Act.

## Confidentiality

The clinician should discuss limits to confidentiality at the start of
the assessment:

- Safeguarding duties take precedence.
- Driving fitness reporting (GMC duty if patient continues to drive
  unfit).
- Disclosure to legal authorities only in specific defined circumstances.
- Routine sharing with GP (with patient's consent).
