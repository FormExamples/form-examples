# Knee replacement surgery evaluation

A knee replacement surgery evaluation is a multi-step medical assessment conducted by an orthopaedic surgeon to determine if a patient is a suitable candidate for total or partial knee arthroplasty. It weighs the severity of joint degradation against conservative treatment outcomes and overall surgical readiness. [1, 2, 3]

## 1. Orthopaedic Consultation & Medical History

The process begins with an in-depth clinical consultation. The surgeon will review: [1, 4]

- Pain Characteristics: Severity, duration, impact on sleep, and location.
- Functional Limitations: Ability to walk, climb stairs, and sit or stand comfortably.
- Prior Treatments: Success or failure of physiotherapy, steroid injections, weight loss, and NSAIDs.
- General Health: Underlying conditions like diabetes, heart disease, or bleeding disorders that affect surgical risk. [3, 5, 6, 7, 8]

## 2. Physical Examination

The specialist physically assesses the knee joint to determine structural compromise. [4, 9, 10]

- Range of Motion: Testing how far the knee can bend and straighten.
- Stability & Alignment: Checking for knock-knees, bow-leggedness, and ligament laxity.
- Muscle Strength: Testing the quadriceps and surrounding musculature.
- Joint Tracking: Observing the movement and positioning of the kneecap. [4, 6, 11, 12, 13]

## 3. Diagnostic Imaging

Radiology provides objective structural data to plan the surgical approach: [1, 2, 14]

- Weight-Bearing X-rays: The primary tool used to measure joint space narrowing and bone spurs.
- MRI Scan: Used selectively to evaluate soft tissues, ligaments, or specific bone defects.
- CT Scan: Frequently required if mapping out a robotic-assisted knee replacement. [1, 15, 16, 17, 18]

## 4. General Pre-Operative Clearing

Once surgery is recommended, a systemic medical evaluation ensures safe anesthesia administration: [2]

- Blood Work: Complete blood count (CBC), kidney function, and clotting profiles.
- Electrocardiogram (ECG): Assessment of heart health before undergoing anaesthesia.
- Urinalysis: Screening for active infections that could compromise a sterile implant environment. [1, 6, 19, 20, 21]

## Evaluation Outcomes & Next Steps

Following the assessment, your team will offer a treatment recommendation. If severe damage is present, a total knee replacement (TKR) or partial knee replacement (PKR) is scheduled. Alternatively, non-surgical management may be optimized.
