# Scoring Rules — Ergonomic Assessment (REBA)

## Rapid Entire Body Assessment (REBA)

The primary instrument is REBA, a postural-loading screening tool for
whole-body work-related musculoskeletal risk.

- Canonical reference: Hignett S, McAtamney L. *Rapid Entire Body
  Assessment (REBA).* Appl Ergon. 2000;31(2):201-205.
  DOI: 10.1016/S0003-6870(99)00039-3. PMID: 10711982.

### Scoring procedure

REBA has two segment groups, each scored from a table:

- Group A: trunk, neck, legs
- Group B: upper arm, lower arm, wrist

Each group total is combined with a force / load adjustment (Group A)
and a coupling adjustment (Group B). The combined score is then combined
with an activity score (static / repetitive / unstable) to produce the
final REBA score (1–15).

### Action levels (Hignett & McAtamney 2000)

| Final score | Risk level | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Negligible | None necessary |
| 2–3 | Low | Change may be needed |
| 4–7 | Medium | Further investigation, change soon |
| 8–10 | High | Investigate and implement change |
| 11–15 | Very high | Implement change immediately |

The form uses these action levels verbatim.

## Composite mapping

The form's composite category is the worst-case of:

- REBA action level
- MAC red / amber / green
- ART tool red / amber / green (if used)
- Symptom severity (step 6) on a NRS

Any "very high" REBA escalates immediately; symptoms with sickness
absence escalate one band.
