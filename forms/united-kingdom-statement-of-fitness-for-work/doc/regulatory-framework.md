# Regulatory framework — Statement of Fitness for Work (Med 3 / "fit note")

The Statement of Fitness for Work (commonly the **fit note**, on form
**Med 3**) is the statutory medical statement issued in the United
Kingdom to certify that a patient has, or has had, an illness or injury
that may affect their ability to work. It is used by employers, the
Department for Work and Pensions (DWP), and other benefit-paying bodies.

## Primary statute

- **Social Security Contributions and Benefits Act 1992** (c. 4), Part
  XI (statutory sick pay) and section 171ZA-B and Part II (employment
  and support allowance).
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1992/4/contents>
- **Social Security (Medical Evidence) Regulations 1976** (SI 1976/615)
  — the foundational regulations specifying that a statement of
  fitness for work is the prescribed medical evidence for SSP and
  benefit claims; amended in 2010 to introduce "may be fit for work"
  and in 2022 to expand the list of authorised issuers.
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1976/615/contents>
- **Statutory Sick Pay (Medical Evidence) Regulations 1985** (SI 1985/
  1604). <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1985/1604/contents>

## 2022 expansion of issuers

The Social Security (Medical Evidence) and Statutory Sick Pay (Medical
Evidence) (Amendment) Regulations 2022 (SI 2022/630) expanded the list
of healthcare professionals authorised to issue a Med 3 beyond doctors
to include:

- Registered nurses (NMC).
- Occupational therapists (HCPC).
- Pharmacists (GPhC).
- Physiotherapists (HCPC).

Source: <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2022/630/contents>.

## The two outcomes

A Med 3 fit note has exactly two outcomes:

1. **Not fit for work** — the patient cannot do their normal job; this
   acts as evidence for SSP, ESA, UC, and similar benefits.
2. **May be fit for work** — the patient may be able to return to work
   with workplace adjustments. The healthcare professional may tick one
   or more of the four standard suggestions:
   - a phased return to work;
   - amended duties;
   - altered hours;
   - workplace adaptations.

The healthcare professional cannot certify "fit for work" — there is no
such tick-box. When the patient is fit, no Med 3 is needed.

## Maximum duration

- For the **first six months** of an episode, a Med 3 may not be issued
  for more than **three months** at a time.
- After six months, a Med 3 may be issued "for any clinically
  appropriate period".
- Source: SI 1976/615 as amended; DWP fit-note guidance for healthcare
  professionals.

## Self-certification (SC2)

For sickness absences of **seven calendar days or fewer**, no Med 3 is
required; the employee uses self-certification (form **SC2** or the
employer's equivalent). The Med 3 is required from day 8 onwards.

- HMRC SC2 self-certification: <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/statutory-sick-pay-employees-statement-of-sickness-sc2>
