# Regulatory Framework

The Consent to Treatment form records a patient's informed consent to a
medical procedure. In England, Wales, and Northern Ireland, valid consent
is defined by the common-law of negligence (as restated by the Supreme
Court in *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board*), the Mental Capacity
Act 2005, and the professional standards of the General Medical Council.

## Three elements of valid consent

The form is structured around the three elements that case law and
professional guidance require:

| Element | Source |
| --- | --- |
| **Capacity** — the patient understands, retains, weighs, and communicates the decision | Mental Capacity Act 2005, ss.1–3 |
| **Information** — the patient is told of the nature of the procedure, material risks, reasonable alternatives, and benefits | *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11 |
| **Voluntariness** — the consent is given freely, without coercion or undue influence | Common law; GMC *Decision Making and Consent* (2020) |

  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/section/1>
- Section 3 — inability to make decisions —
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/section/3>
- Mental Capacity Act 2005 Code of Practice (2007), Chapter 4 (assessing

Where the patient lacks capacity to consent, the form does not capture
consent; instead the procedure proceeds (or does not) under a best-
interests decision under s.4 MCA 2005, supplemented by any ADRT or LPA HW.

## Case law — *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board*

The leading modern authority on disclosure of risk and the patient's
right to be informed of material risks and reasonable alternatives:

- *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11 —
  <https://www.supremecourt.uk/cases/uksc-2013-0136.html>

Key holding (paragraph 87): "An adult person of sound mind is entitled to
decide which, if any, of the available forms of treatment to undergo, and
her consent must be obtained before treatment interfering with her bodily
integrity is undertaken. The doctor is therefore under a duty to take
reasonable care to ensure that the patient is aware of any material risks
involved in any recommended treatment, and of any reasonable alternative
or variant treatments."

Materiality test (paragraph 87): "a reasonable person in the patient's
position would be likely to attach significance to the risk, or the
doctor is or should reasonably be aware that the particular patient would
be likely to attach significance to it."

The 2020 GMC guidance organizes consent around seven decision-making
principles, all of which are reflected in the form's eight wizard steps.

## Special category data — UK GDPR

The form processes UK GDPR Article 9 special-category data. Lawful basis:

- UK GDPR Article 6(1)(e) — task in the public interest.
- UK GDPR Article 9(2)(h) — provision of health care.

The form's signed consent record is **not** the UK GDPR Article 9(2)(a)
basis (explicit consent for data processing); it is the common-law
consent to the treatment itself.

ICO guidance on consent vs. GDPR consent:
<https://ico.org.uk/for-organizations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/consent/>

## Children and young people

For patients under 18, the form's capacity declaration follows:

- Children aged 16–17 with capacity may consent under Family Law Reform
  Act 1969 s.8 —
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1969/46/section/8>
- Children under 16 may consent if they meet the Gillick competence test
  (*Gillick v West Norfolk and Wisbech AHA* [1985] UKHL 7) —
  see GMC *Protecting children and young people* —
  <https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/professional-standards-for-doctors/protecting-children-and-young-people>

## Surgical safety

Where the consented treatment is a surgical procedure, the form is read
alongside the WHO Surgical Safety Checklist (see
`forms/who-surgical-safety-checklist/`):
<https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/patient-safety/research/safe-surgery>
