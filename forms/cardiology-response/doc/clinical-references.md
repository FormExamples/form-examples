# Cardiology Response — clinical references

Reference notes grounding the four-axis interpretation engine. These inform the
response classification, the severity / structured-finding score, and the
follow-up urgency rules; they are not patient-facing.

## Response classification (Axis A)

- A response is classified **no-abnormality** when no significant cardiac
  abnormality is found, **cardiac-condition** when a cardiac diagnosis is made,
  **critical** when a critical or unexpected significant result is present, and
  **inconclusive** when the assessment cannot yet answer the clinical question.

## Severity & structured findings (Axis B)

- **2021 ESC/EACTS valvular heart disease guidelines** — moderate–severe valve
  disease drives severity and may require specialist management or intervention.
  <https://academic.oup.com/eurheartj/article/43/7/561/6358470>
- **NICE NG106 — heart failure** — reduced left-ventricular ejection fraction
  (HFrEF, LVEF ≤ 40 %) is a major-severity structured finding requiring
  disease-modifying therapy.
  <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng106>
- Significant arrhythmia and structural abnormality (e.g. cardiomyopathy) raise
  severity and the structured-finding category label.

## Completeness (Axis C)

- Mandatory sections: clinical summary, examination findings, diagnosis
  category, management plan, and recommended follow-up. The diagnosis and
  management plan are weighted highest.

## Follow-up urgency (Axis D)

- **Critical result** — a critical arrhythmia, severe symptomatic aortic
  stenosis, or acute coronary syndrome auto-escalates follow-up urgency to
  critical-alert and must be communicated directly to the referrer / responsible
  team.
- Otherwise urgency follows the dominant finding severity and the diagnosis,
  mapping to routine / recommended / urgent with an associated target timeframe.
