# Regulatory Framework

The WHO Surgical Safety Checklist is an evidence-based three-phase safety
intervention published by the World Health Organization in 2008 as part of
the *Safe Surgery Saves Lives* programme. This document describes the
international, national, and professional frameworks within which the
checklist operates.

## Origin — WHO *Safe Surgery Saves Lives*

The checklist was developed and field-tested under the WHO Patient Safety
Programme's *Safe Surgery Saves Lives* second Global Patient Safety
Challenge (2007–2008). The First Edition checklist and implementation
manual were published in 2008–2009.

- WHO — Safe Surgery Saves Lives programme:
  <https://www.who.int/teams/integrated-health-services/patient-safety/research/safe-surgery>
- WHO. *Implementation Manual: WHO Surgical Safety Checklist, First
  Edition* (2008) — archived through the WHO publications site at the
  Safe Surgery programme page above.
- WHO. *Safe Surgery Saves Lives — Starter Kit for Surgical Checklist
  Implementation*, Version 1.0 —
  <https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/patient-safety/safe-surgery/starter_kit-sssl.pdf>

## Evidentiary base

The pivotal multinational evaluation of the checklist appeared in the
*New England Journal of Medicine*:

- Haynes A.B., Weiser T.G., Berry W.R., Lipsitz S.R., Breizat A.-H.S.,
  Dellinger E.P., et al. *A surgical safety checklist to reduce morbidity
  and mortality in a global population.* New England Journal of Medicine.
  2009 Jan 29;360(5):491–9.
  <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa0810119>

Implementation in the NHS England system is supported by NHS Improvement
and NHS England patient-safety guidance:

- NHS England — Patient Safety:
  <https://www.england.nhs.uk/patient-safety/>
- NHS National Reporting and Learning System (NRLS) — Never Events policy
  framework (includes "wrong site surgery"):
  <https://www.england.nhs.uk/patient-safety/never-events/>

## Three phases (operative framework)

The checklist comprises three sequential phases performed by the operating
team:

| Phase | Trigger | Required participants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sign In | Before induction of anaesthesia | At least the nurse and the anaesthesia professional |
| Time Out | Before skin incision | Nurse, anaesthesia professional, and surgeon |
| Sign Out | Before the patient leaves the operating room | Nurse, anaesthesia professional, and surgeon |

The phases are described in the WHO *Implementation Manual* (2008). Each
phase has a coordinator, normally the circulating nurse, who reads the
items aloud and confirms each one verbally with the team.

## NatSSIPs and LocSSIPs

NHS England adopts the WHO checklist within the *National Safety
Standards for Invasive Procedures* (NatSSIPs) and locally-adapted
LocSSIPs:

- NHS England — National Safety Standards for Invasive Procedures
  (NatSSIPs 2, 2023) —
  <https://www.cas.mhra.gov.uk/ViewandAcknowledgment/ViewAlert.aspx?AlertID=103218>

NatSSIPs 2 explicitly retains the three-phase structure with additional
standardised "stop" moments and team briefings.

## Professional and consent grounding

Each phase intersects with consent and confidentiality standards:

- Sign In item 1 confirms identity, site, procedure, and consent — links
  to GMC *Decision Making and Consent* (2020) and *Montgomery v
  Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11:
  - GMC *Decision Making and Consent*:
    <https://www.gmc-uk.org/professional-standards/professional-standards-for-doctors/decision-making-and-consent>
  - *Montgomery v Lanarkshire Health Board* [2015] UKSC 11:
    <https://www.supremecourt.uk/cases/uksc-2013-0136.html>
- Sign In item 2 confirms site marking — links to NHS England Never
  Events policy.

## Information governance

The checklist record is part of the patient's operative record and is
governed by:

- UK GDPR Article 6(1)(e) public task; Article 9(2)(h) provision of
  health care.
- NHS England Records Management Code of Practice 2021 (operative records
  retention).

## Device classification

- MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 — Class I where the checklist functions as a
  process-adherence record (no clinical decision support output). See
  also `safety-case-notes.md`.
