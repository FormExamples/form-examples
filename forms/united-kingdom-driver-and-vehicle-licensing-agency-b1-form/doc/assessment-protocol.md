# Assessment protocol — DVLA B1 neurological screening

The B1 questionnaire screens for neurological conditions that may impair the
ability to drive safely. This document maps each B1 section to the
corresponding chapter of the DVLA "Assessing fitness to drive" guide and
records the standard duration of disqualification ranges.

Authoritative source: **"Assessing fitness to drive: a guide for medical
professionals"** — DVLA Drivers Medical Group. Current edition on gov.uk:
<https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/assessing-fitness-to-drive-a-guide-for-medical-professionals>.

## Sections and corresponding guidance chapters

### 1. Epilepsy and seizures

Guidance: Chapter "Neurological disorders — Epilepsy and seizures".

Key Group 1 thresholds (subject to current edition):

- First unprovoked epileptic seizure with no abnormal investigations: must not
  drive for 6 months (Group 1).
- Established epilepsy: must be seizure-free for at least 12 months.
- Group 2: more stringent (typically 10-year seizure-free and off
  anti-epileptic medication, plus low recurrence risk).

### 2. Syncope and loss of consciousness

Guidance: Chapter "Neurological disorders — Syncope".

The DVLA classifies syncope events as simple faint, unexplained syncope with
low/high risk factors, or cough syncope. Disqualification ranges from no bar
(simple faint with full recovery) to 6 months or more (unexplained syncope).

### 3. Stroke and transient ischaemic attack (TIA)

Guidance: Chapter "Neurological disorders — Stroke and TIA".

- Single TIA / stroke without significant residual disability: Group 1 must
  not drive for 1 month; no need to notify DVLA unless residual deficit.
- Group 2: 1-year off driving, full clinical recovery required.

### 4. Chronic neurological disorders

Guidance: Chapter "Neurological disorders — Chronic neurological disorders".

Includes Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, motor neurone disease,
muscular dystrophies. Licensing depends on motor/cognitive impact, fatigue,
and progression. Adapted vehicle controls may be required.

### 5. Head injury / intracranial surgery

Guidance: Chapter "Neurological disorders — Head injury and intracranial
surgery".

Post-traumatic seizures, post-craniotomy seizures, and cognitive deficits all
trigger time-limited bars.

### 6. Cognitive impairment / dementia

Guidance: Chapter "Neurological disorders — Dementia and mild cognitive
impairment".

Driving is permitted if early dementia with no significant impairment of
attention, judgement, or visuospatial perception; licence is typically issued
for short renewal periods (1–3 years) subject to review.

### 7. Sleep disorders

Guidance: Chapter "Neurological disorders — Sleep disorders".

Confirmed moderate or severe obstructive sleep apnoea syndrome (OSAS) with
excessive sleepiness: must not drive until effective control demonstrated.
Group 2 standards are stricter.

## Reporting outcome

The DVLA Medical Adviser issues one of:

- Licence retained / re-issued (with or without time limit).
- Short-period licence (1, 2, 3, or 5 years).
- Adapted-vehicle licence (codes 78, 96, etc. — see Annex I of Directive
  2006/126/EC).
- Refusal / revocation under Road Traffic Act 1988 s.93.

## Appeal

A driver whose licence is refused or revoked may appeal to the magistrates'
court (Road Traffic Act 1988 s.100). Time limit: 6 months from notification.
