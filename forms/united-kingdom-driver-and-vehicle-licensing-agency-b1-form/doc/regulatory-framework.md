# Regulatory framework — DVLA B1 (neurological)

The B1 questionnaire collects neurological medical information used by the
Driver & Vehicle Licensing Agency (DVLA, Swansea) to assess fitness to drive
Group 1 (cars and motorcycles) and Group 2 (large goods vehicles and passenger
carrying vehicles) licences.

## Primary statute

- **Road Traffic Act 1988**, sections 92–94 (notification of relevant or
  prospective disability to the Secretary of State; revocation/refusal of
  licence on medical grounds). Consolidated text at
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/52/contents>.
- **Motor Vehicles (Driving Licences) Regulations 1999** (SI 1999/2864),
  Schedule 8 (relevant and prospective disabilities).
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1999/2864/contents>
- EU Directive **2006/126/EC** on driving licences, Annex III (minimum
  standards of physical and mental fitness), retained in UK law post-Brexit.

## DVLA medical standards

The clinical thresholds for issuing, refusing, or revoking a licence on
neurological grounds are published in:

- **"Assessing fitness to drive: a guide for medical professionals"** —
  current edition issued by the DVLA Drivers Medical Group. The guide is
  updated periodically; readers should always consult the version dated
  on or after the assessment.
  <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/assessing-fitness-to-drive-a-guide-for-medical-professionals>

Relevant chapters for B1 include:

1. Neurological disorders — epilepsy and seizures, syncope, stroke and TIA,
   chronic neurological disorders (Parkinson's, multiple sclerosis, motor
   neurone disease), head injury, intracranial tumours, dementia, sleep
   disorders.
2. Disorders likely to cause sudden incapacity at the wheel.

## Driver duty to notify

Drivers must notify the DVLA of any "relevant or prospective disability"
(Road Traffic Act 1988 s.94). Failure to do so is a criminal offence
punishable by a fine of up to £1,000 and may invalidate motor insurance.

- Notification overview: <https://www.gov.uk/driving-medical-conditions>
- Online notification service: <https://www.gov.uk/health-conditions-and-driving>

## Clinician role

The B1 form is completed by the patient (or with assistance) and returned to
the DVLA. A medical questionnaire (e.g. PK1 / FEP1 / EPIL1) or an examination
by an independent doctor may be requested by the DVLA Medical Adviser before a
licensing decision is made under regulation 71 of SI 1999/2864.

## Data protection

Information disclosed on a B1 is processed under:

- UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) Article 9(2)(g) —
  substantial public interest (driver licensing safety).
- Data Protection Act 2018, Schedule 1, Part 2, paragraph 6 (statutory etc.
  purposes).

DVLA's processing notice: <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dvla-privacy-policy>
