# Clinical references

Authoritative guidance underpinning the four-axis grading engine.

## Appropriateness (ACR Appropriateness Criteria / RCR iRefer)

Ordinal 1–9 scale in three bands: usually-appropriate (7–9),
may-be-appropriate (4–6), usually-not-appropriate (1–3). The ACR Appropriateness
Criteria rank imaging alternatives from 9 (most appropriate) to 1 (least
appropriate) per clinical scenario; the Royal College of Radiologists *iRefer*
guidelines provide the UK equivalent. Mapped per (indication × body region).

## Radiation safety (IR(ME)R 2017)

Justification is the intellectual process of weighing the expected benefit of an
exposure against the detriment of the radiation dose. Under IR(ME)R 2017 every
medical exposure must be justified before it is carried out; the referrer
supplies sufficient clinical information for the practitioner to justify it.

| Safety band | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| safe | Justified, no pregnancy or repeat-exposure concern |
| caution | Pregnancy possible/unknown, repeat recent image, or weak justification |
| contraindicated | Pregnant patient without overriding justification, or no justification supplied |

Relative effective-dose banding (low / moderate / high) is approximated per body
region: extremities, chest, skull and dental are low; abdomen, pelvis and hip
are moderate; spine series are moderate–high.

## Triage tiers

| Tier | Target | Triggers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency | same hour | suspected pneumothorax, acute trauma-fracture, line-position-check on unstable patient |
| Urgent | same day / 24–72 h | acute infection, foreign body, abdominal obstruction |
| Routine | standard booking | joint pain, arthritis, pre-operative, follow-up |
