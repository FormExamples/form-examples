Glasgow-Blatchford Bleeding Score (GBS)

Pre-endoscopy risk-stratification score for suspected acute upper GI bleeding: eight weighted parameters summed to a total of 0-23 and a risk band.

Complete the eight Glasgow-Blatchford parameters below for an adult (16 years or over) presenting with suspected acute upper gastrointestinal bleeding. Each parameter contributes 0 points at its normal band and an increasing number of points as the value becomes more abnormal, for a total of 0 to 23. A score of 0 identifies a very-low-risk patient who may be considered for outpatient management; higher scores prompt admission and endoscopy. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

The GBS is calculated from data available at first assessment, before any endoscopy. It supports the decision to admit or discharge and the timing of endoscopy; it does not replace resuscitation of the shocked patient, and a low score does not override clinical concern. Post-endoscopy risk is assessed with the full Rockall score.

The Glasgow-Blatchford Bleeding Score was derived by Blatchford, Murray and Blatchford (Lancet 2000) and is recommended by NICE CG141 at first assessment of acute upper GI bleeding. This tool is for information only, is not a diagnosis, and does not replace clinical judgement — use it alongside local pathways, resuscitation of the unstable patient, and the post-endoscopy Rockall score.
