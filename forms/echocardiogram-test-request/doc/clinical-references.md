# Clinical references

Authoritative guidance underpinning the four-axis grading engine.

## Appropriateness (ACC/AHA/ASE & BSE Appropriate Use Criteria)

Ordinal 1–9 scale in three bands: appropriate (7–9), may-be-appropriate (4–6),
rarely-appropriate (1–3). Mapped per (indication × echo type). Sources: the 2019
ACC/AHA/ASE multisociety AUC for nonvalvular heart disease and the 2017 AUC for
valvular heart disease, with BSE referral guidance for UK practice. Examples of
appropriate indications: new/changed murmur, suspected valve disease, heart
failure with raised natriuretic peptide, cardiomyopathy, suspected endocarditis,
baseline/serial assessment before and during cardiotoxic chemotherapy.

## Clinical priority — natriuretic peptide (NICE NG106)

| NT-proBNP | NICE action | Priority contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| > 2000 ng/L | Specialist assessment + echo within **2 weeks** | high |
| 400–2000 ng/L | Specialist assessment + echo within **6 weeks** | moderate |
| < 400 ng/L (untreated) | Heart failure less likely | low |

Priority is also raised by NYHA class III–IV and by suspected severe structural
pathology (e.g. severe symptomatic valve disease).

## Urgency triage tiers

| Tier | Target | Triggers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emergency | same day | suspected endocarditis with instability, acute heart failure, tamponade |
| Urgent | within 2 weeks | suspected endocarditis (stable), severe symptomatic valve disease, NT-proBNP > 2000 ng/L, NYHA III–IV |
| Routine | standard booking | surveillance, stable murmur, hypertension, pre-chemotherapy baseline |
