Complete the five Child-Pugh parameters below for an adult (16 years or over) with established chronic liver disease. Each parameter scores 1, 2, or 3 points against fixed thresholds, for a total of 5 to 15. The total maps to a class: A (5-6, well-compensated), B (7-9, significant compromise), or C (10-15, decompensated), each with an estimated survival and peri-operative-risk band. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

The Child-Pugh score supports prognostic discussion, transplant assessment, and surgical-risk stratification. It is not an organ-allocation priority score (MELD / UKELD serve that role) and does not replace clinical judgement.

The Child-Pugh (Child-Turcotte-Pugh) score was described by Child and Turcotte (1964) and refined by Pugh et al. (1973). Survival and surgical-risk figures are widely cited approximations from the original and follow-up cohorts and support prognostic discussion; they are not guarantees for an individual patient. This tool is for information only, is not a diagnosis, and does not determine transplant priority — use it alongside MELD / UKELD, local policy, and clinical judgement.
