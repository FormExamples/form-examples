Complete the five sections below to prepare a birth registration for England and Wales. The engine does not register the birth and is not legal advice. It checks that the application is complete, that the informant is a qualified informant, that a second parent (such as an unmarried father) has a valid basis and the signed form or court document that goes with it, that your identity evidence is sufficient, and that the birth is registered within the 42-day statutory period. It classifies the application as ready to submit, incomplete, needs additional evidence, or out of scope (a stillbirth, or a birth in Scotland, Northern Ireland or abroad), and raises flags. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

A class of ready to submit does not guarantee registration: the registrar checks your evidence and makes the entry in the register, which is the legal record.

Birth registration in England and Wales is governed by the Births and Deaths Registration Act 1953 and the Registration of Births and Deaths Regulations 1987, and administered by the General Register Office and local register offices. The guidance used here is GOV.UK "Register a birth" and "Order a copy of a birth, death, marriage or civil partnership certificate". This tool checks completeness only and makes no statutory decision; the registrar's entry in the register remains the legal record.
