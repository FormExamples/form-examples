# Clinical guideline alignment — cognitive assessment

## NICE NG97 — Dementia: assessment, management and support for people living with dementia and their carers

Source: National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE).
Guideline ID: **NG97** (published June 2018; last updated June 2018).
URL: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng97

NG97 §1.2 — *Diagnosis* — sets out the recommended approach to cognitive
testing. This form aligns with:

- **§1.2.2**: At initial assessment in a non-specialist setting, take a
  history (including cognitive, behavioural and psychological symptoms)
  from the person and, if possible, someone who knows them well.
  Captured in the Referral Information and Functional History steps.
- **§1.2.3**: At initial assessment use a validated brief structured
  cognitive instrument such as the **10-point cognitive screener
  (10-CS)**, the **6-item cognitive impairment test (6CIT)**, the
  **6-item screener**, the **Memory Impairment Screen (MIS)**, the
  **Mini-Cog**, or the **Test Your Memory (TYM)** tool. NG97 explicitly
  notes that the **MMSE** is not recommended for initial primary-care use
  because of its copyright restrictions, but it remains widely used in
  specialist settings and is the instrument implemented here.
- **§1.2.10**: Do not rule out dementia solely because a person has a
  normal score on a cognitive instrument. The form's `severityBand`
  output is advisory only and must be combined with clinical judgement
  and collateral history.

## DSM-5-TR

American Psychiatric Association (2022) *Diagnostic and Statistical Manual
of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision (DSM-5-TR)*.

DSM-5-TR uses two main neurocognitive categories that this form supports:

- **Mild neurocognitive disorder** (code **331.83 / G31.84**) — modest
  cognitive decline from previous performance, not interfering with
  independence in everyday activities.
- **Major neurocognitive disorder** (codes vary by aetiology, e.g.
  Alzheimer disease F02.8x) — significant cognitive decline that
  interferes with independence in everyday activities.

The MMSE total contributes to the cognitive-decline domain but does not
itself diagnose either category; the clinician must also evidence
functional impact (captured in Functional History).

## ICD-11

World Health Organization. ICD-11 MMS.
URL: https://icd.who.int/browse11/l-m/en

Relevant codes:

- **6D70** — Mild neurocognitive disorder.
- **6D71** — Amnestic disorder.
- **6D80–6D8Z** — Dementia (with sub-codes for Alzheimer disease,
  vascular dementia, Lewy body dementia, frontotemporal dementia, etc.).

## Comparison instruments referenced for triangulation

- **Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA)** — Nasreddine et al. (2005);
  more sensitive than MMSE for mild cognitive impairment.
  https://www.mocatest.org
- **Abbreviated Mental Test 4 (AMT-4)** — Swain & Nightingale (1997);
  4-item bedside screen used in acute hospital settings.
- **6-CIT** — Brooke & Bullock (1999).
