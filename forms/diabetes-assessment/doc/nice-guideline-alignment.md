# NICE guideline alignment

The diabetes assessment form encodes the NICE structured diabetes review
for type 2 (NG28) and type 1 (NG17) diabetes, supplemented by the Diabetes
UK *15 Healthcare Essentials*.

## Primary guidance

- NICE NG28 *Type 2 diabetes in adults: management.* National Institute
  for Health and Care Excellence; first published 2015, last updated 2022.
  <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng28>
- NICE NG17 *Type 1 diabetes in adults: diagnosis and management.* NICE;
  first published 2015, last updated 2022.
  <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng17>
- NICE NG18 *Diabetes (type 1 and type 2) in children and young people:
  diagnosis and management.* NICE; first published 2015, last updated 2023.
  <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18>
- NICE NG19 *Diabetic foot problems: prevention and management.* NICE;
  first published 2015, last updated 2019.
  <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng19>
- NICE NG203 *Chronic kidney disease: assessment and management.* NICE; 2021.
  <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng203>
- NICE QS6 *Diabetes in adults quality standard.*
  <https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/qs6>

## Diabetes UK 15 Healthcare Essentials

Diabetes UK summarizes the annual review as the *15 Healthcare Essentials*:

<https://www.diabetes.org.uk/about-us/our-policies/healthcare-services/15-healthcare-essentials>

1. Annual HbA1c.
2. Blood pressure measurement.
3. Cholesterol level.
4. Eye screening.
5. Foot and leg check.
6. Kidney function (eGFR + ACR).
7. Individual care plan.
8. Free education course.
9. See a diabetes specialist when needed.
10. Free flu jab.
11. Good care in hospital.
12. Sexual health support.
13. Emotional and psychological support.
14. Pregnancy support (where applicable).
15. Smoking-cessation support.

## HbA1c targets (NICE)

- Type 2 diabetes, diet and metformin (no hypoglycaemia risk): 48 mmol/mol
  (6.5 %).
- Type 2 diabetes, on a drug with hypoglycaemia risk (sulphonylurea or
  insulin): 53 mmol/mol (7.0 %).
- Individualized: relax target in frail or elderly patients or where
  intensification harms outweigh benefits.
- Type 1 diabetes (adults): 48 mmol/mol or lower if achievable without
  problematic hypoglycaemia (NG17 §1.6.6).

## Treatment escalation (NG28 §1.6, updated 2022)

- First line: metformin standard or modified release.
- After metformin, if QRISK ≥ 10 % or established CVD or CKD or heart
  failure: add SGLT2 inhibitor with proven cardiovascular benefit.
- If further intensification needed: GLP-1 receptor agonist, DPP-4
  inhibitor, sulphonylurea, pioglitazone, basal insulin.

## Foot risk stratification (NG19)

- Low risk: no risk factors.
- Moderate risk: deformity, neuropathy, non-critical limb ischaemia.
- High risk: previous ulcer/amputation, RRT, neuropathy + non-critical limb
  ischaemia, neuropathy + callus/deformity, non-critical limb ischaemia +
  callus/deformity.
- Active diabetic foot problem: ulceration, spreading infection,
  critical limb ischaemia, gangrene, suspicion of Charcot arthropathy or
  unexplained hot, red, swollen foot.
