# Audiology grading rules

The form classifies hearing loss based on the pure-tone average (PTA) at
0.5, 1, 2, and 4 kHz in the better ear, using the **ASHA classification**
(American Speech-Language-Hearing Association) for clinical compatibility
with most US/UK audiology practice. A WHO crosswalk is also produced.

## ASHA hearing loss grades

| Grade | PTA dB HL (better ear) |
| --- | --- |
| Normal | ≤ 25 |
| Mild | 26-40 |
| Moderate | 41-55 |
| Moderately severe | 56-70 |
| Severe | 71-90 |
| Profound | > 90 |

- American Speech-Language-Hearing Association. *Type, Degree, and
  Configuration of Hearing Loss*. ASHA Practice Portal.
  <https://www.asha.org/public/hearing/Degree-of-Hearing-Loss/>
- Clark JG. *Uses and abuses of hearing loss classification*. ASHA
  1981;23:493-500. PMID: 7052898

## WHO crosswalk (2021)

| WHO grade (2021) | PTA dB HL (better ear) |
| --- | --- |
| No hearing loss | < 20 |
| Mild | 20-34 |
| Moderate | 35-49 |
| Moderately severe | 50-64 |
| Severe | 65-79 |
| Profound | 80-94 |
| Complete | ≥ 95 |

- World Health Organization. *World Report on Hearing*. Geneva: WHO, 2021.
  <https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240020481>

## Configuration of hearing loss

In addition to severity, the form classifies configuration:

- **Conductive** — air-bone gap ≥10 dB at two or more contiguous
  frequencies (250, 500, 1000, 2000 Hz).
- **Sensorineural** — air-bone gap <10 dB, normal tympanogram, reduced
  thresholds.
- **Mixed** — conductive + sensorineural elements.
- **High-frequency sloping** — common in presbycusis and noise-induced
  hearing loss.
- **Low-frequency rising** — atypical; consider Ménière's disease or
  superior canal dehiscence.
- **Cookie-bite** — mid-frequency loss, often genetic.

## Speech audiometry

- **Speech reception threshold (SRT)** — within 6 dB of PTA in cooperative
  patients.
- **Word recognition score (WRS)** — % correct at 40 dB sensation level.
  Poor WRS relative to PTA suggests retrocochlear pathology.

## Tinnitus assessment

- **Tinnitus Handicap Inventory (THI)** — 25 items, 0-100.
  - 0-16: slight; 18-36: mild; 38-56: moderate; 58-76: severe; 78-100:
    catastrophic.
- Newman CW, Jacobson GP, Spitzer JB. *Development of the Tinnitus
  Handicap Inventory*. Arch Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg 1996;122:143-148.
  PMID: 8630207

## Communication impact

- **Self-Assessment of Communication (SAC)** — Schow & Nerbonne 1982,
  10 items, used by VA / RNID.
- **Speech, Spatial and Qualities of Hearing (SSQ)** — Gatehouse & Noble 2004,
  49 items, used in cochlear-implant evaluation. *Int J Audiol* 2004;43:85-99.
  PMID: 15035561

## Flagged-issue triggers

- Sudden sensorineural hearing loss (≥30 dB at 3 contiguous frequencies in
  ≤3 days) → emergency ENT referral (AAO-HNS 2019).
- Asymmetric sensorineural loss (≥15 dB difference between ears at 2 or more
  frequencies) → MRI internal auditory meatus to rule out vestibular
  schwannoma.
- Severe-to-profound bilateral loss with poor WRS → cochlear implant
  candidacy assessment per NICE TA566.
- Conductive loss in adult → ENT referral for otosclerosis / cholesteatoma
  workup.
- Tinnitus + asymmetric loss → MRI per BTA guidelines.
