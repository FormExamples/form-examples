# UK MEC Eligibility Criteria

The UK Medical Eligibility Criteria for Contraceptive Use (UK MEC) is the
domestic adaptation of the WHO Medical Eligibility Criteria, published and
maintained by the Faculty of Sexual & Reproductive Healthcare (FSRH) of the
Royal College of Obstetricians and Gynaecologists.

## Four-category framework

UK MEC classifies each combination of method and medical condition into one of
four categories:

| Category | Definition (UK MEC 2016, amended 2019) |
| -------- | -------------------------------------- |
| UKMEC 1  | A condition for which there is no restriction for the use of the method. |
| UKMEC 2  | A condition where the advantages of using the method generally outweigh the theoretical or proven risks. |
| UKMEC 3  | A condition where the theoretical or proven risks usually outweigh the advantages of using the method. The provision of the method requires expert clinical judgement and/or referral to a specialist contraceptive provider. |
| UKMEC 4  | A condition which represents an unacceptable health risk if the method is used. |

Source: FSRH Clinical Effectiveness Unit. *UK Medical Eligibility Criteria
for Contraceptive Use (UKMEC 2016) — Amended September 2019.*
<https://www.fsrh.org/standards-and-guidance/uk-medical-eligibility-criteria/>

## Methods covered

The questionnaire's risk model is aligned with the categories applied across
the methods listed in UKMEC 2016:

- Combined hormonal contraception (CHC): pill, patch, ring
- Progestogen-only pill (POP)
- Progestogen-only injectable (DMPA)
- Progestogen-only implant (IMP)
- Levonorgestrel-releasing intrauterine system (LNG-IUS)
- Copper intrauterine device (Cu-IUD)
- Barrier methods and fertility awareness methods
- Emergency contraception

## Selected UKMEC 3 / UKMEC 4 conditions screened by this form

The form's risk model focuses on the conditions where UKMEC explicitly assigns
categories of 3 or 4 for combined hormonal contraception. Categories below are
taken directly from UKMEC 2016 (amended 2019) and must not be paraphrased in
clinical output.

- Smoking ≥15 cigarettes/day and age ≥35 — UKMEC 4 for CHC
- Migraine with aura at any age — UKMEC 4 for CHC, initiation; UKMEC 3 for
  continuation
- Current or history of ischaemic heart disease — UKMEC 4 for CHC
- Stroke (history) — UKMEC 4 for CHC initiation
- BMI ≥35 kg/m² — UKMEC 3 for CHC
- Current or past VTE — UKMEC 4 for CHC
- Known thrombogenic mutations — UKMEC 4 for CHC
- Systolic ≥160 or diastolic ≥100 mmHg — UKMEC 4 for CHC
- Breast cancer (current) — UKMEC 4 for all hormonal methods
- < 6 weeks postpartum and breastfeeding — UKMEC 4 for CHC

Where the form is uncertain whether a category 3 or 4 condition applies, it
records a flagged issue and defers the recommendation to the prescribing
clinician.

## Out of scope

The form does not implement WHO MEC categories for methods not licensed in
the UK (for example, monthly combined injectable contraceptives), and does
not advise on emergency contraception dosing — see FSRH's separate
*Emergency Contraception* CEU guideline for that pathway.
