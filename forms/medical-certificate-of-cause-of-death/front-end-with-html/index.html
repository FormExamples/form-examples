Complete the seven sections below to document a Medical Certificate of Cause of Death. The engine does not compute a clinical score and does not diagnose. It checks that the certificate is internally complete and consistent — that Part I(a) is present, that the causal sequence reads downward in a plausible order, and that no unacceptable "mode of death" (such as "cardiac arrest") stands as the only cause — and it classifies the certificate as valid, incomplete, or refer to coroner, derives the underlying cause, and raises statutory, safety, and governance flags. Answers are saved automatically in your browser as you go.

A classification of valid does not discharge the certifying doctor's legal duty to consider referral to the coroner, and every death not investigated by a coroner must be scrutinised by an NHS medical examiner before registration. This tool documents and validates the certificate; the prescribed statutory certificate remains the definitive legal record.

This certificate is governed by the UK death-certification framework: the Births and Deaths Registration Act 1953, the Coroners and Justice Act 2009 and the Coroners (Investigations) Regulations 2013, the statutory NHS medical-examiner system, and the Office for National Statistics guidance on completing an MCCD. This tool documents and validates the certificate and makes no diagnostic judgement; the prescribed statutory certificate remains the definitive legal record, and referral and registration decisions rest with the certifying doctor, coroner, and medical examiner.
