# Methodology reference — Objectives and Key Results (OKR)

Objectives and Key Results (OKR) is a goal-setting framework that pairs
qualitative *objectives* (what we want to achieve) with quantitative
*key results* (how we know we have achieved them).

## Origin

OKRs were developed at Intel by **Andy Grove** in the late 1970s and
formalized in his 1983 book *High Output Management*. Grove described
the predecessor "iMBO" (Intel Management by Objectives) system that
became the modern OKR.

- Grove, A. S. (1983). *High Output Management*. Random House. (Re-
  issued 2015 by Vintage Books, ISBN 978-0679762881.)

**John Doerr** carried the practice from Intel to Kleiner Perkins
portfolio companies, most famously to Google in 1999, and described
the framework in his 2018 book *Measure What Matters*.

- Doerr, J. (2018). *Measure What Matters*. Portfolio / Penguin. ISBN
  978-0525536222.
- Companion site: <https://www.whatmatters.com/>

## Definitions (per Doerr 2018 and whatmatters.com)

### Objective

A qualitative, ambitious, action-oriented statement of intent. It
should be:

- significant;
- concrete and clearly defined;
- action-oriented;
- inspirational.

### Key result

A measurable milestone that, if achieved, advances the objective. Each
KR should be:

- specific and time-bound;
- aggressive yet realistic;
- measurable and verifiable.

Doerr's guideline: 3-5 KRs per objective.

### Scoring

KRs are typically scored on a 0.0–1.0 (or 0–100 %) scale at the end of
the cycle. Doerr's heuristic for stretch ("moonshot") OKRs:

- 0.7–1.0: green — achievement;
- 0.4–0.6: yellow — progress;
- 0.0–0.3: red — fail.

A perfect 1.0 across all KRs suggests the OKRs were not ambitious
enough. This nuance is sometimes called "committed vs aspirational"
OKRs (Google internal practice as described by Rick Klau).

- Klau, R. (2013). How Google sets goals: OKRs.
  <https://library.gv.com/how-google-sets-goals-okrs-a1f69b0b72c7>

## Cadence

OKRs are typically run on:

- **Annual** objectives at the company level.
- **Quarterly** objectives at team level (cascading from annual).
- **Weekly** check-ins on KR progress.
- **End-of-cycle** scoring and retrospective.

## Cascading vs aligned

Two patterns:

- **Cascading**: each team's OKRs derive from a parent OKR. Risk of
  rigidity.
- **Aligned**: teams set their own OKRs in dialogue with neighbours,
  with explicit links to the parent objectives they support. Preferred
  by Doerr and Google.

This implementation models **alignment** as a many-to-one link from
child OKR to parent OKR, allowing flexible alignment graphs rather than
strict trees.

## Related frameworks (for comparison)

- **MBO** (Drucker, 1954) — predecessor to OKR; annual cadence.
- **SMART goals** (Doran 1981) — Specific, Measurable, Achievable,
  Relevant, Time-bound; commonly used at individual level.
- **Balanced Scorecard** (Kaplan & Norton, 1992) — multi-perspective
  enterprise performance system.
