# Regulatory Framework

An **Advance Statement about Care** ("AS") is a non-binding written
expression of a person's wishes, beliefs, values, and preferences about
their future care. Unlike an Advance Decision to Refuse Treatment (which is
binding under s.26 Mental Capacity Act 2005), an advance statement carries
**evidential** rather than legal weight: it informs the s.4 MCA 2005
"best interests" decision that must be made on behalf of an adult who has
lost capacity.

## Primary statute — Mental Capacity Act 2005

Although the MCA 2005 does not name the document "advance statement", the
following sections supply its operative legal effect.

- Mental Capacity Act 2005 (c. 9) —
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/contents>
- Section 4 — best-interests checklist —
  <https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2005/9/section/4>

Key provisions:

| Section | Relevance |
| --- | --- |
| s.1(5) | Acts done for a person without capacity must be in their best interests. |
| s.4(6)(a) | The decision-maker must consider the person's "past and present wishes and feelings (and, in particular, any relevant written statement made by him when he had capacity)". |
| s.4(6)(b)–(c) | The decision-maker must consider the person's beliefs and values and other factors they would be likely to consider. |
| s.4(7) | The decision-maker must take into account the views of carers, family, and anyone named by the person as someone to be consulted. |

The Code of Practice (DCA, 2007), Chapter 5, gives the operational guidance
that healthcare professionals must "have regard to" when applying s.4 to a
patient with an advance statement on file.

## Distinction from binding instruments

The advance statement deliberately captures matters that fall **outside** the
binding scope of an ADRT (refusal of specific treatments under MCA 2005
ss.24–26) or a Lasting Power of Attorney (decision-making authority granted
to an attorney under MCA 2005 s.9). A patient may have all three documents
simultaneously, and the form's audit trail should record which exists so
clinicians can apply them in the correct order:

1. Apply any Lasting Power of Attorney for Health and Welfare (LPA HW)
   under MCA 2005 s.9.
2. Honour any valid and applicable Advance Decision to Refuse Treatment
   under MCA 2005 s.26.
3. Make a best-interests decision under MCA 2005 s.4 *informed by* the
   advance statement.

## Information governance

The advance statement records UK GDPR Article 9 special-category data
(health, religious or philosophical beliefs). The lawful basis for storage
within an NHS organisation is:

- UK GDPR Article 6(1)(e) — task carried out in the public interest.
- UK GDPR Article 9(2)(h) — preventive medicine, provision of health or
  social care.

Information Commissioner's Office guidance:
<https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/a-guide-to-lawful-basis/special-category-data/>
