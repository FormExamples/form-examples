# Scoring Rules — Anesthesiology Assessment

This document records the four primary instruments combined into the composite
perioperative risk grade, with citation to the canonical source for each.

## ASA Physical Status Classification

The American Society of Anesthesiologists Physical Status Classification System
defines six grades (I, II, III, IV, V, VI) plus an "E" emergency modifier.

- Canonical reference: American Society of Anesthesiologists, *ASA Physical
  Status Classification System* (most recent approval October 15, 2014; last
  amended December 13, 2020).
- Source URL: https://www.asahq.org/standards-and-practice-parameters/statement-on-asa-physical-status-classification-system

Summary of categories (verbatim from the ASA statement):

| Grade | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| I | A normal healthy patient |
| II | A patient with mild systemic disease |
| III | A patient with severe systemic disease |
| IV | A patient with severe systemic disease that is a constant threat to life |
| V | A moribund patient who is not expected to survive without the operation |
| VI | A declared brain-dead patient whose organs are being removed for donor purposes |
| E suffix | Emergency operation |

The ASA classification is a clinician judgment; the form records the chosen
grade plus structured drivers that justify it.

## Mallampati Classification

Used to predict difficult intubation by visualising oropharyngeal structures
with the patient sitting upright, mouth open, tongue protruded.

- Canonical reference: Mallampati SR, Gatt SP, Gugino LD, et al. *A clinical
  sign to predict difficult tracheal intubation: a prospective study.*
  Canadian Anaesthetists' Society Journal. 1985;32(4):429-434.
  DOI: 10.1007/BF03011357.

Classes:

| Class | Visible structures |
| --- | --- |
| I | Soft palate, uvula, fauces, pillars |
| II | Soft palate, uvula, fauces |
| III | Soft palate, base of uvula |
| IV | Hard palate only |

Class III or IV alone is a recognised flag for anticipated difficult airway,
and combined with other markers (limited mouth opening, short thyromental
distance, BMI ≥ 35, neck circumference > 43 cm) raises the composite risk.

## Revised Cardiac Risk Index (Lee)

A six-item index for predicting major cardiac complications after non-cardiac
surgery.

- Canonical reference: Lee TH, Marcantonio ER, Mangione CM, et al.
  *Derivation and prospective validation of a simple index for prediction of
  cardiac risk of major noncardiac surgery.* Circulation. 1999;100(10):1043-1049.
  DOI: 10.1161/01.CIR.100.10.1043. PMID: 10477528.

One point each is scored for:

1. High-risk type of surgery (intraperitoneal, intrathoracic, suprainguinal vascular)
2. History of ischaemic heart disease
3. History of congestive heart failure
4. History of cerebrovascular disease
5. Preoperative insulin treatment for diabetes mellitus
6. Preoperative serum creatinine > 2.0 mg/dL (177 µmol/L)

Risk estimates (Lee 1999): 0 → 0.4 %, 1 → 0.9 %, 2 → 6.6 %, ≥ 3 → 11 %.

## STOP-BANG

Screening tool for obstructive sleep apnoea, particularly relevant to
peri-operative airway and respiratory planning.

- Canonical reference: Chung F, Yegneswaran B, Liao P, et al. *STOP
  questionnaire: a tool to screen patients for obstructive sleep apnea.*
  Anesthesiology. 2008;108(5):812-821. DOI: 10.1097/ALN.0b013e31816d83e4.
- Toolkit and licensing: http://www.stopbang.ca/

Eight items, one point each: Snoring, Tiredness, Observed apnoea, high blood
Pressure, BMI > 35, Age > 50, Neck circumference > 40 cm, male Gender. Scores
≥ 5 indicate high probability of moderate-to-severe OSA.

## Composite risk algorithm

The composite grade is the **maximum** of the per-instrument risk bands. Any
single critical driver (ASA IV/V, Mallampati IV with comorbidity, RCRI ≥ 3,
STOP-BANG ≥ 7 with hypercapnia, or a hard safety flag) escalates the
composite to High or Critical regardless of the other instruments.
